Solve the Following Differential Equation

Direct solution only.

$$3e^x\tan y\,dx + \left(1-e^x\right)\sec^2 y\,dy = 0$$
Rooms

Room 1: Start

  • Write the equation as given:
  • $$3e^x\tan y\,dx + \left(1-e^x\right)\sec^2 y\,dy = 0.$$

Room 2: Substitute

  • Set $$u=1-e^x.$$
  • Then $$du=-e^x\,dx.$$

Room 3: Rewrite

  • $$3e^x\tan y\,dx=-3\tan y\,du.$$
  • $$-3\tan y\,du+u\sec^2 y\,dy=0.$$

Room 4: Divide

  • Divide by $$u\tan y:$$
  • $$-3\frac{du}{u}+\frac{\sec^2 y}{\tan y}\,dy=0.$$

Room 5: Integrate

  • Use $$\int \frac{du}{u}=\ln|u|$$ and $$\int \frac{\sec^2 y}{\tan y}\,dy=\ln|\tan y|.$$
  • $$-3\ln|u|+\ln|\tan y|=C.$$

Room 6: Simplify

  • $$\ln\left|\frac{\tan y}{u^3}\right|=C \Rightarrow \frac{\tan y}{u^3}=C.$$

Room 7: Back-Substitute

  • Put back $$u=1-e^x:$$
  • $$\boxed{\left(1-e^x\right)^3=C\tan y}$$
Memory Clues

Clue 1

Use substitution when a bracket and its derivative both appear.

Clue 2

For $u=1-e^x$, keep the minus sign.

Clue 3

$\sec^2 y/\tan y$ integrates to $\ln|\tan y|$.

Clue 4

The coefficient $3$ becomes a cube after simplifying the logs.
