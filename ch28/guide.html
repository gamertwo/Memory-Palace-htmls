Guide

How To Study These Magnetic-Field Pages

Keep the emphasis narrow. For this section, memorize the force law, the magnitude law, the right-hand rule, the perpendicular property, the tesla unit, the crossed-fields cancellation rule, the Hall-effect balance rule, the circular-motion force balance, the helical-motion velocity split, and the wire-force formula. Everything else supports those anchors.

$$\vec F_B=q\vec v\times\vec B,\qquad F_B=|q|vB\sin\phi,\qquad 1\,\mathrm{T}=\frac{1\,\mathrm{N}}{1\,\mathrm{A\cdot m}}$$
Core Route

1. Define

Start with how \(\vec B\) is defined from magnetic force on a moving charge, not from a stationary test charge.

2. Measure

Lock the special perpendicular case first: when \(\vec v\perp\vec B\), the magnitude reduces to \(B=F_B/(|q|v)\).

3. Generalize

Then upgrade to the general vector form and the angle form with \(\sin\phi\).

4. Direct

Use the right-hand rule for \(\vec v\times\vec B\), then flip the answer if the charge is negative.

5. Interpret

Remember the force is perpendicular to both \(\vec v\) and \(\vec B\), so it changes direction of motion more naturally than speed.

6. Apply

Practice one moving-charge problem where you compute speed, then force, then direction.

7. Cancel

For crossed fields, remember that zero deflection means $$|q|E=|q|vB$$ so the beam speed is $$v=E/B.$$

8. Balance

For the Hall effect, remember that charge separation keeps growing until $$eE=ev_dB,$$ which leads to the Hall voltage.

9. Circle

For perpendicular motion in a uniform field, start with $$|q|vB=mv^2/r$$ and derive everything else from that one line.

10. Split

For helical motion, resolve velocity into $$v_\perp$$ and $$v_\parallel,$$ then use $$p=v_\parallel T.$$

11. Wire

For current-carrying wires, remember $$F_B=iLB\sin\phi$$ and $$\vec F_B=i\vec L\times\vec B.$$

Keep These Tidbits
Zero Force
$$F_B=0$$ when $$\phi=0^\circ,180^\circ$$ or when $$v=0.$$
Maximum Force
$$F_B$$ is largest when $$\phi=90^\circ.$$
Field-Line Rule
Outside a magnet, field lines run from north pole to south pole.
Unit
$$1\,\mathrm{T}=1\,\mathrm{N}/(\mathrm{A\cdot m})$$
