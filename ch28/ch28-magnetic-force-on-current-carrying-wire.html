28-8 Wire Force

Magnetic Force on a Current-Carrying Wire

This page turns the single-charge force law into a wire formula. Start with the magnetic force on one moving conduction electron, count how much charge passes through a wire segment in a time interval, and then package the result into the length-vector form for an entire wire segment.

$$F_B=iLB\sin\phi,\qquad \vec F_B=i\,\vec L\times\vec B,\qquad d\vec F_B=i\,d\vec l\times\vec B$$

Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Start from the Force on One Conduction Electron

  • Each moving charge in the wire feels the magnetic force $$F_B=qv_dB\sin\phi$$ in magnitude.
  • The wire as a whole feels a force because the conduction electrons collectively transmit that effect to the lattice.
  • So the wire formula is built from many identical charge-carrier pushes.

Room 2: Count the Charge Crossing a Wire Segment

  • For a wire segment of length $$L,$$ the time to cross the segment at drift speed is $$t=L/v_d.$$
  • The charge passing through in that time is $$q=it=iL/v_d. $$
  • This swaps microscopic charge counting for measurable current.

Room 3: Substitute into the Magnetic Force Formula

  • Substitute $$q=iL/v_d$$ into $$F_B=qv_dB\sin\phi.$$
  • The drift speed cancels, leaving $$F_B=iLB\sin\phi. $$
  • This is the straight-wire magnitude formula.

Room 4: Package the Result into Vector Form

  • Introduce the length vector $$\vec L$$ in the direction of the conventional current.
  • Then the full vector result is $$\vec F_B=i\vec L\times\vec B.$$
  • The right-hand rule now uses current direction rather than electron drift direction.

Room 5: Generalize to Curved Wires or Nonuniform Fields

  • Break the wire into small segments $$d\vec l.$$
  • Each small segment feels $$d\vec F_B=i\,d\vec l\times\vec B.$$
  • Integrating over the wire gives the net force for any shape or field arrangement.

Key Shortcut

Wire-force derivation is just single-charge force plus the current relation $$q=it.$$

Main Tidbit

In the vector formula, the length vector points with conventional current, not with electron drift.

Solution Flow

Single carrier first, current substitution second, wire formula third, differential form last.
