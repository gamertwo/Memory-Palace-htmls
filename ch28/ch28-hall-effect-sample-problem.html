28-5 Sample Problem

Potential Difference Setup Across a Moving Conductor

This page trims the sample problem to the working route. First get the magnetic force direction on the conduction electrons, then infer which face becomes positive or negative, and finally compute the potential difference by setting magnetic and electric forces equal at equilibrium.

$$|q|vB=|q|E,\qquad E=vB,\qquad V=Ed=vBd$$
Chapter GuideOpen in Memory Builder
What You Are Asked To Find
Given
A conducting cube moves through a magnetic field with known speed, field magnitude, and face separation $$d.$$
Find
Which face is at lower potential, and what is the potential difference between the faces.
Core Trick
Electrons move opposite the magnetic-force direction on positive test charge.
Quick Data
Magnetic Force
$$\vec F_B=q\vec v\times\vec B$$
Equilibrium
$$F_E=F_B$$ so $$E=vB$$
Voltage
$$V=Ed=vBd$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Find the Magnetic Force Direction First

  • Use the right-hand rule on $$\vec v\times\vec B$$ to find the direction for a positive charge.
  • Because the mobile carriers are electrons, their magnetic force is opposite that direction.
  • This tells you which face the electrons begin piling up on.

Room 2: Infer Which Face Becomes Negative and Which Becomes Positive

  • The face where electrons gather becomes negative.
  • The opposite face, left with electron deficit, becomes positive.
  • Therefore the positive face is at higher electric potential and the electron-rich face is at lower potential.

Room 3: Let the Charge Separation Create an Electric Field

  • As charge separation increases, an internal electric field grows between the faces.
  • This field exerts an electric force on the electrons opposite the magnetic drift effect.
  • The buildup continues until the two forces balance.

Room 4: Use Force Balance to Solve for the Electric Field

  • At equilibrium, $$|q|E=|q|vB$$ when $$\vec v\perp\vec B.$$
  • So $$E=vB.$$
  • This is the same crossed-fields balance idea from the previous section, now inside a conductor.

Room 5: Convert the Electric Field into a Potential Difference

  • Use $$V=Ed$$ across the face separation $$d.$$
  • Substituting $$E=vB$$ gives $$V=vBd.$$
  • With the sample numbers $$v=4.0\,\text{m/s},$$ $$B=0.050\,\text{T},$$ and $$d=0.030\,\text{m},$$ the result is $$V=(4.0)(0.050)(0.030)=0.0060\,\text{V}=6.0\,\text{mV}. $$

Key Shortcut

For moving-conductor Hall-style problems: direction of electron push, sign of faces, force balance, then $$V=vBd.$$

Main Tidbit

The concept page and the sample problem are really the same movie: charge separation grows until the electric field cancels the magnetic effect.

Solution Flow

Direction first, polarity second, equilibrium third, voltage last.
