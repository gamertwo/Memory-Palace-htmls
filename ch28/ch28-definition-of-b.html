28-3 Core Concept

The Definition of $\vec B$

This page keeps the section tight. First remember why the electric-field style definition fails for magnetism, then lock the special perpendicular setup, and finally upgrade that setup into the full magnetic-force law. The main memory point is simple: magnetic field is defined through the force on a moving charge.

$$B=\frac{F_B}{|q|v}\quad(\vec v\perp\vec B),\qquad \vec F_B=q\,\vec v\times\vec B,\qquad F_B=|q|vB\sin\phi$$

What You Need From This Page
Definition Idea
A magnetic field is defined by the magnetic force it exerts on a moving charged particle.
Special Case
When velocity is perpendicular to the field, $$B=F_B/(|q|v).$$
General Law
The full rule is $$\vec F_B=q\vec v\times\vec B.$$
Quick Data
Vector Form
$$\vec F_B=q\vec v\times\vec B$$
Magnitude
$$F_B=|q|vB\sin\phi$$
Angle
$$\phi$$ is the angle between $$\vec v$$ and $$\vec B$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Start from the Electric-Field Analogy

  • For electric fields, we define $$\vec E$$ by placing a test charge and using $$\vec E=\vec F_e/q.$$
  • That strategy does not transfer directly to magnetism because a stationary charge does not reveal the magnetic force law in the needed way.
  • So magnetic field must be defined from the force on a charge that is moving through the field.

Room 2: Isolate the Clean Perpendicular Setup

  • Imagine sending a charged particle through the point of interest with speed $$v$$ while changing its travel direction.
  • The magnetic force is largest when the particle moves perpendicular to the field.
  • In that special case, define the field magnitude by $$B=\dfrac{F_B}{|q|v}.$$

Room 3: Upgrade the Definition into the Vector Law

  • The direction evidence shows three things at once: the force depends on $$q,$$ depends on $$v,$$ and sits perpendicular to both $$\vec v$$ and $$\vec B.$$
  • That combination points to a cross product, so the compact vector equation is $$\vec F_B=q\vec v\times\vec B.$$
  • This is the magnetic-force law for a charged particle.

Room 4: Derive the Magnitude Formula from the Cross Product

  • For any cross product, the magnitude rule is $$|\vec a\times\vec b|=ab\sin\phi.$$
  • Applying that to $$\vec F_B=q\vec v\times\vec B$$ gives $$F_B=|q|\,|\vec v\times\vec B|.$$
  • So the general magnitude formula becomes $$F_B=|q|vB\sin\phi.$$

Room 5: Recover the Special Case from the General Formula

  • If $$\phi=90^\circ,$$ then $$\sin\phi=1,$$ so the magnitude law reduces to $$F_B=|q|vB.$$
  • Solving for the field magnitude gives $$B=\dfrac{F_B}{|q|v},$$ which is exactly the defining perpendicular setup.
  • This is the clean derivation chain to remember: perpendicular definition first, full formula second.

Key Shortcut

Lock the perpendicular case first, then let the cross product generalize it.

Main Tidbit

Magnetism reveals itself through moving charge. That is the conceptual difference that starts the whole section.

Solution Flow

Use this route: compare with electric field, isolate the perpendicular setup, upgrade to the cross-product law, then derive the magnitude rule.
