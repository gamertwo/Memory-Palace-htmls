28-7 Sample Problem

Uniform Circular Motion of a Charged Particle in a Magnetic Field

This page keeps the mass-spectrometer example stripped to the useful route. First connect the exit speed to the accelerating voltage using energy, then connect the circular path radius to the magnetic field using circular-motion formulas, and finally combine the two relations to solve for mass.

$$qV=\frac12 mv^2,\qquad r=\frac{mv}{qB},\qquad m=\frac{B^2q x^2}{8V}$$

Quick Data
Acceleration Stage
Electrical energy becomes kinetic energy
Magnetic Stage
The ion follows a semicircle in uniform $$B$$
Geometry
In the sample, detector distance $$x=2r$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Use Energy to Connect Speed and Voltage

  • At the source, the ion starts nearly from rest and is accelerated through a potential difference $$V.$$
  • So the gain in kinetic energy is $$qV=\tfrac12 mv^2. $$
  • This gives $$v=\sqrt{\dfrac{2qV}{m}}. $$

Room 2: Use Circular Motion Inside the Magnetic Field

  • Inside the separator chamber, the ion moves perpendicular to the magnetic field.
  • So $$r=\dfrac{mv}{qB}. $$
  • This is the magnetic-field measurement link.

Room 3: Combine the Two Relations

  • Substitute $$v=\sqrt{2qV/m}$$ into $$r=mv/(qB).$$
  • After simplification, $$r=\dfrac{1}{B}\sqrt{\dfrac{2mV}{q}}. $$
  • Now the unknown mass appears explicitly.

Room 4: Use the Geometry of the Semicircle

  • In the sample apparatus, the detector is hit after a semicircle, so $$x=2r.$$
  • Therefore $$r=x/2.$$
  • Substitute that geometric fact into the radius formula before solving for the mass.

Room 5: Solve for the Mass

  • With $$r=x/2,$$ the algebra gives $$m=\dfrac{B^2qx^2}{8V}. $$
  • Substituting the sample values gives $$m\approx3.386\times10^{-25}\,\text{kg}. $$
  • That corresponds to about $$203.93\,\text{u}. $$

Key Shortcut

Mass spectrometer problems are an energy equation glued to a circular-motion equation.

Main Tidbit

The magnetic field does not supply the speed here. The voltage does. The magnetic field only sorts the path by curvature.

Solution Flow

Energy first, circular motion second, geometry third, mass last.
