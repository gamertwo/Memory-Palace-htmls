28-3 Field Picture

Magnetic Field Lines and Magnets

This page is the visual partner to the force law pages. It keeps only the field-line rules that actually help memory and problem solving: tangent gives direction, line density gives strength, the loops close, and outside a magnet the lines run from north pole to south pole.

$$\text{Field direction} = \text{tangent to field line},\qquad \text{stronger field} \Rightarrow \text{denser lines}$$
Chapter GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
Direction Rule
The tangent to a field line gives the local direction of $$\vec B$$
Strength Rule
Closer line spacing means a larger magnetic field
Pole Rule
Outside the magnet, lines go from north pole to south pole
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Read Direction and Strength from Field Lines

  • At any point, the tangent to the field line gives the direction of $$\vec B.$$
  • The spacing of the lines shows the magnitude of the field.
  • Where the lines are packed more tightly, the field is stronger.

Room 2: Lock the Closed-Loop Property

  • Magnetic field lines form closed loops rather than beginning and ending in empty space.
  • Outside the magnet they travel from north pole to south pole.
  • Inside the magnet they complete the loop back from south to north.

Room 3: Compare Bar, Horseshoe, and C-Shaped Magnets

  • A bar magnet spreads the field around both ends, but the field is strongest near the poles.
  • A horseshoe or C-shaped magnet bends the poles closer together, making the field between them more nearly uniform.
  • The geometry changes the field pattern, but not the pole rule.

Room 4: Keep the Pole Interaction Facts

  • Opposite magnetic poles attract each other.
  • Like magnetic poles repel each other.
  • This is the shortest useful memory hook for magnet interactions.

Room 5: Attach the Earth-Field Tidbit Carefully

  • Earth acts like a giant magnet, which is why a compass needle aligns with Earth’s magnetic field.
  • The geography labels are tricky, but the practical takeaway is that Earth has a large-scale magnetic field with pole behavior.
  • Use this as a supporting picture, not as the main thing to memorize from the section.

Key Shortcut

If you see a field-line picture, read it in this order: tangent for direction, spacing for strength, poles for overall flow.

Main Tidbit

The closed-loop idea separates magnetic field lines from the simpler arrow pictures often used at first contact.

Solution Flow

Read the picture, then the loop rule, then the pole rule, then the magnet-shape contrast.
