28-3 Sample Problem

Magnetic Force on a Moving Charged Particle

This page mirrors the sample problem in your screenshot, but trims it to the usable route. First get the speed from kinetic energy, then use the magnetic-force magnitude formula, then determine the deflection direction with the right-hand rule. The memory emphasis is that the small force still gives a big acceleration because the proton mass is tiny.

$$v=\sqrt{\frac{2K}{m}},\qquad F_B=|q|vB\sin\phi,\qquad a=\frac{F_B}{m}$$
Chapter GuideOpen in Memory Builder
What You Are Asked To Find
Given
A proton with kinetic energy $$5.3\,\text{MeV}$$ moves north in a chamber where $$B=1.2\,\text{mT}$$ points upward.
Find
Find the magnetic-force magnitude and its direction.
Extra
Then note the acceleration using $$a=F_B/m.$$
Quick Data
Charge
$$q=1.60\times10^{-19}\,\text{C}$$
Mass
$$m=1.67\times10^{-27}\,\text{kg}$$
Perpendicular Setup
North and up are perpendicular, so $$\phi=90^\circ$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Start with the Kinetic-Energy Speed Formula

  • The magnetic-force formula needs the speed, so start from $$K=\tfrac12 mv^2.$$
  • Solving for speed gives $$v=\sqrt{\dfrac{2K}{m}}.$$
  • Convert $$5.3\,\text{MeV}$$ into joules and substitute the proton mass.

Room 2: Compute the Proton Speed

  • Use $$K=(5.3\times10^6\,\text{eV})(1.60\times10^{-19}\,\text{J/eV})=8.48\times10^{-13}\,\text{J}.$$
  • Then $$v=\sqrt{\dfrac{2(8.48\times10^{-13})}{1.67\times10^{-27}}}\approx3.2\times10^7\,\text{m/s}.$$
  • This is the same speed scale shown in the sample solution.

Room 3: Use the Magnetic-Force Magnitude Formula

  • Now apply $$F_B=|q|vB\sin\phi.$$
  • Here the proton moves north while the field points upward, so $$\phi=90^\circ$$ and $$\sin\phi=1.$$
  • Therefore $$F_B=(1.60\times10^{-19})(3.2\times10^7)(1.2\times10^{-3})\approx6.1\times10^{-15}\,\text{N}.$$

Room 4: Determine the Direction from $\vec v\times\vec B$

  • Use the right-hand rule with $$\vec v$$ toward north and $$\vec B$$ upward.
  • The cross product points toward the east.
  • Because the proton is positively charged, the magnetic force points east as well.

Room 5: Convert the Small Force into a Large Acceleration

  • Even though the force is small, the proton mass is extremely small too.
  • So $$a=\dfrac{F_B}{m}=\dfrac{6.1\times10^{-15}}{1.67\times10^{-27}}\approx3.7\times10^{12}\,\text{m/s}^2.$$
  • The useful interpretation is that magnetic forces can bend charged-particle paths dramatically.

Key Shortcut

In moving-charge sample problems, solve speed first if kinetic energy is given, then force, then direction.

Main Tidbit

Small force does not mean small effect when the particle mass is microscopic.

Solution Flow

Extract speed from energy, use the perpendicular force formula, then finish with direction and acceleration.
