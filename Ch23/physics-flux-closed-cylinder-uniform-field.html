23-3 | Closed Cylinder in Uniform Field

Flux Through a Closed Cylinder

Here the electric field is uniform and parallel to the cylinder axis. That makes the problem a clean test of flux signs: one cap contributes negative flux, the curved wall contributes zero, and the other cap contributes equal positive flux, so the total through the closed surface vanishes.

$$\Phi=\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A=\int_a \vec E\cdot d\vec A+\int_b \vec E\cdot d\vec A+\int_c \vec E\cdot d\vec A=-EA+0+EA=0$$

What To Watch

  • Left cap: outward area vector points opposite the field.
  • Curved wall: field is parallel to the surface, so normal component is zero.
  • Right cap: outward area vector points with the field.
Quick Anchors
Uniform Field
The field has the same magnitude and direction everywhere in the problem.
Left Cap
The angle is $$180^\circ$$, so the cap contributes negative flux.
Curved Surface
The angle is $$90^\circ$$ everywhere, so the cylindrical wall contributes zero.
Right Cap
The angle is $$0^\circ$$, so the cap contributes positive flux.
Rooms

Room 1: Split the Surface into Three Parts

  • The closed cylinder is made of a left cap, a curved wall, and a right cap.
  • Flux through the whole surface is the sum of flux through these three pieces.
  • That turns one closed-surface problem into three smaller ones.

Room 2: Left Cap Gives Negative Flux

  • The outward area vector on the left cap points left, opposite the field.
  • So $$\theta=180^\circ$$ and $$\cos 180^\circ=-1.$$
  • Therefore $$\int_a \vec E\cdot d\vec A = -E\int dA=-EA.$$

Room 3: Curved Wall Gives Zero Flux

  • On the cylindrical wall, each outward area vector points sideways.
  • The uniform field runs along the axis, so it is perpendicular to every wall normal.
  • Thus $$\theta=90^\circ$$ and $$\int_b \vec E\cdot d\vec A=E\cos 90^\circ \int dA=0.$$

Room 4: Right Cap Gives Positive Flux

  • The outward area vector on the right cap points right, the same way as the field.
  • So $$\theta=0^\circ$$ and $$\cos 0^\circ=1.$$
  • That gives $$\int_c \vec E\cdot d\vec A=E\int dA=EA.$$

Room 5: Add the Three Contributions

  • Combine the pieces:
  • $$\Phi=-EA+0+EA.$$
  • The negative and positive cap contributions cancel exactly.

Room 6: Total Flux Is Zero

  • The final answer is $$\Phi=0.$$
  • This does not mean the field is zero; it means the net crossing through the closed surface is zero.
  • As much field leaves through one cap as enters through the other.

Room 7: Why This Matters for Gauss's Law

  • A zero net flux here matches the fact that no charge is enclosed by the cylinder.
  • The field can pass through a Gaussian surface without creating net flux if entry and exit balance.
  • That is a key intuition for later enclosed-charge problems.

Cylinder Breakdown

Separating the closed surface into two end caps and one curved wall makes the flux signs almost automatic.

Sign Memory

Outward area vectors always define the sign. If the field points against the outward normal, the flux is negative. If it points with the outward normal, the flux is positive.

Common Mistake

Do not say the wall has zero flux because it has zero area. The wall area is not zero. Its flux is zero because the field has no normal component there.

Concept Check

A uniform field threading an empty closed surface can still give zero net flux because every entering line is balanced by a leaving line.
