23-8 | Applying Gauss's Law: Planar Symmetry

Planar Symmetry and Two Plates

This page isolates the planar-symmetry ideas from the previous derivation and pushes them into superposition. First, a pillbox through a uniformly charged nonconducting sheet gives the single-sheet field. Then two plates and two oppositely charged sheets show how fields cancel or reinforce in different regions.

$$\varepsilon_0(EA+EA)=\sigma A,\qquad E=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0},\qquad E_{\text{between plates}}=\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$$

Planar Rules

  • Single nonconducting sheet: $$E=\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0).$$
  • Two equal opposite sheets: outside cancels, inside adds.
  • Region-by-region fields come from superposition.
Quick Anchors
Single Sheet
For one infinite nonconducting sheet, the field has magnitude $$\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0)$$ on either side.
Pillbox
Flux passes only through the two flat faces, not through the curved side.
Two Plates
Equal-magnitude sheet fields can add between plates and cancel outside.
Example Logic
Left, between, and right regions are solved by vector addition of the two sheet fields.
Rooms

Room 1: Isolate the Single-Sheet Result

  • Choose a cylindrical pillbox through an infinite uniformly charged nonconducting sheet.
  • The field is perpendicular to the sheet on both sides.
  • The curved surface contributes zero flux.

Room 2: Solve the Pillbox Algebra

  • The two flat faces contribute $$EA+EA.$$
  • The enclosed charge is $$\sigma A.$$
  • So $$\varepsilon_0(EA+EA)=\sigma A,$$ giving $$E=\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0).$$

Room 3: Two Conducting Plates Double the Inside Field

  • For two oppositely charged parallel plates, each plate contributes a field of magnitude $$\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0).$$
  • Between the plates, the two fields point the same way and add.
  • That gives $$E=\sigma/\varepsilon_0$$ between the plates.

Room 4: Outside the Plates the Fields Cancel

  • To the far left or far right of the two-plate system, the fields from the two plates point opposite ways.
  • If their magnitudes are equal, the net field outside is zero.
  • This is the classic capacitor-style field pattern.

Room 5: Nonconducting-Sheet Example Starts with Two Separate Fields

  • For a positively charged sheet, field points away from the sheet.
  • For a negatively charged sheet, field points toward the sheet.
  • Each sheet still has magnitude $$|\sigma|/(2\varepsilon_0).$$

Room 6: Region L Uses Opposing Contributions

  • On the left side of both sheets, one field points left and the other points right.
  • The net field is the difference in magnitudes.
  • Direction follows the stronger sheet's contribution.

Room 7: Region B Uses Reinforcing Contributions

  • Between a positive sheet on the left and a negative sheet on the right, both fields point from positive toward negative.
  • The magnitudes add in this middle region.
  • So the between-region field is the largest of the three regions.

Room 8: Region R Uses Opposing Contributions Again

  • On the right side of both sheets, the two fields oppose each other.
  • The net field is again a difference, with direction set by the stronger contribution.
  • The left and right regions are often mirror-style bookkeeping problems.

Room 9: Numerical Example Pattern

  • Compute each sheet's field separately from $$E=|\sigma|/(2\varepsilon_0).$$
  • Then combine them in L, B, and R using vector addition.
  • This avoids memorizing special-case formulas when the charge magnitudes differ.

Room 10: Main Lesson

  • Gauss's law gives the field of one infinite sheet cleanly.
  • Superposition then handles multiple sheets and multiple regions.
  • The right habit is: derive once, then add vectors carefully.

Two Plates and Regions

Best Calculation Habit

Do not guess the final direction by memory alone. Draw the direction of each sheet's field in each region first, then add the vectors. That removes almost all sign mistakes.

Memory Palace Path

Use this as a planar-superposition route: one sheet first, then two sheets split into three regions.

Station 1

A pillbox punches through a giant charged sheet while only its two end caps begin to glow.

Trigger

The curved wall fades out completely, forcing all flux through the two faces.

Station 2

The pillbox solves into $$E=\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0),$$ which you store as the single-sheet field.

Trigger

A second sheet slides in, splitting the floor into left, between, and right zones.

Station 3

In the left zone, two arrows oppose; in the middle zone, two arrows reinforce; in the right zone, they oppose again.

Trigger

Each zone asks you to draw both sheet fields separately before combining them.

Station 4

The middle corridor brightens the most because it is the addition region between unlike sheets.

Trigger

The outside corridors dim when equal contributions cancel.

Station 5

The exit plaque reads: derive one-sheet field once, then use superposition region by region.
