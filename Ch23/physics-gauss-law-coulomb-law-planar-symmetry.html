Gauss's Law, Point Charge, and Planar Symmetry

This page uses Gauss's law in its most famous high-symmetry settings. First, a spherical Gaussian surface centered on a point charge recovers the same inverse-square field found from Coulomb's law. Then a cylindrical Gaussian surface applied to a nonconducting sheet gives the constant field result for planar symmetry.

$$\varepsilon_0\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A=q_{\mathrm{enc}},\qquad E=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2},\qquad E=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$$

Two Symmetries

  • Spherical symmetry makes $$E$$ constant on a sphere.
  • Planar symmetry makes flux pass through only the two flat faces of a pillbox.
  • The curved side of the pillbox contributes zero flux.
Quick Anchors
Point Charge
On a spherical Gaussian surface, the field magnitude is constant at radius $$r.$$
Sphere Flux
The surface area is $$4\pi r^2,$$ so the flux becomes $$E(4\pi r^2).$$
Sheet Symmetry
For a uniformly charged sheet, field lines are perpendicular to the sheet on both sides.
Pillbox Result
The sheet field is $$E=\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0)$$ for an infinite nonconducting sheet.
Rooms

Room 1: Start with the Gauss's-Law Form

  • Use $$\varepsilon_0\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A=q_{\mathrm{enc}}.$$
  • The main job is to choose a Gaussian surface that matches the symmetry.
  • Then the flux integral simplifies into area times field.

Room 2: Point Charge Uses a Sphere

  • For a single point charge $$q,$$ choose a spherical Gaussian surface centered on the charge.
  • At every point on the sphere, the field is radial and has the same magnitude.
  • That makes $$E$$ a constant in the integral.

Room 3: Pull the Constant Field Outside

  • Since $$E$$ is constant and parallel to $$d\vec A,$$ the flux becomes
  • $$\varepsilon_0\oint E\,dA=q.$$
  • The remaining integral is just the total spherical area.

Room 4: Recover Coulomb's-Law Result

  • Using $$\oint dA=4\pi r^2,$$ we get
  • $$\varepsilon_0 E(4\pi r^2)=q.$$
  • So $$E=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2},$$ exactly the Coulomb-law field for a point charge.

Room 5: Now Switch to Planar Symmetry

  • For an infinite uniformly charged nonconducting sheet, field lines are perpendicular to the sheet and point away on both sides.
  • A closed cylindrical pillbox is the natural Gaussian surface.
  • The two flat faces catch the flux, while the curved wall does not.

Room 6: Curved Wall Contributes Zero

  • On the curved surface, the field is parallel to the wall.
  • That means $$\vec E\cdot d\vec A=0$$ there.
  • Only the two end caps contribute to the total flux.

Room 7: Add the Two Face Contributions

  • Each flat face contributes $$EA.$$
  • So the total flux is $$2EA.$$
  • The enclosed charge is $$q_{\mathrm{enc}}=\sigma A,$$ where $$A$$ is the cap area inside the sheet.

Room 8: Solve for the Sheet Field

  • Gauss's law gives $$\varepsilon_0(2EA)=\sigma A.$$
  • Cancel $$A$$ to get
  • $$E=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}.$$

Room 9: Main Lesson

  • Gauss's law reproduces familiar field formulas when the symmetry is strong enough.
  • Sphere for point charge, pillbox for sheet.
  • The surface choice is what turns the law from true into useful.

Fast Diagnostic

If you cannot argue that the field is constant over a chosen part of the surface and either parallel or perpendicular to $$d\vec A,$$ the Gaussian surface is probably not the right one.
