Spherical Symmetry Results

This page is the radius story. As you move from outside to inside, remember what charge is enclosed and how that changes the final field formula.

Start Formula
$$\varepsilon_0 E(4\pi r^2)=q_{enc}$$
End Formula
Shell inside: $$E=0$$, outside: $$E=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}$$, solid sphere inside: $$E=\frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0R^3}r$$
Rooms

Room 1: Use a Spherical Surface

  • Spherical symmetry asks for a spherical Gaussian surface.
  • That keeps E constant on the surface.

Room 2: Outside Any Sphere

  • Outside, the full charge is enclosed.
  • The result behaves like a point charge.

Room 3: Inside a Shell

  • Inside a spherical shell, no charge is enclosed.
  • So the field is zero.

Room 4: Inside a Solid Sphere

  • Inside a uniformly charged solid sphere, only part of the total charge is enclosed.
  • The enclosed amount grows with volume.

Room 5: Radius Controls Charge

  • The enclosed charge scales like r cubed for the solid sphere interior.
  • That changes the final field behavior.

Room 6: Final Radius Story

  • Shell interior is zero.
  • Solid interior grows with r, and outside falls like one over r squared.
Simple Summary

For spherical symmetry, the outside looks like a point charge, the shell interior has zero field, and the solid-sphere interior grows with radius because the enclosed charge grows with volume.
