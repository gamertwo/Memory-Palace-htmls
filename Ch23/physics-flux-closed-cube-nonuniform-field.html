23-4 | Closed Cube in Nonuniform Field

Flux Through a Closed Cube

This example upgrades the bookkeeping. The field is no longer uniform everywhere in the same way: $$\vec E=3.0x\,\hat i+4.0\,\hat j.$$ That means the x-component changes from face to face, while the y-component stays constant, so different cube faces must be handled on their own terms.

$$\vec E=3.0x\,\hat i+4.0\,\hat j,\qquad \Phi=\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A$$

Key Pattern

  • Right and left faces care about the x-component.
  • Top and bottom faces care about the y-component.
  • Front and back faces are not used here because the field has no z-component.
Quick Anchors
Field
The x-part is $$3.0x$$, so it depends on which x-face you are on.
Constant Part
The y-part is $$4.0$$, so top and bottom faces see equal-magnitude contributions.
Area
With side lengths $$x=1.0\ \mathrm m$$ and $$z=3.0\ \mathrm m,$$ each shown face has area $$4.0\ \mathrm{m^2}.$$
Method
Match each face normal to the field component that points along it.
Rooms

Room 1: Separate the Cube by Face Normals

  • A closed cube has six faces, but each face only cares about the field component parallel to its outward normal.
  • The x-faces use the $$\hat i$$ part of the field.
  • The y-faces use the $$\hat j$$ part of the field.

Room 2: Right Face Uses the x-Component at Larger x

  • On the right face, the outward area vector is $$d\vec A=(dA)\hat i.$$
  • So only the $$3.0x\,\hat i$$ part contributes.
  • Because the right face sits at the larger x-value, that face picks up the larger positive x-field.

Room 3: Right Face Flux Is Positive

  • $$\Phi_r=\int \vec E\cdot d\vec A=\int (3.0x\,\hat i+4.0\,\hat j)\cdot (dA\,\hat i)$$
  • $$\Phi_r=\int 3.0x\,dA$$
  • Using the face's x-value and area from the problem gives a positive contribution of $$36\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}.$$

Room 4: Left Face Uses the x-Component at Smaller x

  • On the left face, the outward area vector points in the negative x-direction: $$d\vec A=-(dA)\hat i.$$
  • The same x-component contributes, but now the normal flips the sign.
  • That face gives a negative flux of $$-12\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}.$$

Room 5: Top and Bottom Use the Constant y-Component

  • For the top face, $$d\vec A=(dA)\hat j,$$ so the constant $$4.0\,\hat j$$ part contributes positively.
  • That gives $$\Phi_t=4.0\int dA=16\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}.$$
  • The bottom face has the opposite normal, so it contributes $$-16\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}.$$

Room 6: Front and Back Contribute Zero

  • The remaining faces point along $$\pm\hat k.$$
  • The field has no $$\hat k$$ component.
  • So the dot product on those faces is zero.

Room 7: Add the Face Contributions

  • The y-face pair cancels: $$+16-16=0.$$
  • The front and back pair contribute zero.
  • The net flux comes from the unequal x-face pair: $$36-12=24\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}.$$

Room 8: Why the Net Flux Is Not Zero

  • The x-component of the field grows with x, so more field exits the right face than enters the left face.
  • This imbalance creates positive net flux through the closed cube.
  • That is the signature of enclosed charge according to Gauss's law.

Component View

The orange x-arrow changes with position, but the blue y-arrow stays constant. That is why opposite faces do not all cancel in the same way.

Fast Rule

On any face, only the field component parallel to that face's outward normal can contribute to flux.

Why Right Beats Left

The right face sits at larger x, so the value of $$3.0x$$ is larger there. A growing field makes the outgoing x-flux exceed the incoming x-flux.

Gauss Link

A positive net flux means the cube encloses net positive charge. The exact amount would follow from $$q_{\mathrm{enc}}=\varepsilon_0\Phi.$$
