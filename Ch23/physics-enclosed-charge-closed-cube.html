Enclosed Charge in a Nonuniform Field

This page finishes the cube example. Once the total electric flux through the six faces is known, Gauss's law turns that flux into net enclosed charge. The conceptual jump is small, but it is the whole point of introducing Gaussian surfaces in the first place.

$$q_{\mathrm{enc}}=\varepsilon_0\Phi=(8.85\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{C^2/(N\cdot m^2)})(24\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C})\approx 2.1\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm C$$

One-Step Finish

  • Take the net flux from the previous page.
  • Multiply by $$\varepsilon_0.$$
  • Read the sign to identify whether the enclosed charge is net positive or negative.
Quick Anchors
Known Flux
From the cube page, the total flux is $$24\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}.$$
Gauss's Law
For a closed surface, $$q_{\mathrm{enc}}=\varepsilon_0\Phi.$$
Sign
Positive net flux implies net positive enclosed charge.
Result
The cube encloses approximately $$2.1\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm C.$$
Rooms

Room 1: Start from the Total Flux, Not from Scratch

  • The hard part was already done on the previous page by computing flux through each face.
  • Those six face contributions gave a net flux of $$24\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}.$$
  • Now Gauss's law converts that into charge.

Room 2: Write Gauss's Law in the Charge Form

  • Start from $$\Phi=\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A=\frac{q_{\mathrm{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}.$$
  • Solve for enclosed charge:
  • $$q_{\mathrm{enc}}=\varepsilon_0\Phi.$$

Room 3: Substitute the Known Flux

  • Insert $$\Phi=24\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}.$$
  • Also use $$\varepsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{C^2/(N\cdot m^2)}.$$
  • That makes the calculation numerical and direct.

Room 4: Multiply the Numbers

  • $$q_{\mathrm{enc}}=(8.85\times10^{-12})(24)$$
  • $$q_{\mathrm{enc}}\approx 2.1\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm C$$
  • The units reduce to coulombs, as they must for charge.

Room 5: Read the Sign Physically

  • The flux was positive, so more field leaves the surface than enters it.
  • That means the net enclosed charge is positive.
  • If the flux had been negative, the net enclosed charge would have been negative instead.

Room 6: Key Idea of the Whole Chapter

  • Gauss's law does not require us to know every charge location inside the surface.
  • It only gives the net enclosed charge.
  • Many positive and negative charges could be inside, but their algebraic sum must equal the value found.

Room 7: Final Answer

  • The cube encloses a net positive charge of about $$2.1\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm C.$$
  • This is the same physics statement as saying the cube has positive net outward flux.
  • The flux page and charge page are two ways of reading the same closed-surface information.
Key Idea
Key Idea

Flux First, Charge Second

The net enclosed charge in a real or mathematical closed surface is related to total electric flux by Gauss's law. That means a complicated field calculation can end in a very compact charge result once the total flux is known.

Compressed Summary

From the six cube faces we found $$\Phi=24\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C}.$$ Multiplying by $$\varepsilon_0$$ gives $$q_{\mathrm{enc}}\approx 2.1\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm C,$$ so the enclosure contains net positive charge.

What We Learn

Unequal outward and inward flux means the field is being sourced net from inside the surface. Positive net outward flux means a net positive enclosed source.

What We Do Not Learn

Gauss's law here does not tell us where the charge sits inside the cube or how it is distributed. It only reveals the total enclosed charge.

Bridge Forward

This is the same move we will use in the high-symmetry cases: simplify the flux, then read off the charge or the field from Gauss's law.
