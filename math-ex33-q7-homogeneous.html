Exercise 3.3 Question 7

This is homogeneous. Rewrite the right side in terms of $y/x$, put $y=vx$, and then separate the resulting $v$ equation.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{3xy+y^2}{3x^2}$$
Rooms

Room 1: Put in Ratio Form

  • Rewrite the numerator in ratio form:
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{3x y+y^2}{3x^2}=\frac{3\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)+\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^2}{3}.$$

Room 2: Use $y=vx$

  • Put $$y=vx,$$ so
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}=v+x\frac{dv}{dx}.$$
  • Then
  • $$v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=v+\frac{v^2}{3}.$$

Room 3: Simplify

  • Subtract $v$ from both sides:
  • $$x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{v^2}{3}.$$

Room 4: Separate Variables

  • Bring the $v$ terms left:
  • $$\frac{dv}{v^2}=\frac{dx}{3x}.$$

Room 5: Integrate

  • Integrate both sides:
  • $$\int v^{-2}\,dv=\int \frac{dx}{3x}.$$
  • $$-\frac{1}{v}=\frac{1}{3}\ln|x|+C.$$
  • Multiply by $-3$ and absorb the sign into the constant:
  • $$\frac{3}{v}=\ln|x|+C.$$

Room 6: Back-Substitute

  • Since $$v=\frac{y}{x},$$ we have $$\frac{3}{v}=\frac{3x}{y}.$$
  • So
  • $$\frac{3x}{y}=\ln|x|+C.$$
  • Multiply by $y$:
  • $$3x-y\ln|x|=Cy.$$
  • After absorbing the sign into the constant, the textbook form is
  • $$\boxed{3x+y\log x+Cy=0}$$
  • for $x>0$.
Final Answer

The homogeneous substitution gives $$\boxed{\frac{3x}{y}=\ln|x|+C}.$$ In the textbook's rearranged form for positive $x$, this is written as $$\boxed{3x+y\log x+Cy=0}.$$
