Exercise 7-27

A parallel RLC circuit has $$R=1\ \text{k}\Omega,$$ $$C=1\ \mu\text{F},$$ $$L=100\ \text{mH},$$ $$I_0=100\ \text{mA},$$ and $$V_0=0.$$ Plot the zero-input responses of the inductor, resistor, and capacitor currents on one axis, then show that Kirchhoff's current law holds at every instant.

$$i_L(t)=e^{-500t}\left(100\cos 3122.5t+16.0\sin 3122.5t\right)\ \text{mA}$$ $$i_R(t)=-32.0e^{-500t}\sin 3122.5t\ \text{mA},\qquad i_C(t)=e^{-500t}\left(-100\cos 3122.5t+16.0\sin 3122.5t\right)\ \text{mA}$$

Rooms

Room 1: Write the Parallel Characteristic Equation

  • For a zero-input parallel RLC circuit, use
  • $$LCs^2+\frac{L}{R}s+1=0.$$
  • Substitute the values:
  • $$10^{-7}s^2+10^{-4}s+1=0.$$
  • Normalized form:
  • $$s^2+1000s+10^7=0.$$

Room 2: Find the Roots and Pick the Current Template

  • The roots are
  • $$s_{1,2}=-500\pm j3122.5.$$
  • So the zero-input inductor current is underdamped:
  • $$i_L(t)=e^{-500t}\left(K_1\cos 3122.5t+K_2\sin 3122.5t\right).$$

Room 3: Use the Initial Current

  • At $$t=0,$$ the cosine term is 1 and the sine term is 0.
  • So
  • $$i_L(0)=K_1=100\ \text{mA}.$$

Room 4: Convert the Initial Voltage into a Derivative Condition

  • In the parallel circuit,
  • $$v_C(0)=v_L(0)=L\left.\frac{di_L}{dt}\right|_0.$$
  • Since $$v_C(0)=0,$$ we require
  • $$\left.\frac{di_L}{dt}\right|_0=0.$$
  • This gives
  • $$-500K_1+3122.5K_2=0.$$

Room 5: Solve for $$K_2$$ and Finish $$i_L(t)$$

  • With $$K_1=100,$$
  • $$K_2=\frac{500(100)}{3122.5}\approx 16.0.$$
  • Therefore
  • $$\boxed{i_L(t)=e^{-500t}\left(100\cos 3122.5t+16.0\sin 3122.5t\right)\ \text{mA}}.$$

Room 6: Differentiate to Get the Branch Voltage

  • Use $$v=L\,di_L/dt.$$
  • The derivative condition cancels the cosine term at the start, leaving
  • $$v_C(t)=v_L(t)=-32.0e^{-500t}\sin 3122.5t\ \text{V}. $$

Room 7: Use the Voltage to Get $$i_R(t)$$

  • Apply Ohm's law on the resistor branch:
  • $$i_R(t)=\frac{v_C(t)}{R}=\frac{-32.0e^{-500t}\sin 3122.5t}{1000}. $$
  • So
  • $$\boxed{i_R(t)=-32.0e^{-500t}\sin 3122.5t\ \text{mA}}.$$

Room 8: Differentiate the Voltage to Get $$i_C(t)$$

  • Use $$i_C=C\,dv_C/dt.$$
  • After differentiation and simplification,
  • $$\boxed{i_C(t)=e^{-500t}\left(-100\cos 3122.5t+16.0\sin 3122.5t\right)\ \text{mA}}.$$

Room 9: Validate KCL from the Three Currents

  • Add the three branch currents:
  • $$i_L+i_R+i_C=e^{-500t}\left(100\cos+16\sin-32\sin-100\cos+16\sin\right).$$
  • The cosine terms cancel and the sine terms also cancel, so
  • $$\boxed{i_L(t)+i_R(t)+i_C(t)=0}$$ for every $$t.$$

Fast Memory Rule

For a parallel zero-input problem, solve the current first, use the shared parallel voltage to get $$v(t),$$ then derive $$i_R$$ and $$i_C$$ from that same voltage. KCL becomes the final consistency check.

Memory Palace Path

This route starts with the parallel characteristic equation and ends with the sum of the three branch currents collapsing to zero.

Station 1: Parallel Gate

Store $$LCs^2+\frac{L}{R}s+1=0.$$

Transition 1 to 2

The equation immediately creates the underdamped root pair.

Station 2: Root Chamber

Keep $$-500\pm j3122.5$$ and the inductor-current template.

Transition 2 to 3

The first constant comes straight from the initial current.

Station 3: Current Lock

Use $$i_L(0)=100\ \text{mA}$$ to set $$K_1=100.$$

Transition 3 to 4

The zero initial voltage becomes a derivative condition.

Station 4: Derivative Bridge

Use $$v(0)=L\,di_L/dt|_0=0$$ to solve for $$K_2.$$

Transition 4 to 5

With the current fixed, the common branch voltage is forced next.

Station 5: Voltage Hall

Differentiate once to get $$v_C(t)=v_L(t).$$

Transition 5 to 6

The resistor and capacitor currents both come from the same voltage waveform.

Station 6: Branch Desk

Use $$i_R=v/R$$ and $$i_C=C\,dv/dt.$$

Transition 6 to 7

The final test is whether all branch currents satisfy KCL together.

Station 7: KCL Seal

Store the final identity $$i_L+i_R+i_C=0.$$
