⬡ The Universal First-Order RC Template
$$x(t) = \underbrace{x(\infty)}_{\text{forced}} + \underbrace{\bigl[x(0) - x(\infty)\bigr]}_{\text{difference}}\, e^{-t/\tau}$$
Every single first-order circuit response — no matter what the numbers are — fits this one skeleton. Your job is never to memorize the numbers. Your job is to read the skeleton and extract the bones.

Every time you see an equation with $e$ raised to a negative power of $t$, this skeleton flashes in your mind automatically. It's your pattern lock screen — the door opens only when you recognize it.
⏱
II
Kitchen
The Flip Move — Extracting τ
The exponent tells you everything about speed
👁 Pattern Trigger — You see this form:
$e^{-k\,t}$ where $k$ is a number
Your brain fires: "The coefficient in the exponent is $1/\tau$. Flip it."
Always and without exception: bigger $k$ = faster decay = smaller $\tau$.
The One Operation You Do
$$e^{-k\,t} \;\xrightarrow{\text{flip}}\; \tau = \frac{1}{k}$$
If you see $e^{-1000t}$ → $\tau = 1/1000 = 1\text{ ms}$
If you see $e^{-200t}$ → $\tau = 1/200 = 5\text{ ms}$
If you see $e^{-50t}$ → $\tau = 1/50 = 20\text{ ms}$
🏛 Memory Palace Scene (Kitchen)
In the Kitchen, a chef grabs a fraction and physically flips it upside-down, slamming it on the counter. The number on top becomes the bottom. That's all. Flip the exponent. Get τ. The faster it decays, the hotter the stove — large $k$ = small $\tau$ = very hot.
💡 Physical Intuition (Why This Works)
$\tau = RC$ for a series RC circuit. Bigger resistance or bigger capacitance → slower charge/discharge → bigger $\tau$. The exponent $k = 1/RC$ — it literally is 1 divided by the time constant.
🔬
III
Bedroom
The Freeze/Melt Moves — Boundary Conditions
Two key substitutions that extract initial and final values
❄️ FREEZE: $t=0$
$e^{0} = 1$
Every term is fully alive. No decay has happened yet. Sum them all → initial value $x(0)$
🔥 MELT: $t\to\infty$
$e^{-\infty} = 0$
Transient term dies completely. Only the non-exponential terms survive → forced response $x(\infty)$
The Two Substitutions
$$x(0) = A + B\cdot e^0 = A + B$$ $$x(\infty) = A + B\cdot 0 = A$$
🏛 Memory Palace Scene (Bedroom)
The bedroom has two light switches on opposite walls.

Left switch (FREEZE): you flip it OFF → time stops at $t=0$. The exponential is frozen at full amplitude ($e^0=1$). Read every term — that's your start.

Right switch (MELT): you hold it down until the exponential melts away to nothing. What's left glowing on the wall — just the DC term — that's your endpoint.
✂️
IV
Living
Room
The Split Move — Natural vs. Forced
Every response is two things glued together. Learn to see both.
The Decomposition — Always True
$$\underbrace{x(t)}_{\text{total}} = \underbrace{x(\infty)}_{\substack{\text{forced}\\\text{(DC input)}}} + \underbrace{[x(0)-x(\infty)]\,e^{-t/\tau}}_{\substack{\text{natural}\\\text{(circuit memory)}}}$$
  • →Forced = where the circuit is being driven to by the input source. Determined entirely by the source, not the initial state.
  • →Natural = how much the circuit deviates from forced, decaying away. It "remembers" the initial condition and forgets it at rate $1/\tau$.
  • →If $x(0) = x(\infty)$: natural term = 0, no transient. Already at steady state.
  • →If $x(0) = 0$: coefficient = $-x(\infty)$. Starts at zero, charges up to $x(\infty)$.
🏛 Memory Palace Scene (Living Room)
The living room sofa has a seam down the middle. Left cushion = forced (a glowing DC power cable plugged in — the input source owns it). Right cushion = natural (a spring that's compressed or stretched — the circuit's own memory, slowly releasing). Pull the cushions apart and you understand the whole response.

V
Study
Room
The Blueprint Move — Building the ODE
Read the solution backwards to find the equation that produced it
👁 Pattern Trigger
Given a response, reconstruct the governing equation
The ODE for any first-order circuit with a step input follows the same blueprint. Once you read $\tau$ and $x(\infty)$, the ODE is immediate.
Universal First-Order ODE Blueprint
$$\tau\,\frac{dx}{dt} + x = x(\infty)\cdot u(t)$$
Left side: τ × (rate of change) + current value = how the circuit behaves
Right side: the forcing function — what it's being driven toward × unit step
  • 1Read $\tau$ from the exponent (flip move from Room II)
  • 2Read $x(\infty)$ by melting the equation (melt move from Room III)
  • 3Plug both into $\tau\,\dot{x} + x = x(\infty)\,u(t)$ — done
🏛 Memory Palace Scene (Study Room)
The study room has a blueprint pinned to the wall — always the same blueprint. Left beam of the blueprint says "$\tau\,\dot{x}+x$". Right beam says "$x(\infty)$". You just fill in the numbers you already extracted from the other rooms. The blueprint never changes — only the numbers do.
