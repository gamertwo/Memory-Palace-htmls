Exercise 3.3 Question 2

This is another homogeneous differential equation. Rewrite it in $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ form, then apply the substitution $y=vx$ and reduce it to a separable equation.

$$(x^2-y^2)\,dx+2xy\,dy=0$$
Rooms

Room 1: Convert to Differential Form

  • Move to $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ form:
  • $$2xy\frac{dy}{dx}=-(x^2-y^2)=y^2-x^2.$$
  • So
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{y^2-x^2}{2xy}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{y}{x}-\frac{x}{y}\right).$$

Room 2: Identify the Homogeneous Pattern

  • The right side depends on the ratio $\dfrac{y}{x}$ and its reciprocal.
  • So put
  • $$y=vx \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \frac{dy}{dx}=v+x\frac{dv}{dx}.$$

Room 3: Substitute and Simplify

  • Replace $\dfrac{y}{x}$ by $v$ and $\dfrac{x}{y}$ by $\dfrac{1}{v}$:
  • $$v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{1}{2}\left(v-\frac{1}{v}\right).$$
  • Multiply by $2v$:
  • $$2v^2+2vx\frac{dv}{dx}=v^2-1.$$
  • Hence
  • $$2vx\frac{dv}{dx}=-(v^2+1).$$

Room 4: Separate Variables

  • Bring $v$ terms left and $x$ terms right:
  • $$\frac{2v}{v^2+1}\,dv=-\frac{dx}{x}.$$

Room 5: Integrate

  • Integrate both sides:
  • $$\int \frac{2v}{v^2+1}\,dv=-\int \frac{dx}{x}.$$
  • $$\ln(v^2+1)=-\ln|x|+C.$$
  • Combine the logarithms:
  • $$v^2+1=\frac{C}{x}.$$

Room 6: Back-Substitute

  • Since $$v=\frac{y}{x},$$
  • $$\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^2+1=\frac{C}{x}.$$
  • Multiply by $x^2$:
  • $$y^2+x^2=Cx.$$
  • So the final answer is
  • $$\boxed{x^2+y^2=Cx}.$$
Final Answer

After the homogeneous substitution $y=vx$, the equation reduces to a separable form and gives $$\boxed{x^2+y^2=Cx}.$$ This matches the textbook answer written as $$\boxed{x^2+y^2=ax}.$$
