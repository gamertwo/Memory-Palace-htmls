Sample Problem

Charge Sharing by Two Identical Conducting Spheres

One identical conducting sphere starts with charge $$+Q$$ and the other starts neutral. After they touch, the total charge is shared equally, and only then do we compute the repulsive force between the final charges.

$$q_1=q_2=\frac{Q}{2},\qquad F=\frac{1}{16\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{Q}{a}\right)^2$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Carry the Total Charge First

  • Before contact, the total charge is $$Q+0=Q.$$
  • Charge is conserved during the sharing process.
  • So the final charges must still add up to $$Q.$$

Room 2: Use Identical Conductors to Split the Charge Evenly

  • Because the spheres are identical conductors, the shared charge divides equally after contact.
  • That gives $$q_1=q_2=\dfrac{Q}{2}.$$
  • This is the key physical shortcut in the problem.

Room 3: Rebuild the Final Two-Charge Picture

  • After separation, the charges are $$Q/2$$ and $$Q/2$$ at distance $$a.$$
  • Both are positive, so the force is repulsive.
  • Now the problem is just a standard two-charge Coulomb calculation.

Room 4: Apply Coulomb's Law to the Shared Charges

  • Use $$F=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{|q_1||q_2|}{a^2}.$$
  • Substitute $$q_1=q_2=Q/2.$$
  • Then $$F=\dfrac{1}{16\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\dfrac{Q}{a}\right)^2.$$
