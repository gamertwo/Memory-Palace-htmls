Sample Problem

Mutual Electric Repulsion in a Nucleus

This sample problem compares two laws on the same pair of protons. First compute the electrostatic repulsion between two protons separated by $$4.0\times10^{-15}\text{ m}.$$ Then compare that with the tiny gravitational attraction between those same particles.

$$F_e=14\text{ N},\qquad F_g=1.2\times10^{-35}\text{ N}$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Use the Proton Charge in Coulomb's Law

  • Each proton carries charge $$e=1.602\times10^{-19}\text{ C}.$$ The separation is $$r=4.0\times10^{-15}\text{ m}.$$
  • Use $$F_e=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{e^2}{r^2}.$$
  • Substitution gives $$F_e=14\text{ N}.$$

Room 2: Compare With Gravitational Attraction

  • For the same two protons, use $$F_g=G\dfrac{m_p^2}{r^2}$$ with $$m_p=1.67\times10^{-27}\text{ kg}.$$
  • This gives only $$F_g=1.2\times10^{-35}\text{ N}.$$
  • The electric repulsion is enormously larger than the gravitational attraction.

Room 3: Read the Physical Meaning of the Contrast

  • The same pair of particles gives wildly different force scales under the two laws.
  • This explains why electrostatics cannot be ignored at nuclear separations.
  • It also shows why gravity alone cannot account for proton binding inside nuclei.
