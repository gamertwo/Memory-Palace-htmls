Sample Problem

Net Force with an Angled Charge

This is the component version of the previous problem. Particle 4 is now placed at angle $$\theta=60^\circ$$ and distance $$3R/4$$ from particle 1. The new move is to split the force from particle 4 into x and y components before adding it to the old x-direction force.

$$\vec F_{14}=(1.025\times10^{-24})\hat i+(1.775\times10^{-24})\hat j,\qquad |\vec F_{1,net}|=1.78\times10^{-24}\text{ N}$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Keep the Old Force from Particle 2

  • From the earlier arrangement, the force from particle 2 on particle 1 is still $$\vec F_{12}=-(1.15\times10^{-24}\text{ N})\hat i.$$
  • This gives the leftward x-part of the final vector.
  • Now the new work is entirely about the angled force from particle 4.

Room 2: Compute the Magnitude of the Angled Force

  • Particle 4 has charge $$q_4=-3.20\times10^{-19}\text{ C}$$ and lies at distance $$3R/4$$ from particle 1.
  • Using Coulomb's law gives $$F_{14}=2.05\times10^{-24}\text{ N}.$$
  • Because the charges are unlike, particle 4 attracts particle 1 toward the angled line.

Room 3: Split the Angled Force into Components

  • Resolve $$\vec F_{14}$$ as $$\vec F_{14}=(F_{14}\cos\theta)\hat i+(F_{14}\sin\theta)\hat j.$$
  • With $$F_{14}=2.05\times10^{-24}\text{ N}$$ and $$\theta=60^\circ,$$ this becomes $$\vec F_{14}=(1.025\times10^{-24})\hat i+(1.775\times10^{-24})\hat j.$$
  • Now the force is ready for vector addition.

Room 4: Add x and y Separately

  • Combine the x-parts: $$F_{1,net,x}=-1.15\times10^{-24}+1.025\times10^{-24}=-1.25\times10^{-25}\text{ N}.$$
  • The y-part comes only from particle 4: $$F_{1,net,y}=1.775\times10^{-24}\text{ N}.$$
  • So $$\vec F_{1,net}\approx(-1.25\times10^{-25})\hat i+(1.78\times10^{-24})\hat j.$$

Room 5: Recover Magnitude and Direction

  • The magnitude is $$|\vec F_{1,net}|=\sqrt{F_{1,net,x}^2+F_{1,net,y}^2}=1.78\times10^{-24}\text{ N}.$$
  • The direction comes from $$\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{F_{1,net,y}}{F_{1,net,x}}\right).$$
  • The force points almost straight upward, slightly to the left because the x-part is negative and small.
