Sample Problem

Equilibrium of Two Forces on a Particle

A proton is placed somewhere on the x-axis near charges $$q_1=+8q$$ at the origin and $$q_2=-2q$$ at $$x=L.$$ The trick is not starting with algebra. First find where the two forces could possibly point in opposite directions. Only then do you set their magnitudes equal.

$$\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{8qq_p}{x^2}=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2qq_p}{(x-L)^2},\qquad x=2L$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Search by Direction Before Algebra

  • Check three regions: between the charges, left of both, and right of both.
  • Between the charges, both forces point the same way, so they cannot cancel.
  • Left of both, one force is definitely stronger, so cancellation still fails.

Room 2: Identify the Only Viable Region

  • To the right of both charges, the repulsion from $$q_1$$ points right while the attraction toward $$q_2$$ points left.
  • That means cancellation is possible there.
  • Only in this region is the algebra worth doing.

Room 3: Set the Magnitudes Equal

  • If the proton sits at position $$x$$ to the right of both charges, its distances are $$x$$ from $$q_1$$ and $$x-L$$ from $$q_2.$$
  • Set the magnitudes equal: $$\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{8qq_p}{x^2}=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{2qq_p}{(x-L)^2}.$$
  • The common constants cancel immediately.

Room 4: Solve for the Equilibrium Position

  • The equation reduces to $$\left(\dfrac{x-L}{x}\right)^2=\dfrac14.$$
  • Taking the positive square root gives $$\dfrac{x-L}{x}=\dfrac12.$$
  • So $$x=2L.$$
