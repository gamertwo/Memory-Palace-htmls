Section 21-6

Charge Is Conserved

The chapter closes with the balance rule that powers many earlier problems. Charge may move from one object to another, and matter may transform in particle processes, but the total charge before and after remains the same.

$$e^-+e^+\rightarrow\gamma+\gamma,\qquad \gamma\rightarrow e^-+e^+$$

Rooms: Core Idea

Room 1: Total Charge Before Equals Total Charge After

  • Charge can move, split, and recombine, but the total amount is conserved.
  • This rule already appeared in the charge-sharing sphere problem.
  • Now it is stated as a general chapter principle.

Room 2: Pair Annihilation Still Conserves Charge

  • In $$e^-+e^+\rightarrow\gamma+\gamma,$$ the initial charges are $$-e$$ and $$+e,$$ which sum to zero.
  • The photons have zero charge, so the final total is also zero.
  • The particles change form, but charge balance is preserved.

Room 3: Pair Production Also Conserves Charge

  • In $$\gamma\rightarrow e^-+e^+,$$ the photon begins with zero charge.
  • The electron and positron end with charges $$-e$$ and $$+e,$$ so the total is still zero.
  • This is the same balance rule running in reverse.
