Sample Problem

Finding the Net Force Due to Two Other Particles

This sample problem trains the one-dimensional force movie. First find the force on particle 1 from particle 2. Then add a third charge, compute that extra force on particle 1, and combine the signed x-direction forces carefully.

$$\vec F_{12}=-(1.15\times10^{-24}\text{ N})\hat i,\qquad \vec F_{1,net}=(9.00\times10^{-25}\text{ N})\hat i$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Lock the Particle of Interest

  • The particle of interest is particle 1.
  • Every force vector must be drawn on particle 1.
  • This keeps the directions consistent through both parts.

Room 2: Compute the Force from Particle 2

  • Because $$q_1$$ and $$q_2$$ are both positive, particle 2 repels particle 1.
  • The force on particle 1 points left, so it is negative x.
  • Coulomb's law gives $$\vec F_{12}=-(1.15\times10^{-24}\text{ N})\hat i.$$

Room 3: Add the Third Charge and Recompute

  • Now $$q_3$$ is negative and sits at $$3R/4$$ from particle 1.
  • Unlike charges attract, so particle 3 pulls particle 1 to the right.
  • The added force is $$\vec F_{13}=(2.05\times10^{-24}\text{ N})\hat i.$$

Room 4: Add the Signed x-Forces

  • Both forces lie on the x-axis, so no component split is needed.
  • Add them algebraically: $$\vec F_{1,net}=\vec F_{12}+\vec F_{13}.$$
  • This gives $$\vec F_{1,net}=-(1.15\times10^{-24})\hat i+(2.05\times10^{-24})\hat i=(9.00\times10^{-25}\text{ N})\hat i.$$
