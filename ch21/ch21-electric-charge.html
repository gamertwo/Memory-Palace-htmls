Section 21-2

Electric Charge

This page locks the foundation of the chapter. An object can contain huge amounts of positive and negative charge at once, but if those amounts balance the object is neutral. Charge problems begin when that balance is broken and a net charge appears.

$$\text{neutral}:\ q_{net}=0,\qquad \text{charged}:\ q_{net}\neq 0$$

Quick Data
Electric Charge
An intrinsic property of the particles making up matter.
Electrically Neutral
Equal amounts of positive and negative charge, so the net charge is zero.
Charged Object
An imbalance of charge, so the net charge is nonzero.
Rooms: Core Idea

Room 1: Charge Is Built Into Matter

  • Electric charge is an intrinsic property of fundamental particles.
  • That is why everyday objects can participate in sparks and static effects.
  • The chapter begins by treating charge as something physically real and countable.

Room 2: Neutral Does Not Mean No Charge Exists

  • Most objects contain vast amounts of positive and negative charge.
  • If those amounts balance exactly, the object is electrically neutral.
  • Neutral means balanced charge, not absent charge.

Room 3: Net Charge Means Imbalance

  • If positive and negative charge do not balance, the object has a net charge.
  • We then say the object is charged.
  • The imbalance is usually small compared with the total charge inside the object.

Room 4: Static Effects Reveal Charge Imbalance

  • Carpet sparks, sweater cling, and similar effects are everyday clues that charge balance has been disturbed.
  • Those examples motivate why charge and electric force matter physically.
  • They lead directly into Coulomb's law on the next page.

Key Shortcut

Neutral means balanced charge. Charged means unbalanced charge. That one split drives the rest of Chapter 21.
