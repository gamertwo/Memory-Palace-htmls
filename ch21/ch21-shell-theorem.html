Shell Analogy

Uniform Charge Shell Rule

The shell theorem from gravitation has a direct electrostatics partner. Outside the shell, the charge acts as if concentrated at the center. Inside the shell, the net electrostatic force from the shell is zero.

$$\text{outside shell} \rightarrow \text{treat as point charge at center},\qquad \text{inside shell} \rightarrow \vec F=0$$

Rooms: Core Idea

Room 1: Outside the Shell, Collapse It to the Center

  • A shell of uniform charge attracts or repels an outside particle as if all the shell's charge were concentrated at the center.
  • This means outside problems can often be treated like ordinary point-charge problems.
  • The shell is visually spread out, but its force acts like one central charge.

Room 2: Inside the Shell, the Net Force Vanishes

  • If a charged particle is located inside a shell of uniform charge, the shell exerts no net electrostatic force on it.
  • The different parts of the shell cancel perfectly.
  • This is the inside-zero rule you want to memorize as a reflex.
