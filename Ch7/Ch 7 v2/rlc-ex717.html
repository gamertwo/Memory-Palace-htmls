Series RLC · Characteristic Roots

Example 7-17

A series RLC circuit has $C=0.25\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}$ and $L=1\ \text{H}.$ Find the characteristic roots for $R_T = 8.5\ \text{k}\Omega,\ 4\ \text{k}\Omega,$ and $1\ \text{k}\Omega.$ The key insight: compute $\omega_0$ once, then compare each $\alpha = R_T/(2L)$ against it — the comparison immediately reveals the root type before any arithmetic.

Characteristic Equation
$$LCs^2+R_TC s+1=0$$
Root Formula
$$s_{1,2}=-\alpha\pm\sqrt{\alpha^2-\omega_0^2}$$
Quick Data
Capacitance
$C=0.25\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}$
Inductance
$L=1\ \text{H}$
Undamped Frequency
$\omega_0 = 2000\ \text{rad/s}$
Damping Constant
$\alpha = R_T/(2L) = R_T/2$
Test Rule
Compare $\alpha$ vs $\omega_0$
Three Cases
A: over · B: critical · C: under
⚡
One Comparison Rule
Compute $\omega_0$ once. Then for each resistance, ask: is $\alpha$ greater than, equal to, or less than $\omega_0$? That single comparison tells the root pattern — overdamped, critically damped, or underdamped — before you calculate anything.

The Three Cases
🔩
Case A · Overdamped
Two Real Unequal Roots
Resistance $R_T = 8.5\ \text{k}\Omega$
α = 4250 $\alpha > \omega_0$ · radical is positive
Characteristic Roots
$s_{1,2} = -4250 \pm 3750$
$s_1 = -500,\quad s_2 = -8000$
The quantity under the radical is positive, so roots are real and unequal. No imaginary part means no oscillation — the system decays monotonically along two different exponential curves.
⚖️
Case B · Critical
One Repeated Real Root
Resistance $R_T = 4\ \text{k}\Omega$
α = 2000 $\alpha = \omega_0$ · radical is zero
Characteristic Roots
$s_{1,2} = -2000 \pm 0$
$s_1 = s_2 = -2000$
The radical collapses to zero, yielding one repeated real root. This is the knife-edge between non-oscillatory split-root behavior and oscillatory complex-root behavior.
🔔
Case C · Underdamped
Complex Conjugate Roots
Resistance $R_T = 1\ \text{k}\Omega$
α = 500 $\alpha < \omega_0$ · radical is negative
Characteristic Roots
$s_{1,2} = -500 \pm j500\sqrt{15}$
A negative radicand yields complex conjugate roots. The real part $-500$ sets the decay rate; the imaginary part $\pm 500\sqrt{15}$ sets the oscillation frequency.
Step-by-Step Solution
1
Write the Series Characteristic Equation
For a series RLC natural response, write $$LCs^2+R_TC s+1=0$$ once and keep it fixed. Substituting $L=1$ and $C=0.25\times 10^{-6}$ gives $0.25\times 10^{-6}s^2+0.25\times 10^{-6}R_T s+1=0.$
2
Compute the Common Benchmark $\omega_0$
$$\omega_0=\frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{0.25\times 10^{-6}}}=2000\ \text{rad/s}$$ This is computed once and never changes. Every case shares this reference.
3
Set Up the Comparison
The damping constant is $\alpha=\dfrac{R_T}{2L}=\dfrac{R_T}{2}$ because $L=1\ \text{H}.$ Each resistance only changes $\alpha.$ The moment you know $\alpha$, compare it against $\omega_0=2000$ to predict the root type immediately.
1
Compute $\alpha$
For $R_T=8500\ \Omega:$ $$\alpha=\frac{8500}{2\times 1}=4250\ \text{rad/s}$$
2
Compare with $\omega_0$
Since $4250 > 2000,$ this is Case A (overdamped). The discriminant $\alpha^2 - \omega_0^2 = 4250^2 - 2000^2$ is positive, so roots are real and unequal.
3
Calculate the Radical
$$\sqrt{\alpha^2-\omega_0^2}=\sqrt{4250^2-2000^2}=\sqrt{(3750)^2}=3750$$
4
Find the Roots
$$s_{1,2}=-4250\pm 3750$$ $$s_1=-4250+3750=\mathbf{-500}$$ $$s_2=-4250-3750=\mathbf{-8000}$$
1
Compute $\alpha$
For $R_T=4000\ \Omega:$ $$\alpha=\frac{4000}{2\times 1}=2000\ \text{rad/s}$$
2
Compare with $\omega_0$
$\alpha = \omega_0 = 2000.$ This is Case B (critically damped). The discriminant is exactly zero.
3
The Radical Collapses
$$\sqrt{\alpha^2-\omega_0^2}=\sqrt{4\times 10^6-4\times 10^6}=\sqrt{0}=0$$
4
Find the Roots
$$s_{1,2}=-2000\pm 0$$ $$s_1=s_2=\mathbf{-2000}$$ One repeated real root — the exact boundary between non-oscillatory and oscillatory behavior.
1
Compute $\alpha$
For $R_T=1000\ \Omega:$ $$\alpha=\frac{1000}{2\times 1}=500\ \text{rad/s}$$
2
Compare with $\omega_0$
Since $500 < 2000,$ this is Case C (underdamped). The discriminant $\alpha^2 - \omega_0^2 = 500^2 - 2000^2$ is negative → imaginary part → oscillation.
3
Handle the Negative Radicand
$$\sqrt{500^2-2000^2}=\sqrt{-15\cdot 500^2}=j\cdot 500\sqrt{15}$$
4
Find the Roots
$$s_{1,2}=\mathbf{-500\pm j500\sqrt{15}}$$ Real part $-500$ = decay rate. Imaginary part $\pm 500\sqrt{15}$ = oscillation frequency.

Full Detail Rooms
  • For a series RLC natural response, the characteristic equation is $LCs^2+R_TC s+1=0.$
  • Substitute $L=1$ and $C=0.25\times 10^{-6}:$
  • $0.25\times 10^{-6}s^2+0.25\times 10^{-6}R_T s+1=0.$
  • The undamped natural frequency is $\omega_0=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{0.25\times 10^{-6}}}=2000\ \text{rad/s}.$
  • The damping constant is $\alpha=\dfrac{R_T}{2L}=\dfrac{R_T}{2}$ because $L=1\ \text{H}.$
  • Now every case reduces to comparing $\alpha$ with $\omega_0.$
  • For $R_T=8500\ \Omega,$ the damping constant is $\alpha=4250.$
  • Since $4250>2000,$ this is Case A and the roots are $s_{1,2}=-\alpha\pm\sqrt{\alpha^2-\omega_0^2}.$
  • Compute the radical: $\sqrt{4250^2-2000^2}=\sqrt{(3750)^2}=3750.$
  • Therefore $s_{1,2}=-4250\pm 3750,$ so the roots are $-500$ and $-8000.$
  • The quantity under the radical is positive, so the roots are real and unequal.
  • That is the textbook signature of Case A, the overdamped case.
  • Nothing oscillates here because there is no imaginary part in the roots.
  • For $R_T=4000\ \Omega,$ the damping constant is $\alpha=2000.$
  • Now $\alpha=\omega_0,$ so the radical becomes zero.
  • The roots are $s_{1,2}=-2000\pm\sqrt{4\times 10^6-4\times 10^6}=-2000,-2000.$
  • A zero quantity under the radical gives one repeated real root.
  • That is Case B, the critically damped case.
  • It sits exactly between the nonoscillatory split-root case and the oscillatory complex-root case.
  • For $R_T=1000\ \Omega,$ the damping constant is $\alpha=500.$
  • Since $500 < 2000,$ this is Case C and the roots are $s_{1,2}=-500\pm\sqrt{500^2-2000^2}.$
  • The radical becomes $\sqrt{-15\cdot 500^2}=500\sqrt{-15}=j500\sqrt{15}.$
  • Therefore $s_{1,2}=-500\pm j500\sqrt{15}.$
  • A negative quantity under the radical gives complex conjugate roots.
  • That is Case C, the underdamped case.
  • The real part $-500$ sets the decay rate, while the imaginary part $\pm 500\sqrt{15}$ sets the oscillation.
