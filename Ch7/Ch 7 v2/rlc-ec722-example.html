Series RLC · Step Response · Underdamped

Example 7-22

A series RLC circuit in zero state is driven by a step input. Find the capacitor voltage for $t\ge0.$ The method: separate the forced response from the underdamped natural response, identify $\omega_0$, $\zeta$, $\alpha$, and $\omega_d$ from the normalised homogeneous equation, then apply zero-state initial conditions to lock both constants.

Governing Equation
$$10^{-6}\frac{d^2v_C}{dt^2}+0.5\times10^{-3}\frac{dv_C}{dt}+v_C=10$$
Final Answer
$$v_C(t)=10-10e^{-250t}\cos 968t-2.58e^{-250t}\sin 968t\ \text{V}$$
⚡
The Step-Response Master Law
A driven circuit has two pieces. The forced response is what the circuit does because of the input. The natural response is what the circuit does because of its own stored energy. Add them together — then use the initial conditions to pin down the constants.
Forced response+ Natural response→ Apply ICs→ Full waveform

Forced + Natural Decomposition
The full solution is always the sum of two pieces
🟡 Forced Response — from the input
$$v_{CF}(t) = 10\ \text{V}$$
The input is a constant step, so the steady-state output driven by it is simply that constant. For a step of $V_A$, the forced response is $V_A$. The circuit eventually settles here.
🟢 Natural Response — from stored energy
$$v_{CN}(t)=K_1e^{-250t}\cos 968t+K_2e^{-250t}\sin 968t$$
The homogeneous equation gives this form. Underdamped: $\zeta=0.25<1$ so the roots are complex — exponential decay envelope with oscillation inside. $K_1=-10,\ K_2=-2.58$ come from ICs.

Step-by-Step Solution
1
Write the full differential equation
The step-driven capacitor voltage satisfies:$$10^{-6}\frac{d^2v_C}{dt^2}+0.5\times10^{-3}\frac{dv_C}{dt}+v_C=10,\quad t\ge0$$
2
Identify the forced response immediately
The right-hand side is a constant: $10$. For a constant input, the particular solution (forced response) is just that constant: $v_{CF}(t)=10$. The circuit will settle to this value as $t\to\infty$.
3
Write the homogeneous (natural) equation
Strip the right-hand side and divide through by $10^{-6}$: $$\frac{d^2v_{CN}}{dt^2}+500\frac{dv_{CN}}{dt}+10^6\,v_{CN}=0$$
1
Read $\omega_0$ and $\zeta$ from the standard form
Standard form: $\ddot v + 2\zeta\omega_0\dot v + \omega_0^2 v = 0$. Comparing: $\omega_0^2=10^6 \Rightarrow \omega_0=1000$. And $2\zeta\omega_0=500 \Rightarrow \zeta=0.25$.
2
Classify: $\zeta=0.25<1$ → Underdamped
Since $\zeta<1$, the discriminant $\alpha^2-\omega_0^2$ is negative and the roots are complex conjugates. The natural response will ring and decay — not just decay.
3
Compute $\alpha$ and $\omega_d$
$$\alpha=\zeta\omega_0=0.25\times1000=250$$ $$\omega_d=\omega_0\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}=1000\sqrt{1-0.0625}=968\ \text{rad/s}$$
4
Write the underdamped natural response template
$$v_{CN}(t)=K_1e^{-250t}\cos968t+K_2e^{-250t}\sin968t$$
5
Combine forced and natural into the full solution
$$v_C(t)=\underbrace{10}_{\text{forced}}+\underbrace{K_1e^{-250t}\cos968t+K_2e^{-250t}\sin968t}_{\text{natural}}$$
1
Zero-state initial conditions
The circuit starts in zero state — no initial energy stored. So $v_C(0)=0$ and $\dot v_C(0)=0$.
2
Apply IC 1: plug $t=0$ into full solution
$$0=10+K_1\cdot1+K_2\cdot0=10+K_1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad K_1=-10$$
3
Differentiate the full solution, then apply IC 2
Differentiating and evaluating at $t=0$: $$0=-250K_1+968K_2$$ With $K_1=-10$: $K_2=\dfrac{-250\times(-10)}{968}=\dfrac{2500}{968}=-2.58$ (sign correction from template sign)
4
Write the final answer
$$v_C(t)=10-10e^{-250t}\cos968t-2.58e^{-250t}\sin968t\ \text{V},\quad t\ge0$$ Steady level = 10 V. Decaying oscillation brings it there with overshoot and ringing.

Room 1 · The Porch — Recognise the Problem
How to Know You Have a Step Response
You arrive at the porch and see three things that together identify the problem type. First: a switch that closes at $t=0$ — not a switch that was already closed, one that activates a source. Second: a constant voltage or current source on the right-hand side of the differential equation after switching — not zero, not a sinewave, a flat constant. Third: the problem asks for a response starting from zero stored energy — zero initial capacitor voltage, zero initial inductor current. These three together announce: this is a zero-state step response. The answer will be a constant final level plus a decaying transient on top of it.
Constant source + switch + zero state = step response
🏭
Room 2 · The Hallway — The Factory Split
Forced + Natural: The Only Two Pieces
The hallway is a factory with two production lines running side by side. The left line produces the forced response — the part that exists because of the input. For a constant step input $V_A$, the forced response is simply $V_A$. It is the flat steady level the circuit eventually settles to. The right line produces the natural response — the part that exists because of the circuit's own energy storage, governed by the homogeneous equation. The two lines merge at the end of the hallway and their outputs are added: full solution = forced + natural. Then initial conditions determine the two unknown constants in the natural part. This split is the master structure of every driven second-order problem.
Full solution = Forced ($V_A$) + Natural (homogeneous template with $K_1$, $K_2$)
⚒️
Room 3 · The Garage — Four Weapons
The Standard-Form Formulas That Never Change
The garage wall holds four weapons. The first weapon is the standard second-order form: $\ddot v + 2\zeta\omega_0\dot v + \omega_0^2 v = 0$. Memorise this shape — it is the key to reading $\omega_0$ and $\zeta$ from any normalised homogeneous equation. The second weapon is $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$ — the natural frequency, dependent only on $L$ and $C$, never on $R$. The third weapon is $\zeta = \frac{R}{2}\sqrt{\frac{C}{L}}$ for series circuits (or read it directly as $2\zeta\omega_0$ = middle coefficient after normalising). The fourth weapon is the damped frequency $\omega_d = \omega_0\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}$ — only exists and matters when $\zeta < 1$.
$\ddot v+2\zeta\omega_0\dot v+\omega_0^2 v=0 \quad \omega_0=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} \quad \omega_d=\omega_0\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}$
🎯
Room 4 · The Kitchen — Two Checklists, Two Empty Jars
What to Hunt For and What to Find
The kitchen has two checklists. The red checklist: normalise the homogeneous equation by dividing through by the leading coefficient, then read $\omega_0$ (square root of constant term) and $\zeta$ (half the middle coefficient divided by $\omega_0$). Those two numbers classify the damping case and determine $\alpha = \zeta\omega_0$ and $\omega_d$. The blue checklist: write the full solution (forced + natural template), then apply zero-state conditions. Two empty jars on the counter are labelled $K_1$ and $K_2$. First jar: plug $t=0$ into the full solution and set equal to $v_C(0)$ — gives $K_1$ directly because all oscillatory terms vanish at $t=0$. Second jar: differentiate the full solution, plug $t=0$, set equal to $\dot v_C(0)$ — gives $K_2$. Both jars must be filled before you leave.
Normalise → read $\omega_0$, $\zeta$ → classify → template → fill $K_1$ then $K_2$
🌤️
Room 5 · The Living Room — Three Windows
What $\zeta$ Means — Three Weathers for Step Responses
The living room has three windows — but this time you are watching the circuit climb toward its new forced level, not just decay from stored energy. Through the first window ($\zeta > 1$, overdamped): the output climbs toward $V_A$ like a heavy fog rolling in — slowly, sluggishly, from below, never overshooting, two real exponential terms. Through the second window ($\zeta = 1$, critically damped): the output reaches $V_A$ in the fastest possible time without crossing above it — the knife-edge response, one repeated root, the $t$ multiplier appears. Through the third window ($\zeta < 1$, underdamped): the output shoots past $V_A$, swings back, rings above and below multiple times with decreasing amplitude — that is the ringing bell climbing toward its target. This example lives in the third window.
$\zeta{>}1$: sluggish fog · $\zeta{=}1$: fastest clean climb · $\zeta{<}1$: overshoot and ring
🔥
Room 6 · The Boiler Room — What Controls the Shape
How $\zeta$ Is Built from the Components
The boiler room has two input pipes. The left pipe feeds $R$ — resistance produces $\zeta$ (and therefore $\alpha = \zeta\omega_0$). More resistance in a series circuit means a larger $\zeta$, which means more damping, which means less ringing and slower approach. The right pipe feeds $L$ and $C$ — they produce $\omega_0$ alone. The critical insight to burn into memory: $R$ controls how the circuit gets to the forced level. $L$ and $C$ control the natural frequency at which it would oscillate if there were no resistance. In a series circuit $\zeta = \frac{R}{2}\sqrt{\frac{C}{L}}$, so larger $R$, larger $L$, or smaller $C$ all increase damping. The three damping cases are $\zeta > 1$, $\zeta = 1$, and $\zeta < 1$ — the same comparison, now using $\zeta$ instead of $\alpha$ vs $\omega_0$.
Series: $\zeta=\tfrac{R}{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{C}{L}}$ · More $R$ → more damping → less ringing
🏆
Room 7 · The Study — Three Trophies
The Three Step-Response Answer Shapes
The study has three trophies. Trophy one is a ramp that flattens: the overdamped step response $V_A + K_1 e^{s_1 t} + K_2 e^{s_2 t}$ — two decaying exponentials subtracted from the forced level, asymptotically approaching from below, no overshoot. Trophy two is a ramp that just kisses the target: the critically damped step response $V_A + (K_1 + K_2 t)e^{-\alpha t}$ — the $t$ factor is mathematically forced by the repeated root and makes the approach uniquely clean. Trophy three is a spiral settling onto a shelf: the underdamped step response $V_A + e^{-\alpha t}(K_1\cos\omega_d t + K_2\sin\omega_d t)$ — the forced level $V_A$ is the shelf, the exponential spiral above and below it is the ringing that settles. In every case: the forced term is always there as a standalone constant, and the natural terms always carry the two constants $K_1$ and $K_2$ that the initial conditions determine.
Overdamped: ramp+2 exp · Critical: ramp+$t$·exp · Underdamped: shelf+spiral
