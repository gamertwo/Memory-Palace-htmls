Example 7-21

The switch has been open for a long time and closes at $t=0.$ Find the initial conditions, inductor current, and capacitor voltage for $t\ge0.$ The post-switch network is a zero-input parallel RLC — overdamped, two real unequal roots.

Initial Conditions
$$v_C(0)=0,\qquad i_L(0)=\frac{9}{250+50}=30\ \text{mA}$$
Final Answers
$$i_L(t)=30.3e^{-50.51t}-0.309e^{-4950t}\ \text{mA}$$ $$v_C(t)=-1.53e^{-50.51t}+1.53e^{-4950t}\ \text{V}$$

Step-by-Step Algebra
1
Inductor is a short at DC
Before $t=0$ the switch has been open long enough for all transients to die. The inductor has zero impedance to DC — it is a copper wire. All source current flows through it.
2
Capacitor is an open at DC
The capacitor blocks DC entirely. No current reaches it, so no voltage builds across it. Therefore $v_C(0)=0.$
3
KVL for the initial current
$$i_L(0)=\frac{9}{250+50}=\frac{9}{300}=30\ \text{mA}$$
1
Write the parallel characteristic equation
$$LCs^2+\frac{L}{R}s+1=0$$
2
Substitute values and find roots
$L=1,\ C=4\times10^{-6},\ R=50.$ Roots: $s_1=-50.51$ and $s_2=-4950.$
3
Two real unequal roots → overdamped template
$$i_L(t)=K_1e^{-50.51t}+K_2e^{-4950t}$$
1
Apply $i_L(0)=30\ \text{mA}$ — Equation 1
$$K_1+K_2=30\times10^{-3}$$
2
Convert $v_C(0)=0$ into derivative condition
$v_C(0)=v_L(0)=L\dot i_L(0)=0.$ Since $L=1$: $\dot i_L(0)=0.$
Differentiate template at $t=0$: $$-50.51K_1-4950K_2=0\quad\text{(Equation 2)}$$
3
Solve the 2×2 system
$K_1=30.3\ \text{mA},\quad K_2=-0.309\ \text{mA}$
$$i_L(t)=30.3e^{-50.51t}-0.309e^{-4950t}\ \text{mA}$$
1
Differentiate $i_L$ using $v_C=L\,di_L/dt$
$$v_C(t)=-1.53e^{-50.51t}+1.53e^{-4950t}\ \text{V}$$
2
Switch current from branch currents
$$i_{sw}(t)=\frac{v_C}{50}+\frac{9-v_C}{250}$$

The Porch — How to Recognise This Problem Type
Before you even open the door, you scan the porch for three clues. First: a light switch on the wall — it cuts time in half. Everything before it is frozen steady state. Everything after is natural response, driven by stored energy alone. Second: three components hanging side by side, sharing the same two wires — parallel topology, not a series chain. Third: a red stamp reading no source after switching — zero-input means the response comes entirely from what was already stored in the inductor and capacitor. Spot these three together and you know immediately: this is a zero-input parallel RLC natural response. That identification unlocks the door.
Switch · Parallel topology · Zero-input → this problem type
🏭
The Hallway — The Five-Station Assembly Line
Inside, the hallway is a factory floor. A conveyor belt runs through five numbered stations and every RLC problem — this one and every future one — must pass through all five in order. Station 1: freeze the pre-switch circuit, inductor becomes a short, capacitor becomes an open, read $i_L(0)$ and $v_C(0)$. Station 2: write the characteristic equation for the topology. Station 3: compute $\alpha$ and $\omega_0$, compare them, name the damping case. Station 4: write the response template that matches the case. Station 5: apply the two initial conditions to get two equations, solve for $K_1$ and $K_2$, write the final waveform. The belt only moves forward. No station can be skipped.
ICs → Char. Eq. → $\alpha$ vs $\omega_0$ → Template → Solve $K_1, K_2$
⚒️
The Garage — Four Weapons That Never Change
The garage has a blacksmith's bench. Four weapons hang on the wall — the formulas that are true regardless of what numbers the problem gives you. First: the characteristic equation — parallel form $LCs^2 + \frac{L}{R}s + 1 = 0$, series form $LCs^2 + R_T C s + 1 = 0$. Second: the damping constant $\alpha$ — forged from $R$ alone. Parallel: $\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC}$. Series: $\alpha = \frac{R_T}{2L}$. Third: the natural frequency $\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$ — forged from $L$ and $C$ only, never from $R$. Fourth: the root formula $s_{1,2} = -\alpha \pm \sqrt{\alpha^2 - \omega_0^2}$ — the blade that cuts the quadratic. These four weapons are the same in every problem.
$LCs^2+\tfrac{L}{R}s+1=0 \quad \alpha=\tfrac{1}{2RC} \quad \omega_0=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} \quad s=-\alpha\pm\sqrt{\alpha^2-\omega_0^2}$
🎯
The Kitchen — Two Checklists and Two Empty Jars
The kitchen has two checklists pinned to the wall and two empty jars on the counter. The red checklist is for before the switch: the inductor is a copper chain on the floor (short circuit, zero voltage) — read its current from the source network. The capacitor is a glass arch with no wires (open circuit) — read the voltage across it. Those two are the initial conditions you carry into the analysis. The blue checklist is for after the switch: topology, values of $R$, $L$, $C$, then compute $\alpha$ and $\omega_0$. The two jars are labelled $K_1$ and $K_2$ — you cannot leave until both are filled. The first jar is filled by plugging $t=0$ into the response template. The second jar is filled by using the inductor voltage law $v_L = L\,\dot{i}_L$ or capacitor current law $i_C = C\,\dot{v}_C$ to convert the second initial condition into a derivative condition, then differentiating the template and evaluating at $t=0$.
Before: $i_L(0)$ and $v_C(0)$ · After: $\alpha$, $\omega_0$, classify · Fill $K_1$ and $K_2$
🌤️
The Living Room — Three Windows, Three Weathers
The living room has three windows — look through each one and feel the damping case, not just calculate it. The first window shows heavy fog: two grey layers settling toward the ground at different speeds, no ripples, no ringing — that is overdamped. When $\alpha > \omega_0$ the radical is positive, the roots are two distinct negative real numbers, the response is two separate exponential decays and nothing oscillates. The second window shows perfectly still air, the fastest possible calm after a storm with zero lingering swirl — that is critically damped. When $\alpha = \omega_0$ the radical is zero, one repeated root, and the $t$ multiplier in the template is mathematically forced. The third window shows a bell ringing, each peal slightly quieter — that is underdamped. When $\alpha < \omega_0$ the radical is negative, roots are complex conjugates, the response oscillates while the exponential envelope shrinks. The real part of each root gives the decay rate. The imaginary part $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \alpha^2}$ gives the ringing frequency.
Fog: $\alpha{>}\omega_0$, real roots · Still air: $\alpha{=}\omega_0$, repeated root · Bell: $\alpha{<}\omega_0$, complex roots
🔥
The Boiler Room — What R Does and How Roots Are Born
The boiler room has two input pipes feeding a furnace. The left pipe is labelled R — feed resistance in and it produces $\alpha$ alone. $R$ cannot reach $\omega_0$ under any circumstances. The right pipe is labelled L and C — feed them in together and they produce $\omega_0$ alone. The most counter-intuitive fact in parallel circuits: more resistance means less damping. Because $\alpha = \frac{1}{2RC}$, a larger $R$ makes $\alpha$ smaller, pushing the circuit toward underdamped oscillation. In series circuits it is exactly the opposite — $\alpha = \frac{R_T}{2L}$ puts $R$ in the numerator, so more resistance means more damping. Once you have both $\alpha$ and $\omega_0$, the root blade cuts: positive under the radical gives two real roots, zero gives one repeated root, negative gives a complex conjugate pair where the imaginary part becomes the damped oscillation frequency $\omega_d$.
Parallel: $R\uparrow \Rightarrow \alpha\downarrow \Rightarrow$ more oscillation · Series: $R\uparrow \Rightarrow \alpha\uparrow \Rightarrow$ more damping
🏆
The Study — Three Trophies, Three Answer Shapes
The study has three trophy pedestals — the three possible shapes your final answer can take. Trophy one is a forked road: the overdamped response $K_1 e^{s_1 t} + K_2 e^{s_2 t}$, two arms decaying independently at the two different root rates. Trophy two is a single arrow with a clock attached: the critically damped response $(K_1 + K_2 t)e^{-\alpha t}$, where the clock — the $t$ multiplier — is not a choice but a mathematical requirement imposed by the repeated root. Trophy three is a diminishing spiral: the underdamped response $e^{-\alpha t}(K_1 \cos\omega_d t + K_2 \sin\omega_d t)$, where the exponential is the shrinking envelope and the cosine-sine pair is the oscillation inside it. In every case the same closing law applies: the roots decide which trophy shape you're building, and the two initial conditions inscribe the exact waveform on it. Also remember: once you have $i_L(t)$, one differentiation through $v_C = L\,di_L/dt$ gives you $v_C(t)$ for free. No new constants needed.
Roots → shape · ICs → waveform · $v_C = L\,di_L/dt$ always
