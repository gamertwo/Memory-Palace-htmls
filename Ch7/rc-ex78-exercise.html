Exercise 7-8

This exercise now stands on its own page with the same palace cues as the main lesson, so the problem is not reduced to a single pattern label.

$$v_C(t)=20-20e^{-1000t}\text{ V}$$
Recognize

The response has the classic first-order charging shape: final constant minus a decaying exponential.

Flip

$k=1000$, so $\tau=\frac{1}{1000}=1\text{ ms}$. This is a fast RC response.

Freeze / Melt

At $t=0$, $v_C(0)=0\text{ V}$. At long time, the exponential disappears and the final value is $20\text{ V}$.

Blueprint

With $\tau=10^{-3}$ and final target $20\text{ V}$, the ODE is $$10^{-3}\dot v_C+v_C=20u(t).$$

Room-By-Room Walkthrough

Room I: Recognize the Pattern

The structure is "destination minus memory." That is the standard charging response form for a first-order RC system.

Room II: Flip the Exponent

Match $e^{-1000t}$ with $e^{-t/\tau}$. This forces $\tau=1/1000$ seconds.

Room III: Freeze and Melt

  • Freeze at $t=0$: $20-20(1)=0\text{ V}$.
  • Melt at $t\to\infty$: $20-20(0)=20\text{ V}$.

Room IV: Split the Response

Forced part: $20\text{ V}$

Natural part: $-20e^{-1000t}$

The negative sign simply says the response starts below the final target and rises upward.

Room V: Rebuild the ODE

Use $\tau\dot x + x = x(\infty)u(t)$.

Substitute $\tau=10^{-3}$ and $x(\infty)=20$ to get $$10^{-3}\dot v_C+v_C=20u(t).$$

Memory Cues
Cue 1

If the constant term is positive and the exponential is subtracted, expect a charging curve rising toward that constant.

Cue 2

Freeze tells you where the capacitor starts. Melt tells you where the source wants it to end.

Cue 3

The natural term is the gap: $v(0)-v(\infty)=0-20=-20$.

Active Recall
Why is the natural term negative here?
Because the response begins below the final value, so the decaying correction is a negative gap that shrinks to zero.
What single number tells you the time constant?
The exponent coefficient. Here $1000$ means $\tau=1/1000$ seconds.
How can you rebuild the whole response from memory?
Use final value plus initial-minus-final times $e^{-t/\tau}$.
