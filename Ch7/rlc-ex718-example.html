Example 7-18

The circuit uses the same $$L=1\ \text{H}$$ and $$C=0.25\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}$$ as Example 7-17, but now the task is to find the zero-input capacitor voltage after the switch closes at $$t=0.$$ The initial conditions are $$I_0=0$$ and $$V_0=15\ \text{V}.$$ Reuse the roots from Example 7-17, pick the correct zero-input template for each damping case, and solve for the constants from the initial conditions.

$$v_C(0)=15\ \text{V},\qquad \left.\frac{dv_C}{dt}\right|_{0}=\frac{I_0}{C}=0$$ $$\text{Case A: }K_1e^{s_1 t}+K_2e^{s_2 t},\quad \text{Case B: }(K_1+K_2 t)e^{st},\quad \text{Case C: }e^{-\alpha t}\left(K_1\cos \omega_d t+K_2\sin \omega_d t\right)$$
Quick Data
Capacitance
$$C=0.25\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}$$
Inductance
$$L=1\ \text{H}$$
Initial Voltage
$$v_C(0)=V_0=15\ \text{V}$$
Initial Current
$$I_0=0$$ so $$\dot v_C(0)=I_0/C=0$$
Roots from Ex 7-17
$$-500,-8000;\quad -2000,-2000;\quad -500\pm j500\sqrt{15}$$
Goal
Find the zero-input capacitor voltage for the overdamped, critical, and underdamped cases.
Answer Board
For $$R=8.5\ \text{k}\Omega$$
$$v_C(t)=16e^{-500t}-e^{-8000t}\ \text{V},\quad t\ge 0$$
For $$R=4\ \text{k}\Omega$$
$$v_C(t)=15(1+2000t)e^{-2000t}\ \text{V},\quad t\ge 0$$
For $$R=1\ \text{k}\Omega$$
$$v_C(t)=e^{-500t}\left[15\cos(500\sqrt{15}\,t)+\sqrt{15}\sin(500\sqrt{15}\,t)\right]\ \text{V},\quad t\ge 0$$
Rooms

Room 1: Pick the Right Template from the Roots

  • Example 7-17 already tells you which root pattern belongs to each resistance.
  • Case A uses $$K_1e^{s_1 t}+K_2e^{s_2 t}.$$
  • Case B uses $$(K_1+K_2 t)e^{st}.$$
  • Case C uses $$e^{-\alpha t}\left(K_1\cos \omega_d t+K_2\sin \omega_d t\right).$$

Room 2: Lock the Initial Conditions

  • The capacitor voltage starts at $$v_C(0)=15\ \text{V}.$$
  • Because $$I_0=0,$$ the derivative at the switching instant is $$\left.\dfrac{dv_C}{dt}\right|_0=\dfrac{I_0}{C}=0.$$
  • These two equations are enough to solve the constants in every form.

Room 3: Solve Case A for $$R=8.5\ \text{k}\Omega$$

  • From Example 7-17, the roots are $$s_1=-500$$ and $$s_2=-8000.$$
  • Use $$v_C(t)=K_1e^{-500t}+K_2e^{-8000t}. $$
  • The initial conditions give $$15=K_1+K_2$$ and $$0=-500K_1-8000K_2.$$
  • Solving those equations gives $$K_1=16$$ and $$K_2=-1.$$

Room 4: Write the Case A Response

  • Substitute the constants back into the case-A template.
  • The zero-input response is $$\boxed{v_C(t)=16e^{-500t}-e^{-8000t}\ \text{V}},\quad t\ge 0.$$
  • The slow term $$e^{-500t}$$ dominates the long-time tail, while the fast term $$e^{-8000t}$$ disappears almost immediately.

Room 5: Solve Case B for $$R=4\ \text{k}\Omega$$

  • For case B, both roots are $$-2000.$$
  • Use $$v_C(t)=(K_1+K_2 t)e^{-2000t}. $$
  • At $$t=0,$$ the first condition gives $$15=K_1.$$
  • Differentiate and apply $$\dot v_C(0)=0$$ to get $$0=-2000K_1+K_2,$$ so $$K_2=2000\cdot 15.$$

Room 6: Write the Case B Response

  • Substitute the constants into the repeated-root form.
  • The zero-input response is $$\boxed{v_C(t)=15(1+2000t)e^{-2000t}\ \text{V}},\quad t\ge 0.$$
  • This is the fastest nonoscillatory return to zero because the circuit is critically damped.

Room 7: Solve Case C for $$R=1\ \text{k}\Omega$$

  • From Example 7-17, the roots are $$-500\pm j500\sqrt{15},$$ so $$\alpha=500$$ and $$\omega_d=500\sqrt{15}. $$
  • Use $$v_C(t)=e^{-500t}\left(K_1\cos(500\sqrt{15}\,t)+K_2\sin(500\sqrt{15}\,t)\right).$$
  • The first initial condition gives $$15=K_1.$$
  • Differentiate and apply $$\dot v_C(0)=0$$ to get $$0=-500K_1+500\sqrt{15}K_2,$$ so $$K_2=\sqrt{15}. $$

Room 8: Write the Case C Response and Compare the Family

  • Substitute the constants into the underdamped form.
  • The zero-input response is $$\boxed{v_C(t)=e^{-500t}\left[15\cos(500\sqrt{15}\,t)+\sqrt{15}\sin(500\sqrt{15}\,t)\right]\ \text{V}},\quad t\ge 0.$$
  • Case A decays with two real exponentials, case B decays with a repeated-root factor, and case C decays while oscillating.

Three Templates, Same Initial Conditions

Every part uses the same starting data: $$v_C(0)=15\ \text{V}$$ and $$\dot v_C(0)=0.$$ Only the root pattern changes the algebraic template. Once the correct form is chosen, the constants fall out of the same two equations.

Key Shortcut

Second-Order Zero-Input Rule

For second-order zero-input problems, solve the roots first, choose the matching response form, then use the initial conditions to determine the constants. The roots decide the shape; the initial conditions decide the exact waveform.

  • Three arches hang in one hall: two-real-root, repeated-root, and damped-sinusoid.
  • The hall teaches the master rule: the roots choose the response template before any constants are solved.

  • No matter which damping door opens, these two conditions solve the unknown constants.
  • The lock says: same starting data, different response shape.

Room 3: The Overdamped Stone Door

  • This heavy stone door corresponds to $$R=8.5\text{ k}\Omega$$ with roots $$-500$$ and $$-8000.$$
  • Stone means two separate real decays, so the form is a sum of two exponentials.
  • The palace stamp is that one equation gives the slow term and the other kills the fast mismatch.

  • This balcony helps you remember why the overdamped response looks like one dominant tail plus one quick correction.
  • The showcased waveform is the proof of that picture.

Room 5: The Critical Mirror

  • A mirror split perfectly down the middle stores $$R=4\text{ k}\Omega$$ and the repeated root $$-2000.$$
  • The mirror means balance: the response is not two different exponentials but one repeated-root form with a multiplying $$t$$.
  • The palace lesson is that equal roots force the extra factor.

Room 6: The Fastest Corridor

  • This corridor narrows sharply and returns to zero as fast as possible without ringing.
  • It stores the idea of critical damping better than any formula: no oscillation, no split roots, fastest nonoscillatory return.
  • The worked showcase reminds you how that idea becomes $$15(1+2000t)e^{-2000t}.$$

Room 7: The Silver Sinusoid Chamber

  • A silver chamber rings softly for $$R=1\text{ k}\Omega,$$ where the roots are complex.
  • Silver ringing means underdamped motion: exponential envelope outside, sine-cosine content inside.
  • The two initial conditions still solve the constants, but now the constants sit inside a decaying oscillation.

Room 8: Solomon's Rule Table

  • At the end, a gold table states the whole law of the example.
  • First inspect the roots, then choose the matching template, then use the initial conditions, then write the exact waveform.
  • The memory seal is: roots decide the shape; initial conditions decide the exact answer.
