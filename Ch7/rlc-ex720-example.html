Example 7-20

For a parallel RLC circuit with $$R=500\ \Omega,$$ $$C=1\ \mu\text{F},$$ $$L=0.2\ \text{H},$$ $$I_0=50\ \text{mA},$$ and $$V_0=0\ \text{V},$$ find the zero-input responses of the inductor current, resistor current, and capacitor voltage.

$$s^2+2000s+5\times 10^6=0,\qquad s_{1,2}=-1000\pm j2000$$ $$i_L(t)=50e^{-1000t}\cos 2000t+25e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t\ \text{mA}$$
Quick Data
Resistance
$$R=500\ \Omega$$
Capacitance
$$C=1\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}$$
Inductance
$$L=0.2\ \text{H}$$
Initial Current
$$i_L(0)=I_0=50\ \text{mA}$$
Initial Voltage
$$v_C(0)=V_0=0\ \text{V}$$
Parallel Facts
$$v_L=v_C=v_R,\quad i_R=\dfrac{v}{R},\quad i_C=C\dfrac{dv}{dt}$$
Answer Board
Inductor Current
$$i_L(t)=50e^{-1000t}\cos 2000t+25e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t\ \text{mA}$$
Capacitor Voltage
$$v_C(t)=-25e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t\ \text{V}$$
Resistor Current
$$i_R(t)=-50e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t\ \text{mA}$$
Extra Check
$$i_C(t)=-50e^{-1000t}\cos 2000t+25e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t\ \text{mA}$$
Rooms

Room 1: Write the Parallel Characteristic Equation

  • For a zero-input parallel RLC circuit, use
  • $$LCs^2+\frac{L}{R}s+1=0.$$
  • Substitute the values:
  • $$0.2(10^{-6})s^2+\frac{0.2}{500}s+1=0,$$
  • so
  • $$2\times 10^{-7}s^2+4\times 10^{-4}s+1=0.$$

Room 2: Find the Roots and Pick the Form

  • The roots are
  • $$s_{1,2}=-1000\pm j2000.$$
  • Because the roots are complex conjugates, the current has the underdamped form
  • $$i_L(t)=K_1e^{-1000t}\cos 2000t+K_2e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t.$$

Room 3: Use the Current Initial Condition

  • At $$t=0,$$ the cosine term is 1 and the sine term is 0.
  • So
  • $$i_L(0)=I_0=50=K_1\ \text{mA}.$$
  • Therefore
  • $$K_1=50.$$

Room 4: Convert the Voltage Initial Condition into a Derivative Condition

  • For the inductor,
  • $$v_L(t)=L\frac{di_L(t)}{dt}=v_C(t).$$
  • Since $$v_C(0)=0,$$ we need
  • $$\left.\frac{di_L}{dt}\right|_0=0.$$

Room 5: Differentiate and Solve for $$K_2$$

  • Differentiating gives
  • $$\frac{di_L}{dt}=e^{-1000t}\left[(-1000K_1+2000K_2)\cos 2000t+(-1000K_2-2000K_1)\sin 2000t\right].$$
  • At $$t=0,$$ the sine term vanishes, so
  • $$0=-1000K_1+2000K_2.$$
  • With $$K_1=50,$$
  • $$K_2=25.$$

Room 6: Final Inductor Current

  • Substitute the constants back into the template:
  • $$\boxed{i_L(t)=50e^{-1000t}\cos 2000t+25e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t\ \text{mA}}.$$

Room 7: Use the Current to Get the Voltage

  • Differentiate the final current and multiply by $$L=0.2\ \text{H}.$$
  • The cosine term cancels because of the derivative condition, leaving
  • $$v_L(t)=L\frac{di_L}{dt}=-25e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t\ \text{V}.$$
  • Since all three branches are in parallel,
  • $$\boxed{v_C(t)=v_L(t)=-25e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t\ \text{V}}.$$

Room 8: Get the Resistor Current and Check KCL

  • Use Ohm's law on the resistor branch:
  • $$i_R(t)=\frac{v_C(t)}{R}=\frac{-25e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t}{500}.$$
  • So
  • $$\boxed{i_R(t)=-50e^{-1000t}\sin 2000t\ \text{mA}}.$$
  • If you also compute $$i_C=C\,dv_C/dt,$$ the three branch currents add to zero, as KCL requires.

Fast Memory Rule

Parallel RLC zero-input work here runs in one chain: solve the parallel characteristic equation, build the underdamped inductor-current form, use $$i_L(0)$$ and $$v_C(0)=L\,di_L/dt$$ to find the constants, then get voltage and resistor current from that one current waveform.

Memory Palace Path

Start from the parallel characteristic equation, then walk from current to voltage to the resistor branch. Each station exists because the previous one forces it.

Transition 1 to 2

Once the coefficients are known, the next step is the root pattern.

Transition 2 to 3

Station 3: Current Lock

Use $$i_L(0)=50\ \text{mA}$$ to set $$K_1=50.$$

Transition 3 to 4

The zero initial capacitor voltage becomes a derivative condition on the inductor current.

Station 4: Derivative Bridge

Use $$v_C(0)=v_L(0)=L\,di_L/dt|_0=0$$ to solve for $$K_2.$$

Transition 4 to 5

Once both constants are known, the full current waveform is fixed.

Station 5: Current Chamber

Seal the final $$i_L(t).$$

Transition 5 to 6

The current immediately generates voltage through the inductor law.

Station 6: Voltage Hall

Turn $$L\,di_L/dt$$ into $$v_C(t).$$

Transition 6 to 7

Once the voltage exists, the resistor branch is just Ohm's law.

Station 7: Branch Exit

Store $$i_R=v/R$$ and remember KCL closes the loop.
