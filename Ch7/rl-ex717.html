Pattern Palace Hub Example 7-13

Exercise 7-17

This switching problem asks you to find the output voltage v_O(t) after the source moves from position A to B. Do not start from the final expression; build it from the switching logic, the initial value, and the time constant.

Find $$v_O(t),\quad t\ge 0$$
Rooms

Room 1: Recognize the Pattern

  • This is a switching RL problem, so you should not assume the final formula first.
  • Find the post-switch final value, then use the pre-switch current to get the initial output at t=0^+.
  • Only after that can you decide the response is a pure natural decay.

Room 2: Read the Final Value

  • For $t\to\infty$, the inductor reaches DC steady state and behaves like a short circuit.
  • That shorts the middle node to ground.
  • The output node is then tied to ground through the 50-$\Omega$ branch on the left and the 25-$\Omega$ resistor on the right.
  • So the output settles to $$v_O(\infty)=0\text{ V}.$$

Room 3: Derive the Initial Value From the Circuit

  • Before switching, the switch has been at A for a long time, so the inductor is a short circuit.
  • That makes the pre-switch inductor current $$i_L(0^-)=\frac{10}{50}=0.2\text{ A}.$$
  • Inductor current cannot jump, so $$i_L(0^+)=0.2\text{ A}$$ downward through the inductor.
  • At $t=0^+$, the switch moves to B, so the left source applies $-10\text{ V}$ to the first 50-$\Omega$ resistor.
  • Let the inductor node be $v_1$ and the output node be $v_O$.
  • KCL at the output node gives $$\frac{v_O-v_1}{50}+\frac{v_O}{25}=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad v_1=3v_O.$$
  • KCL at the inductor node gives $$\frac{v_1+10}{50}+\frac{v_1-v_O}{50}+0.2=0.$$
  • Substitute $$v_1=3v_O$$ into that equation:
  • $$\frac{3v_O+10}{50}+\frac{3v_O-v_O}{50}+0.2=0.$$
  • That simplifies to $$\frac{5v_O+10}{50}+0.2=0,$$ so $$5v_O+10=-10,$$ and therefore $$v_O(0^+)=-4\text{ V}.$$
  • Now the negative sign is justified by the actual post-switch circuit, not guessed.

Room 4: Extract the Time Constant

  • Match $e^{-t/\tau}$ with $e^{-200t}$.
  • That gives $$\frac{1}{\tau}=200 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \tau=\frac{1}{200}=5\text{ ms}.$$
  • For an RL circuit, $$\tau=\frac{L}{R_N}.$$

Room 5: Recover the Resistance Seen by the Inductor

  • The inductor value is $$L=150\text{ mH}=0.15\text{ H}.$$
  • Using $$\tau=\frac{L}{R_N},$$ the post-switch resistance is
  • $$R_N=\frac{L}{\tau}=\frac{0.15}{0.005}=30\Omega.$$
  • That matches the kind of equivalent resistance the inductor sees after the switch moves to B.

Room 6: Build the Output Form

  • Now that you know $$v_O(0^+)=-4\text{ V} \quad \text{and} \quad v_O(\infty)=0,$$ the forced term is zero.
  • For a pure natural response, use
  • $$v_O(t)=v_O(0)e^{-t/\tau}.$$
  • Substitute $$v_O(0)=-4 \quad \text{and} \quad \tau=5\text{ ms}:$$
  • $$v_O(t)=-4e^{-200t}\text{ V},\quad t\ge 0.$$

Room 7: Rebuild the Governing ODE

  • A pure first-order natural response satisfies
  • $$\tau\frac{dv_O}{dt}+v_O=0.$$
  • With $$\tau=0.005,$$ the output obeys
  • $$0.005\frac{dv_O}{dt}+v_O=0,\quad t\ge 0.$$

Room 8: Quick Verification

  • Differentiate the response:
  • $$\frac{dv_O}{dt}=800e^{-200t}.$$
  • Substitute into the left side:
  • $$0.005\left(800e^{-200t}\right)-4e^{-200t}=4e^{-200t}-4e^{-200t}=0.$$
  • So the waveform is exactly a valid pure natural first-order response.
Memory Clues

Clue 1

No constant term means no forced term. The whole waveform is natural response only.

Clue 2

For pure natural response, the waveform is just initial value times the decaying exponential.

Clue 3

For RL, use the inductor value and tau to recover the resistance seen after switching: R_N = L / tau.

Clue 4

If the waveform decays to zero, the governing ODE has zero on the right side.

Memory Palace Route

Room 1: The Hermetic Gate

  • Picture a huge black bronze gate with three glowing emblems: a capacitor goblet, an inductor coil, and a two-element crown.
  • The guard at the door asks only one question: What family is this? RC, RL, or RLC.
  • If you see one energy-storage symbol, remember first order. If you see two, remember second order.
  • Above the gate is the first command of the palace: classify the family before you touch the numbers.

Room 2: Bruno's Hall of Thirty Statues

  • Imagine a corridor of statues, each holding a seal. You do not solve randomly; you walk statue to statue in order.
  • The first statue holds $$x(0^-)$$ and $$x(0^+)$$. It reminds you to use continuity: capacitor voltage cannot jump, inductor current cannot jump.
  • The second statue holds $$x(\infty)$$ and shows the DC ending: capacitor open, inductor short.
  • The third statue holds $$\tau$$ or the roots. It tells you to find the system speed before writing the waveform.
  • The fourth statue writes the final response form. The sequence is the seal: initial, final, speed, waveform.

Room 3: The Phoenix Cloister

  • Walk around a monastery cloister where the same route repeats again and again. This is the place of repetition, the Phoenix route.
  • At the first archway, a flame writes the first-order master form: $$x(t)=x(\infty)+\big[x(0^+)-x(\infty)\big]e^{-t/\tau}.$$
  • At the second archway, an RC tablet says $$\tau=R_{eq}C.$$
  • At the third archway, an RL anvil says $$\tau=\frac{L}{R_{eq}}.$$
  • At the fourth archway, a crown of sparks writes the second-order characteristic equation: $$s^2+2\alpha s+\omega_0^2=0.$$
  • The lesson of the cloister is repetition: same walk, same formulas, many different questions.

Room 4: The Mnemonic Alphabet Gallery

  • Now enter a long gallery where each symbol becomes a character, so formulas stop feeling abstract.
  • Let $$\tau$$ be a clock, because it measures how fast the scene fades.
  • Let $$\alpha$$ be a damp robe that smothers motion, because it represents damping strength.
  • Let $$\omega_0$$ be a ringing bell, because it is the natural frequency.
  • Let the roots $$s_1$$ and $$s_2$$ be two messengers carrying the actual modes of the response.
  • When you read a problem, convert symbols into pegs immediately so the equations become memorable objects, not dead letters.

Room 5: The Chamber of Roots and Dampness

  • Picture three fountains in one chamber.
  • The first fountain is filled with thick mud and has two separate stone drops. That is overdamped: two real roots, no oscillation, slow heavy return.
  • The second fountain is a perfectly balanced blade. That is critical damping: repeated root, fastest return without ringing.
  • The third fountain is a silver bowl shaking in circles under a shrinking veil. That is underdamped: complex roots and decaying oscillation.
  • Above the chamber wall is the test: $$\alpha>\omega_0$$ overdamped, $$\alpha=\omega_0$$ critical, $$\alpha<\omega_0$$ underdamped.
  • Memorize the meaning, not just the inequality: roots tell you the shape of the transient's death.

Room 6: The Resistance Treasury

  • In this vault, $$R_t$$ sits on a scale and controls how quickly the whole palace quiets down.
  • The keeper tells you: find the resistance seen by the storage element after switching.
  • If you are finding an equivalent resistance, independent sources are turned off while you look back into the network.
  • In first-order rooms, the treasure converts directly into time constant: $$\tau=R_{eq}C$$ or $$\tau=\frac{L}{R_{eq}}.$$
  • In second-order rooms, the treasure feeds damping through $$\alpha$$ and changes the roots.
  • The memory image is simple: resistance is the palace brake.

Room 7: The Scribe of Waveforms

  • A royal scribe writes four scrolls, one for each common transient shape.
  • Scroll one: pure first-order natural response, just initial value times a decay.
  • Scroll two: complete first-order response, final value plus a fading correction.
  • Scroll three: overdamped second-order response, a sum of two real exponentials.
  • Scroll four: critically damped and underdamped forms, including $$(A+Bt)e^{-\alpha t}$$ and $$e^{-\alpha t}\big(A\cos \omega_d t + B\sin \omega_d t\big).$$
  • The scribe's law is: once you know the family and the roots, the waveform form is no longer a guess.

Room 8: Solomon's Throne Room

  • At the center of the palace sits a throne with the answer engraved on gold.
  • The wise judgment for this page is: one inductor means first-order RL.
  • The output dies to zero, so the response is a pure natural decay around zero.
  • The throne therefore commands the form first: $$v_O(t)=v_O(0^+)e^{-t/\tau}.$$
  • Only after the circuit work gives the actual values do you stamp the seal: $$v_O(t)=-4e^{-200t}\text{ V}.$$
  • The palace ending is the real lesson: family, continuity, final state, time scale, then waveform.
