Pattern Palace Hub Example 7-13

Exercise 7-17

This switching problem asks you to find the output voltage v_O(t) after the source moves from position A to B. Do not start from the final expression; build it from the switching logic, the initial value, and the time constant.

Find $$v_O(t),\quad t\ge 0$$
Rooms

Room 1: Recognize the Pattern

  • This is a switching RL problem, so you should not assume the final formula first.
  • Find the post-switch final value, then use the pre-switch current to get the initial output at t=0^+.
  • Only after that can you decide the response is a pure natural decay.

Room 2: Read the Final Value

  • For $t\to\infty$, the inductor reaches DC steady state and behaves like a short circuit.
  • That shorts the middle node to ground.
  • The output node is then tied to ground through the 50-$\Omega$ branch on the left and the 25-$\Omega$ resistor on the right.
  • So the output settles to $$v_O(\infty)=0\text{ V}.$$

Room 3: Derive the Initial Value From the Circuit

  • Before switching, the switch has been at A for a long time, so the inductor is a short circuit.
  • That makes the pre-switch inductor current $$i_L(0^-)=\frac{10}{50}=0.2\text{ A}.$$
  • Inductor current cannot jump, so $$i_L(0^+)=0.2\text{ A}$$ downward through the inductor.
  • At $t=0^+$, the switch moves to B, so the left source applies $-10\text{ V}$ to the first 50-$\Omega$ resistor.
  • Let the inductor node be $v_1$ and the output node be $v_O$.
  • KCL at the output node gives $$\frac{v_O-v_1}{50}+\frac{v_O}{25}=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad v_1=3v_O.$$
  • KCL at the inductor node gives $$\frac{v_1+10}{50}+\frac{v_1-v_O}{50}+0.2=0.$$
  • Substitute $$v_1=3v_O$$ into that equation:
  • $$\frac{3v_O+10}{50}+\frac{3v_O-v_O}{50}+0.2=0.$$
  • That simplifies to $$\frac{5v_O+10}{50}+0.2=0,$$ so $$5v_O+10=-10,$$ and therefore $$v_O(0^+)=-4\text{ V}.$$
  • Now the negative sign is justified by the actual post-switch circuit, not guessed.

Room 4: Extract the Time Constant

  • Match $e^{-t/\tau}$ with $e^{-200t}$.
  • That gives $$\frac{1}{\tau}=200 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \tau=\frac{1}{200}=5\text{ ms}.$$
  • For an RL circuit, $$\tau=\frac{L}{R_N}.$$

Room 5: Recover the Resistance Seen by the Inductor

  • The inductor value is $$L=150\text{ mH}=0.15\text{ H}.$$
  • Using $$\tau=\frac{L}{R_N},$$ the post-switch resistance is
  • $$R_N=\frac{L}{\tau}=\frac{0.15}{0.005}=30\Omega.$$
  • That matches the kind of equivalent resistance the inductor sees after the switch moves to B.

Room 6: Build the Output Form

  • Now that you know $$v_O(0^+)=-4\text{ V} \quad \text{and} \quad v_O(\infty)=0,$$ the forced term is zero.
  • For a pure natural response, use
  • $$v_O(t)=v_O(0)e^{-t/\tau}.$$
  • Substitute $$v_O(0)=-4 \quad \text{and} \quad \tau=5\text{ ms}:$$
  • $$v_O(t)=-4e^{-200t}\text{ V},\quad t\ge 0.$$

Room 7: Rebuild the Governing ODE

  • A pure first-order natural response satisfies
  • $$\tau\frac{dv_O}{dt}+v_O=0.$$
  • With $$\tau=0.005,$$ the output obeys
  • $$0.005\frac{dv_O}{dt}+v_O=0,\quad t\ge 0.$$

Room 8: Quick Verification

  • Differentiate the response:
  • $$\frac{dv_O}{dt}=800e^{-200t}.$$
  • Substitute into the left side:
  • $$0.005\left(800e^{-200t}\right)-4e^{-200t}=4e^{-200t}-4e^{-200t}=0.$$
  • So the waveform is exactly a valid pure natural first-order response.
Memory Clues

Clue 1

No constant term means no forced term. The whole waveform is natural response only.

Clue 2

For pure natural response, the waveform is just initial value times the decaying exponential.

Clue 3

For RL, use the inductor value and tau to recover the resistance seen after switching: R_N = L / tau.

Clue 4

If the waveform decays to zero, the governing ODE has zero on the right side.
