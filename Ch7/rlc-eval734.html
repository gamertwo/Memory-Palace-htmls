Evaluation Exercise 7-34

Part (a) asks for the source-resistance range that produces an underdamped natural response in a series RLC circuit with $$L=200\ \text{mH}$$ and $$C=0.032\ \mu\text{F}.$$ Part (b) compares that result with the parallel-circuit answer from Example 7-26.

$$s^2+\frac{R}{L}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0$$ $$R<2\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}=5\ \text{k}\Omega$$
Quick Data

$$s^2+\frac{R}{L}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0$$
Inductance
$$L=200\ \text{mH}$$
Capacitance
$$C=0.032\ \mu\text{F}$$

$$R_c=5\ \text{k}\Omega$$

$$R<5\ \text{k}\Omega$$

$$R_N>1250\ \Omega$$
Answer Board
Part (a)
$$\boxed{R<5\ \text{k}\Omega}$$
Part (b)
Series and parallel circuits are dual: less damping comes from smaller $$R$$ in series but larger $$R$$ in parallel.
Rooms

Room 1: Write the Series Equation

  • For a series RLC circuit, use $$s^2+\frac{R}{L}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0.$$
  • Why: Because the series characteristic equation is the baseline needed to extract the threshold and compare behaviors correctly.

Room 2: Match the Standard Form

  • Compare with $$s^2+2\zeta\omega_0s+\omega_0^2=0.$$
  • That gives $$2\zeta\omega_0=R/L.$$
  • Why: Because matching to the standard second-order form converts raw coefficients into meaningful parameters like damping and natural frequency.

Room 3: Use the Critical Boundary

  • Underdamped means $$\zeta<1.$$
  • At the boundary, $$R/L=2\omega_0.$$
  • Since $$\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC},$$ the critical resistance is $$R_c=2\sqrt{L/C}.$$
  • Why: Because the critical boundary is the dividing line between underdamped and overdamped behavior, so the inequality must be built around it.

Room 4: Evaluate the Threshold

  • With $$L=0.2\ \text{H}$$ and $$C=32\times10^{-9}\ \text{F},$$
  • $$R_c=2\sqrt{0.2/(32\times10^{-9})}=5000\ \Omega.$$
  • So the underdamped range is $$\boxed{R<5\ \text{k}\Omega}.$$
  • Why: Because the symbolic boundary becomes meaningful only after it is turned into a numeric cutoff.

Room 5: Compare with the Parallel Case

  • In a series circuit, smaller resistance means less damping.
  • In a parallel circuit, larger resistance means less damping.
  • The two results point in opposite directions because series and parallel RLC circuits are dual.
  • Why: Because the comparison is what locks the duality into memory and prevents mixing up the inequality directions later.

Fast Memory Rule

Series likes small resistance for underdamping. Parallel likes large resistance for underdamping.
