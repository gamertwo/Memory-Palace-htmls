Example 7-15

This example is the first place where the RC response is not just one exponential going to a constant. The source itself is exponential, so the forced response must also be exponential, while the circuit still keeps its own natural exponential. The big idea is that the final answer contains two exponentials for two different reasons: one comes from the circuit, and one comes from the source.

$$v(t)=\left[V_0-\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}\right]e^{-t/RC}+\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}e^{-\alpha t},\qquad t\ge 0$$
Quick Result
Natural part
$$Ke^{-t/RC}$$ is still present because the capacitor keeps its own first-order memory.
Forced part
Because the source is $$V_Ae^{-\alpha t},$$ the forced response is guessed with the same exponential shape.
Key coefficient
$$\dfrac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}$$ shows how the source decay interacts with the RC decay.
Rooms

Room 1: Start with the Natural Response

  • Before worrying about the source, remember what the capacitor would do by itself.
  • As before, the natural response of an RC circuit is
  • $$v_N(t)=Ke^{-t/RC},\qquad t\ge 0.$$
  • This part is the circuit's own first-order mode, not something created by the source.

Room 2: Write the Forced-Response Differential Equation

  • For the exponential input, the forced part satisfies
  • $$RC\frac{dv_F(t)}{dt}+v_F(t)=V_Ae^{-\alpha t},\qquad t\ge 0.$$

Room 3: Guess the Forced Response Shape

  • The forcing term is exponential, so the smartest trial form is another exponential with the same decay rate.
  • This is the same logic used when sinusoidal inputs lead to sinusoidal forced-response guesses.
  • $$v_F(t)=K_F e^{-\alpha t}.$$

Room 4: Substitute into the Equation

  • Differentiate:
  • $$\frac{d}{dt}\left(K_F e^{-\alpha t}\right)=-\alpha K_F e^{-\alpha t}.$$
  • Substitute:
  • $$RC\left(-\alpha K_F e^{-\alpha t}\right)+K_F e^{-\alpha t}=V_A e^{-\alpha t}.$$

Room 5: Solve for $K_F$

  • Cancel the common exponential:
  • $$-\alpha RC K_F+K_F=V_A.$$
  • So
  • $$K_F(1-\alpha RC)=V_A$$
  • and therefore
  • $$K_F=\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}.$$
  • This step assumes $$1-\alpha RC\neq 0.$$ If $$\alpha RC=1,$$ the source decay exactly matches the circuit's own decay rate and the trial form has to be changed.

Room 6: Build the Total Response

  • The total response is natural plus forced:
  • $$v(t)=v_N(t)+v_F(t)=Ke^{-t/RC}+\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}e^{-\alpha t},\qquad t\ge 0.$$

Room 7: Apply the Initial Condition

  • Given $$v(0)=V_0,$$ substitute $$t=0:$$
  • $$V_0=K+\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}.$$
  • So
  • $$K=V_0-\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}.$$

Room 8: Final Answer

  • Substitute $K$ back into the total response:
  • $$\boxed{v(t)=\left[V_0-\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}\right]e^{-t/RC}+\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}e^{-\alpha t}},\qquad t\ge 0.$$
  • The waveform is the sum of two decaying exponentials, so the graph is shaped by a competition between source decay and circuit decay.
  • The one with the larger time constant decays more slowly and becomes the dominant exponential later.

Concept Summary

This page upgrades first-order RC thinking from natural plus constant to natural plus matched forcing shape. Once the source is exponential, the response naturally carries two exponentials and you have to keep straight which one belongs to the circuit and which one belongs to the source.

Key Reading Rule

Natural + Forced

The fastest way to remember this page is: keep the natural RC exponential, then guess a forced term with the same shape as the input. Solve its coefficient, then use the initial condition only at the very end to fix the natural constant.

Memory Palace Path

This walk stays causal: natural response first, match the input shape next, solve the forced coefficient, then return to the initial condition only after the full structure is built.

Station 1: Natural Gate

Start with the standard RC decay: $$Ke^{-t/RC}.$$

Transition 1 to 2

Once the natural part is parked, the input demands a forced-response equation.

Station 2: Forcing Hall

Write $$RC\dot v_F+v_F=V_Ae^{-\alpha t}.$$

Transition 2 to 3

The input is exponential, so the smartest guess is to mirror its shape.

Station 3: Guess Chamber

Choose $$v_F(t)=K_F e^{-\alpha t}.$$

Transition 3 to 4

Now the guess must be tested by substitution.

Station 4: Cancellation Bridge

Differentiate, substitute, and cancel the common exponential.

Transition 4 to 5

After cancellation, only the coefficient remains unknown.

Station 5: Coefficient Vault

Store $$K_F=\dfrac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}.$$

Transition 5 to 6

With both pieces known in form, the total response can be assembled before the constant is fixed.

Station 6: Combine Arch

Add natural and forced responses into one full expression.

Transition 6 to 7

The only missing piece is the natural constant, and the initial condition unlocks it.

Station 7: Initial-Value Lock

Use $$v(0)=V_0$$ to solve for $$K.$$

Transition 7 to 8

Once $$K$$ is known, the final response is just substitution and one last interpretation.

Station 8: Dominant Tail

Seal the final answer and remember that the slowest exponential dominates later.
