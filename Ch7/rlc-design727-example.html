Design Example 7-27

Design a parallel RLC circuit with $$\zeta=0.5$$ and $$\omega_0=25\ \text{krad/s}.$$ Turn those targets into coefficient constraints, choose one convenient component, and solve for the other two.

$$s^2+\frac{1}{RC}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0$$ $$\frac{1}{RC}=2\zeta\omega_0=25\times10^3,\qquad \frac{1}{LC}=\omega_0^2=6.25\times10^8$$

$$\omega_0=25\times10^3\ \text{rad/s}$$

$$R=10\ \text{k}\Omega$$

$$C=4000\ \text{pF}$$
Solved Inductor
$$L=0.4\ \text{H}$$
Design Freedom
Three parameters, two constraints
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Start from the Parallel Form

  • Use $$s^2+\frac{1}{RC}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0.$$
  • Compare with $$s^2+2\zeta\omega_0s+\omega_0^2=0.$$
  • Why: Because the parallel standard form is the design template, and every target must be translated into its coefficients before parts can be chosen.

Room 2: Convert the Design Targets

  • From $$\zeta=0.5$$ and $$\omega_0=25\times10^3,$$
  • $$\frac{1}{RC}=2\zeta\omega_0=25\times10^3.$$
  • Also $$\frac{1}{LC}=\omega_0^2=(25\times10^3)^2=6.25\times10^8.$$
  • Why: Because design targets such as zeta and omega0 are useful only after they are rewritten as coefficient constraints.

Room 3: Choose One Component

  • There are three parameters and two equations.
  • Choose $$R=10\ \text{k}\Omega.$$
  • Why: Because a free component choice closes the underdetermined design and lets the remaining values fall out directly.

Room 4: Solve for C

  • $$C=1/((10\times10^3)(25\times10^3))=4\times10^{-9}\ \text{F}=4000\ \text{pF}.$$
  • Why: Because this coefficient relation isolates capacitance directly, so solving it first reduces the rest of the design load.

Room 5: Solve for L

  • $$L=1/(C\omega_0^2)=1/((4\times10^{-9})(6.25\times10^8))=0.4\ \text{H}.$$
  • Why: Because inductance is tied to the constant-term constraint, so solving it here converts the target dynamics into a physical element.

Room 6: Final Design Logic

  • The chosen set meets both the damping-ratio and natural-frequency targets.
  • Many other designs are possible because one component remains free.
  • Why: Because the finished design should be reduced to a portable method, not left as isolated arithmetic.

Fast Memory Rule

Convert $$\zeta$$ and $$\omega_0$$ into the coefficient of $$s$$ and the constant term, then pick one component and let the equations solve the rest.

Why The Order Matters

This page works best when you read it as a chain instead of as isolated formulas. The early rooms set up the model, the middle rooms use that model to solve constants or component constraints, and the last room writes the requested answer. If a room feels sudden, go back one room and ask which number or rule it is using.

What To Watch

Do not choose component values first. In design pages, the targets or poles must be converted into coefficient equations before any component is picked.
