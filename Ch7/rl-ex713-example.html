Example 7-13

This problem gives the inductor current directly and asks you to read the forced component, natural component, time constant, and the Thevenin equivalent seen by the inductor.

$$i_L(t)=50+100e^{-5000t}\text{ mA},\quad t\ge 0$$
Quick Data
Clue 1
In â€œgiven responseâ€ problems, the constant term is the forced part and the exponential term is the natural part.
Clue 2
The exponent coefficient gives the time constant immediately by inversion.
Clue 3
For RL, once you know L and tau, you can recover the Thevenin resistance from R_T = L / tau.
Clue 4
At DC steady state, the inductor is a short, so the final current lets you recover the Thevenin voltage.
Rooms

Room 1: Recognize the Form

  • This is already in the first-order RL response form: constant plus a decaying exponential.
  • So you can read the pieces directly without solving a differential equation from scratch.

Room 2: Identify the Natural Component

  • The natural component is the decaying exponential term.
  • So $$i_N(t)=100e^{-5000t}\text{ mA}.$$

Room 3: Identify the Forced Component

  • The forced component is what remains after the exponential dies out.
  • As $t\to\infty$, $$e^{-5000t}\to 0,$$ so
  • $$i_F=i_L(\infty)=50\text{ mA}.$$

Room 4: Find the Time Constant

  • Match $e^{-t/\tau}$ with $e^{-5000t}$.
  • That gives $$\frac{1}{\tau}=5000 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \tau=\frac{1}{5000}=0.2\text{ ms}.$$

Room 5: Use the Inductor Value

  • The figure gives $$L=200\text{ mH}=0.2\text{ H}.$$
  • For an RL circuit, $$\tau=\frac{L}{R_T}.$$
  • So the Thevenin resistance is
  • $$R_T=\frac{L}{\tau}=\frac{0.2}{0.0002}=1000\Omega=1k\Omega.$$

Room 6: Find the Thevenin Source Voltage

  • At DC steady state, the inductor acts like a short circuit.
  • So the final current obeys $$i_L(\infty)=\frac{v_T}{R_T}.$$
  • Using $$i_L(\infty)=50\text{ mA} \quad \text{and} \quad R_T=1k\Omega,$$
  • $$v_T=R_T\,i_L(\infty)=(1k\Omega)(50\text{ mA})=50\text{ V}.$$

Room 7: Rebuild the Governing ODE

  • The standard first-order RL equation is
  • $$\tau\frac{di_L}{dt}+i_L=i_L(\infty)u(t).$$
  • Using $$\tau=0.0002 \quad \text{and} \quad i_L(\infty)=50,$$
  • the response fits $$0.0002\frac{di_L}{dt}+i_L=50u(t),$$
  • with current measured in mA.

Room 8: Quick Verification

  • Differentiate the response:
  • $$\frac{di_L}{dt}=-500000e^{-5000t}\text{ mA/s}.$$
  • Substitute into the left side:
  • $$0.0002(-500000e^{-5000t})+\left(50+100e^{-5000t}\right)=-100e^{-5000t}+50+100e^{-5000t}=50.$$
  • So the waveform is consistent with the first-order RL model.
