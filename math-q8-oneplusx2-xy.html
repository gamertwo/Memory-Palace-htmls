Q8 · $(1+x^2)dy-xy\,dx=0$

Fully detailed derivation with no skipped algebra.

$$(1+x^2)\,dy-xy\,dx=0$$
Step 1: Move one term to other side: $(1+x^2)dy=xy\,dx$.
Step 2: Divide by $dx$ to write derivative form:
$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x}{1+x^2}y$$
Step 3: Separate variables:
$$\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{x}{1+x^2}dx$$
Step 4: Integrate both sides:
$$\int\frac{1}{y}dy=\int\frac{x}{1+x^2}dx$$
Step 5: Evaluate integrals:
$$\ln|y|=\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+x^2)+C$$
Step 6: Exponentiate and absorb constants:
$$|y|=e^C(1+x^2)^{1/2}\Rightarrow y=C\sqrt{1+x^2}$$
$$\boxed{y=C\sqrt{1+x^2}}$$
Quick verification:
$$y=C\sqrt{1+x^2}\Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{Cx}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}=\frac{x}{1+x^2}\,C\sqrt{1+x^2}=\frac{x}{1+x^2}y$$
Verified against derivative form.
Memory cues:
Room 1 · Balance Board
First isolate $dy$ and $dx$ terms cleanly before dividing.
Room 2 · Half-Log Chamber
$\int x/(1+x^2)dx$ always gives half-log form.
Room 3 · Root Exit
Exponentiating half-log creates a square-root final.
