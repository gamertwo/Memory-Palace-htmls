Example 7-19

A series RLC circuit has the zero-input capacitor voltage $$v_C(t)=10e^{-1000t}\sin(2000t)\ \text{V},\quad t\ge 0,$$ across a $$1\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}$$ capacitor. From that one waveform, recover the circuit characteristic equation, find $$R$$ and $$L,$$ compute the inductor current, and read the initial state values.

$$v_C(t)=10e^{-1000t}\sin(2000t)\ \text{V}$$ $$s_{1,2}=-1000\pm j2000,\qquad s^2+\frac{R}{L}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0$$

$$C=1\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}$$

Rooms

Room 1: Read the Root Pattern from the Waveform

  • The voltage is a damped sine, so the circuit must be underdamped.
  • From $$10e^{-1000t}\sin(2000t),$$ read $$\alpha=1000$$ and $$\omega_d=2000.$$
  • That means the roots are $$s_{1,2}=-1000\pm j2000.$$

Room 2: Rebuild the Characteristic Equation

  • Form the quadratic from the roots: $$\left(s+1000-j2000\right)\left(s+1000+j2000\right)=0.$$
  • Multiply the conjugate pair.
  • The characteristic equation becomes $$s^2+2000s+5\times 10^6=0.$$

Room 3: Compare with the Series RLC Standard Form

  • A series RLC circuit has characteristic equation $$s^2+\frac{R}{L}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0.$$
  • Matching coefficients with $$s^2+2000s+5\times 10^6=0$$ gives
  • $$\frac{R}{L}=2000,\qquad \frac{1}{LC}=5\times 10^6.$$

Room 4: Solve for the Inductance

  • Given $$C=1\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F},$$ use $$\dfrac{1}{LC}=5\times 10^6.$$
  • Then $$LC=2\times 10^{-7},$$ so $$L=\dfrac{2\times 10^{-7}}{10^{-6}}=0.2\ \text{H}. $$

Room 5: Solve for the Resistance

  • Use $$\dfrac{R}{L}=2000$$ with $$L=0.2\ \text{H}. $$
  • Then $$R=2000(0.2)=400\ \Omega.$$
  • Now both unknown circuit elements are identified.

Room 6: Find the Inductor Current

  • In a series circuit, $$i_L(t)=i_C(t).$$
  • Use $$i_C(t)=C\,\dfrac{dv_C}{dt}. $$
  • Differentiate the given voltage: $$\dfrac{dv_C}{dt}=10e^{-1000t}\left(2000\cos(2000t)-1000\sin(2000t)\right).$$
  • Multiply by $$C=10^{-6}\ \text{F}$$ to get $$\boxed{i_L(t)=-10e^{-1000t}\sin(2000t)+20e^{-1000t}\cos(2000t)\ \text{mA}}.$$

Room 7: Read the Initial State Values

  • At $$t=0,$$ the sine term vanishes, so $$v_C(0)=0.$$
  • For the current, substitute $$t=0$$ into the derived expression.
  • That gives $$i_L(0)=-10(1)(0)+20(1)(1)=20\ \text{mA}. $$

Room 8: Quick Consistency Check

  • The current formula gives $$i_L(0)=20\ \text{mA},$$ which matches the direct inspection.
  • The voltage formula gives $$v_C(0)=0,$$ because $$\sin 0=0.$$
  • So the reconstructed equation, the recovered elements, and the state variables all fit one coherent underdamped series RLC model.

Read the Roots Directly

When a second-order response is already written as a damped sine or cosine, do not start from circuit algebra. Read the real decay rate and oscillation frequency from the waveform, rebuild the roots, and compare the resulting quadratic with the standard characteristic equation.

Key Shortcut

Waveform-to-Circuit Rule

A damped sinusoid tells you the root pair immediately. Once the roots are known, expand the characteristic equation and match coefficients with the circuit form. That reverse-engineers the element values without redrawing the circuit.
