Exercise 3.6 Question 5

Multiply through by $$\cos y$$ to reveal the derivative of $$\sin y.$$ That turns the equation into a clean linear ODE in a new variable.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}+\tan x\tan y=\cos x\sec y\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\sin y\sec x=x+C}$$
Quick Data
Useful Move
Multiply by $$\cos y$$
Substitution
$$u=\sin y$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=\sec x$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Clear the Secant

  • Multiply the whole equation by $$\cos y$$:
  • $$\cos y\frac{dy}{dx}+\tan x\sin y=\cos x.$$

Room 2: New Variable

  • Let $$u=\sin y.$$ Then $$\dfrac{du}{dx}=\cos y\dfrac{dy}{dx}.$$
  • The same substitution also gives $$\sin y=u.$$

Room 3: Linear Equation

  • In $$\cos y\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\tan x\sin y=\cos x,$$ replace $$\cos y\dfrac{dy}{dx}$$ by $$\dfrac{du}{dx}.$$
  • Replace $$\sin y$$ by $$u.$$
  • So the equation becomes $$\dfrac{du}{dx}+\tan x\,u=\cos x.$$

Room 4: Product Rule

  • Here $$P(x)=\tan x,$$ so $$\mu(x)=e^{\int \tan x\,dx}=\sec x.$$
  • Multiply through by $$\sec x$$:
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}(u\sec x)=1.$$
  • Integrating gives $$u\sec x=x+C.$$

Room 5: Final Answer

  • Put back $$u=\sin y:$$
  • $$\boxed{\sin y\sec x=x+C}.$$

Key Shortcut

When you see $$\sec y$$ and $$\tan y$$ together, multiplying by $$\cos y$$ often exposes a clean derivative in $$y.$$
