Exercise 3.13 Question 4

This symmetry form becomes quadratic in $$p$$ once we write $$dx/dy=1/p.$$ Then it splits into two simple branches.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{dx}{dy}=\frac{x}{y}-\frac{y}{x}$$

$$xy=c,\qquad y^2-x^2=c$$

Quick Data
Put
$$p=\frac{dy}{dx},\qquad \frac{dx}{dy}=\frac1p$$
Factorization
$$\left(p-\frac{x}{y}\right)\left(p+\frac{y}{x}\right)=0$$
Families
$$xy=c,\qquad y^2-x^2=c$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Rewrite in $$p$$

  • Let $$p=\dfrac{dy}{dx},$$ so $$\dfrac{dx}{dy}=\dfrac1p.$$
  • The equation becomes $$p+\frac1p=\frac{x}{y}-\frac{y}{x}.$$

Room 2: Factor the Branches

  • Multiply by $$p$$ and factor:
  • $$p^2-\left(\frac{x}{y}-\frac{y}{x}\right)p+1=0.$$
  • So $$\left(p-\frac{x}{y}\right)\left(p+\frac{y}{x}\right)=0.$$

Room 3: Solve the Two Families

  • If $$p=\dfrac{x}{y},$$ then $$y\dfrac{dy}{dx}=x,$$ giving $$y^2-x^2=c.$$
  • If $$p=-\dfrac{y}{x},$$ then $$x\dfrac{dy}{dx}+y=0,$$ giving $$xy=c.$$
Solution Flow

Use $$dx/dy=1/p,$$ turn the equation into a factorable quadratic in $$p,$$ and integrate each branch.

Set $$p=dy/dx$$→Use $$dx/dy=1/p$$→Factor→Solve branch 1→Solve branch 2
