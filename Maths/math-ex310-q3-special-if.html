Exercise 3.10 Question 3

This is the deeper power-chain version. The same guess $$\mu=x^h y^k$$ still works, but now exactness pushes the exponents to $$-3.$$

$$y(1+xy)\,dx+x(1+xy+x^2y^2)\,dy=0$$

$$\frac1{2x^2y^2}+\frac1{xy}-\log y=C$$
Ex 3.10 GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
M(x,y)
$$M=y+xy^2$$
N(x,y)
$$N=x+x^2y+x^3y^2$$
Formula Used
Assume $$\mu=x^h y^k$$ and solve exactness by matching powers after multiplication.
Integrating Factor
$$\mu=x^{-3}y^{-3}=\frac1{x^3y^3}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Recognize the Power Ladder

  • The left side rises in powers of $$xy,$$ so try the same special integrating factor $$x^h y^k.$$
  • This question extends the pattern from Question 2 by one more power level.

Room 2: Solve for $h$ and $k$

  • Write $$\mu=x^h y^k$$ and impose exactness.
  • Matching the power terms gives $$h=k=-3.$$
  • So $$\mu=\dfrac1{x^3y^3}.$$

Room 3: Multiply Through

  • The exact equation becomes
  • $$\left(\frac1{x^3y^2}+\frac1{x^2y}\right)dx+\left(\frac1{x^2y^3}+\frac1{xy^2}+\frac1y\right)dy=0.$$

Room 4: Solve the Exact Equation

  • Integrate the new $$M$$ with respect to $$x$$:
  • $$F=-\frac1{2x^2y^2}-\frac1{xy}+g(y).$$
  • Then $$F_y=\dfrac1{x^2y^3}+\dfrac1{xy^2}+g'(y).$$ Matching with $$N$$ gives $$g'(y)=\dfrac1y,$$ so $$g(y)=\log y.$$

Room 5: Final Answer

  • Therefore $$F=-\frac1{2x^2y^2}-\frac1{xy}+\log y=C.$$
  • Equivalently, $$\frac1{2x^2y^2}+\frac1{xy}-\log y=C.$$
