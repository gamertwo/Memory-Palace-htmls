Exercise 3.6 Question 10

This page is another square-swap problem. Once $$y^2$$ is used as the helper variable, the line becomes linear and the final answer ends in the neat log form shown in the book.

$$(x-y^2)\,dx+2xy\,dy=0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\frac{y^2}{x}+\log x=C}$$
Major Guide
Quick Data
Helper Variable
$$z=y^2$$
Linear Form
$$z'-\frac{1}{x}z=-1$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=\frac{1}{x}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Rewrite the Differential Form

  • From $$(x-y^2)\,dx+2xy\,dy=0,$$ divide by $$dx$$ to get $$x-y^2+2xy\frac{dy}{dx}=0.$$
  • So $$2y\frac{dy}{dx}-\frac{y^2}{x}=-1.$$

Room 2: Square Substitution

  • Let $$z=y^2.$$ Then $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}=2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}.$$
  • This swap appears directly because the derivative piece is already $$2yy'.$$

Room 3: Linear Equation

  • Replace $$2yy'$$ by $$z'$$ and $$y^2$$ by $$z.$$
  • So $$2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}-\frac{y^2}{x}=-1$$ becomes $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}-\frac{1}{x}z=-1.$$

Room 4: Integrating Factor

  • Here $$P(x)=-1/x,$$ so $$\mu(x)=e^{\int -1/x\,dx}=1/x.$$
  • Multiply through: $$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{z}{x}\right)=-\frac{1}{x}.$$
  • Integrating gives $$\dfrac{z}{x}=-\log x+C.$$

Room 5: Back-Substitute

  • Replace $$z=y^2$$ to get $$\dfrac{y^2}{x}=-\log x+C.$$
  • Rearrange to the book form: $$\boxed{\dfrac{y^2}{x}+\log x=C}.$$

Key Shortcut

If a differential form gives you $$2yy',$$ the square helper is already hiding in plain sight. Catch it before trying anything longer.
