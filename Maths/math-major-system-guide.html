Bernoulli Major Guide

This page is the fixed rulebook for the peg system used on Exercise 3.6 Q1 to Q10. The goal is not to invent new images for every question. The goal is to reuse the same sound-based Major pegs every time, then hang the formulas on them. Use the Major System for the order of the steps, then use a small symbol-image bank for the recurring formula pieces.

Use This Idea

We are not converting entire formulas into long digit strings. We are using a small fixed peg set to label repeated formula families, then a small fixed image alphabet to make recurring symbols visual.

What To Do

Do

  • Keep the same peg words on every question.
  • Attach the whole formula family to one peg, not every symbol to a new peg.
  • Say the peg words before the formulas when you review: "net, knife, fire, mat, case, coat."
  • Memorize the bridge step explicitly. The swap is not enough by itself.
  • Give repeated symbols like $$1/y,$$ the prime, $$\int,$$ $$e,$$ and $$+C$$ stable images.

Do Not

  • Do not create a brand-new peg word on every page.
  • Do not switch peg words because another image feels cooler that day.
  • Do not memorize only the final answer and skip the product-rule step.
  • Do not forget the sign when the reciprocal swap creates a minus sign.
  • Do not back-substitute too early if the linear ODE is not finished yet.

28

Integral Peg

Use this every time you see an integral sign. The knife cuts out the antiderivative, especially inside the integrating-factor rule and the final integration step.

84

Fire

Exponential Peg

Use this for the universal integrating-factor formula $$\mu(x)=e^{\int P(x)\,dx}.$$ Even when the answer simplifies to $$\sec x$$ or $$x^{-3},$$ the fire reminds you that the rule started from an exponential.

31

Mat

Product-Rule Peg

Use this for the collapse into one derivative: $$\dfrac{d}{dx}(\mu \cdot \text{helper})=\mu Q.$$ The whole line lands on the mat after multiplying by $$\mu.$$

70

Case

Constant Peg

Use this for the arbitrary constant $$+C.$$ The case always drops after integration. If an initial condition exists, the case is later opened and fixed.

71

Back-Sub Peg

Use this for putting the original variable back on. After solving for the helper variable, the coat restores $$y$$ or the original form.

Formula Symbol Bank

Use the Major pegs for the order of the solution, then use a fixed image alphabet for the recurring formula pieces. This follows the equation-memory idea from Art of Memory math discussions and the Magnetic Memory Method advice to prepare symbol systems instead of leaving notation abstract.

Formula Piece Fixed Image Memory Job
$$\dfrac{1}{y}$$ One candle above a yoyo The single object above the yoyo gives you the reciprocal of $$y.$$ Use it anywhere the Bernoulli swap becomes reciprocal.
$$\dfrac{1}{x}$$ One candle above an x-mark Use this on pages like q7 where the reciprocal helper is built from $$x$$ instead of $$y.$$ Keep the same reciprocal idea, just move it onto the x-mark.
$$y^n$$ Yoyo climbing a ladder The ladder turns plain $$y$$ into a power. More steps on the ladder means a higher power or heavier exponent move.
$$y^2$$ Two yoyos fused into one square block Use this for the square helper on q8 and q10. The fusion image tells you the page wants $$z=y^2,$$ not a reciprocal helper.
$$y'$$ or $$z'$$ A dart stabbed into the symbol The dart marks the derivative. A dart in the yoyo means $$y',$$ while a dart in the helper means $$z'$$ or $$u'.$$
$$\int$$ Knife The knife cuts the antiderivative. This stays locked to the Major peg $$28=\text{knife}.$$
$$e^{(\cdot)}$$ Fire or furnace The exponential is always lit by fire. This stays locked to $$84=\text{fire}.$$
$$\mu$$ Mug The mug holds the integrating factor. Fire lights the mug because $$\mu=e^{\int Pdx}.$$
$$\dfrac{d}{dx}(\cdot)$$ Drill moving along an x-track Use the drill for the derivative operator when the whole product becomes one derivative on the mat.
$$\sin y$$ Singer holding a yoyo Useful in q5. The singer tells you sine and the yoyo keeps the variable visible.
$$\cos y$$ Coat wrapped around a yoyo Useful in q5. The coat reminds you of cosine and also pairs well with the final coat peg for restoration.
$$\tan x$$ Tan fan on an x-mark The fan gives tangent and keeps the trig coefficient visually separate from the helper variable.
$$\sec x$$ Security guard on an x-mark The guard gives secant. This is especially useful in q3, q4, and q5.
$$+C$$ Case or briefcase The case drops after integration and may later be opened to fix the constant from an initial condition.
Core Formula Workflow
$$\text{Swap} \rightarrow \text{Bridge Step} \rightarrow \mu(x)=e^{\int P(x)\,dx} \rightarrow \frac{d}{dx}(\mu\cdot\text{helper})=\mu Q \rightarrow \mu\cdot\text{helper}=\int \mu Q\,dx + C \rightarrow \text{Back-substitute}$$
Step Formula Peg Mnemonic Job
1. Swap $$z=\frac{1}{y},\quad z=y^{1-n},\quad u=\sin y$$ `21 net` The net catches the new helper variable.
2. Bridge Replace the old nonlinear pieces with the helper and helper-derivative. `21 net` again The net is thrown across the whole equation, not just the definition line.
3. Integrating factor $$\mu(x)=e^{\int P(x)\,dx}$$ `28 knife` + `84 fire` The knife cuts the integral. The fire lights the exponential rule.
4. Product rule $$\frac{d}{dx}(\mu\cdot\text{helper})=\mu Q$$ `31 mat` The multiplied equation lands on one derivative.
5. Constant $$\mu\cdot\text{helper}=\int \mu Q\,dx + C$$ `70 case` The case drops after integration.
6. Restore original variable Replace $$z$$ or $$u$$ with the original expression in $$y.$$ `71 coat` The coat puts the original variable back on.
How To Memorize A Formula
$$\text{Order Peg} + \text{Symbol Images} + \text{One Moving Scene}$$
Move What To Do Example
1. Chunk it Break the formula into swap, bridge, integrating factor, product rule, constant, and finish. For q3: $$z=1/y \rightarrow z'+\tan xz=\sec x \rightarrow \mu=\sec x \rightarrow z\sec x=\tan x+C.$$
2. Add symbol images Give the repeated symbols stable images so the formula stops looking abstract. A yoyo for $$y,$$ a dart for the prime, a tan fan, a security guard, and the case for $$+C.$$
3. Make one action scene Do not leave the symbols static. Make them cut, burn, wrap, fall, or swap. The knife cuts the integral, fire lights the mug, the mat catches the product derivative, and the case drops.
4. Add extra senses Add sound, heat, motion, pressure, or surprise so the movie sticks. The knife scrapes, the fire roars, the case thuds to the floor, and the coat wraps itself back on.
5. Rewrite cold After replaying the movie, write the formula once without looking. Say the peg chain first, replay the image chain second, then write the line.

Q1 $$z=\frac{1}{y},\quad z'=-\frac{1}{y^2}y'$$ $$\mu=e^{\int 1\,dx}=e^x$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}(ze^x)=-2x$$ $$ze^x=-x^2+C \rightarrow e^x+x^2y+Cy=0$$ Net catches the reciprocal yoyo, fire lights $$e^x,$$ the mat catches $$ze^x,$$ then the case drops and the coat restores $$y.$$
Q2 $$z=y^{-3},\quad z'=-3y^{-4}y'$$ $$\mu=e^{\int -3/x\,dx}=x^{-3}$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}(zx^{-3})=-2x$$ $$zx^{-3}=-x^2+C \rightarrow \frac{1}{y^3}=Cx^3-x^5$$ Net catches a yoyo on a three-step ladder, knife cuts $$-3/x,$$ fire settles to $$x^{-3},$$ then the case and coat finish the power form.
Q3 $$z=\frac{1}{y},\quad z'=-\frac{1}{y^2}y'$$ $$\mu=e^{\int \tan x\,dx}=\sec x$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}(z\sec x)=\sec^2x$$ $$z\sec x=\tan x+C \rightarrow \sec x=(\tan x+C)y$$ Net catches the reciprocal yoyo, the tan fan meets the security guard, then the case drops and the coat puts $$y$$ back in.
Q4 $$z=\frac{1}{y},\quad z'=-\frac{1}{y^2}y'$$ $$\mu=e^{\int 2\tan x\,dx}=\sec^2x$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}(z\sec^2x)=-\tan^2x\sec^2x$$ $$z\sec^2x=-\frac{\tan^3x}{3}+C,\; C=1 \rightarrow \frac{1}{y}\sec^2x=1-\frac{\tan^3x}{3}$$ The tan fan doubles, the security guard squares up, the case drops, then the initial condition opens the case and fixes it to $$1.$$
Q5 $$u=\sin y,\quad u'=\cos y\,y'$$ $$\mu=e^{\int \tan x\,dx}=\sec x$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}(u\sec x)=1$$ $$u\sec x=x+C \rightarrow \sin y\sec x=x+C$$ A coat wraps the yoyo, the singer appears as $$\sin y,$$ the tan fan meets the security guard, and the case plus coat finish the line.
Q6 $$z=\frac{1}{y},\quad z'=-\frac{1}{y^2}y'$$ $$\mu=e^{\int -\tan x\,dx}=\cos x$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}(z\cos x)=-1$$ $$z\cos x=-x+C \rightarrow y(x+C)+\cos x=0$$ The reciprocal yoyo meets a backward tan fan, the fire cools into $$\cos x,$$ and the coat restores $$y$$ inside the final product with $$x+C.$$
Q7 $$z=\frac{1}{x},\quad z'=-\frac{1}{x^2}x'$$ $$\mu=e^{\int -1/y\,dy}=1/y$$ $$\frac{d}{dy}(z/y)=1/y$$ $$z/y=\log y+C \rightarrow \frac{1}{xy}=\log(Cy)$$ The reciprocal image moves onto an x-mark, the fire settles into $$1/y,$$ and the coat restores the final reciprocal product $$1/(xy).$$
Q8 $$z=y^2,\quad z'=2yy'$$ $$\mu=e^{\int 2/x\,dx}=x^2$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}(zx^2)=-2x^3-2x^2$$ $$zx^2=-\frac{x^4}{2}-\frac{2x^3}{3}+C \rightarrow x^2y^2=-\frac{x^4}{2}-\frac{2x^3}{3}+C$$ Two yoyos fuse into a square helper, the fire hardens into $$x^2,$$ and the case plus coat restore the squared answer.
Q9 $$z=y^{-2},\quad z'=-2y^{-3}y'$$ $$\mu=e^{\int -2\,dx}=e^{-2x}$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}(ze^{-2x})=-6e^{-x}$$ $$ze^{-2x}=6e^{-x}+C \rightarrow \frac{1}{y^2}=6e^x+Ce^{2x}$$ The power-yoyo drops into a reciprocal-square pit, the fire lights $$e^{-2x},$$ and the coat restores $$1/y^2.$$
Q10 $$z=y^2,\quad z'=2yy'$$ $$\mu=e^{\int -1/x\,dx}=1/x$$ $$\frac{d}{dx}(z/x)=-1/x$$ $$z/x=-\log x+C \rightarrow y^2/x+\log x=C$$ The square helper lands on the mat as $$d(z/x)/dx,$$ then the case and coat finish the logarithm form.
One-Line Review
$$21\text{ net} \rightarrow 28\text{ knife} + 84\text{ fire} \rightarrow 31\text{ mat} \rightarrow 70\text{ case} \rightarrow 71\text{ coat}$$

If you can say that chain and instantly attach the formulas, the Bernoulli and square-swap workflows are already chunked into one reusable memory routine.
