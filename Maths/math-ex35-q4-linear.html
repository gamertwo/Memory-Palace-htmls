Exercise 3.5 Question 4

This one is linear, but the integrating factor is trigonometric and the right side needs one extra identity. Once you spot that $$e^{\int \sec x\,dx}=\sec x+\tan x,$$ the equation collapses into a product derivative.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}+y\sec x=\tan x\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad y=1+\frac{C-x}{\sec x+\tan x}$$
Quick Data
Method
First-order linear ODE
Standard Form
$$y'+(\sec x)y=\tan x$$
Here
$$P(x)=\sec x,\quad Q(x)=\tan x$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=e^{\int \sec x\,dx}=\sec x+\tan x$$
Useful Identity
$$\tan^2 x=\sec^2 x-1$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Identify the Linear Form

  • The equation is already in linear form
  • $$y'+P(x)y=Q(x).$$
  • Here
  • $$P(x)=\sec x,\qquad Q(x)=\tan x.$$

Room 2: Compute the Integrating Factor

  • Use
  • $$\mu(x)=e^{\int \sec x\,dx}.$$
  • From the standard integral,
  • $$\int \sec x\,dx=\ln|\sec x+\tan x|.$$
  • So on a fixed interval,
  • $$\mu(x)=\sec x+\tan x.$$

Room 3: Multiply Through by $$\sec x+\tan x$$

  • Multiply the whole equation:
  • $$(\sec x+\tan x)\frac{dy}{dx}+y\sec x(\sec x+\tan x)=\tan x(\sec x+\tan x).$$
  • Because
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x+\tan x)=\sec x(\sec x+\tan x),$$
  • the left side becomes
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[y(\sec x+\tan x)\right].$$

Room 4: Simplify the Right Side

  • We must integrate
  • $$\tan x(\sec x+\tan x)=\sec x\tan x+\tan^2 x.$$
  • Use $$\tan^2 x=\sec^2 x-1$$ to get
  • $$\sec x\tan x+\sec^2 x-1=\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x+\tan x)-1.$$

Room 5: Integrate

  • So the differential equation becomes
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[y(\sec x+\tan x)\right]=\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x+\tan x)-1.$$
  • Integrate both sides:
  • $$y(\sec x+\tan x)=\sec x+\tan x-x+C.$$

Room 6: Final Answer

  • Now divide by $$\sec x+\tan x:$$
  • $$y=\frac{\sec x+\tan x-x+C}{\sec x+\tan x}.$$
  • Split the fraction:
  • $$\boxed{y=1+\frac{C-x}{\sec x+\tan x}}.$$

Key Shortcut

After multiplying by $$\sec x+\tan x,$$ always check whether the right side can be rewritten using $$\tan^2 x=\sec^2 x-1.$$ That converts the messy trig product into something immediately integrable.

Memory Palace Path

This path has one extra room compared with the easy linear cases: you must simplify the right side before integrating.

Station 1: Linear Gate

Read the equation as $$y'+(\sec x)y=\tan x.$$

Transition 1 to 2

Once $$P(x)=\sec x$$ is identified, the integrating factor is fixed.

Station 2: IF Desk

Store $$\mu(x)=\sec x+\tan x.$$

Transition 2 to 3

Multiply by the integrating factor so the left side fuses into one derivative.

Station 3: Product Hall

Recognize $$\dfrac{d}{dx}[y(\sec x+\tan x)].$$

Transition 3 to 4

The right side still looks rough, so use a trig identity before integrating.

Station 4: Identity Vault

Rewrite $$\sec x\tan x+\tan^2 x$$ as $$\dfrac{d}{dx}(\sec x+\tan x)-1.$$

Transition 4 to 5

Now the integral is immediate.

Station 5: Integral Seal

Get $$y(\sec x+\tan x)=\sec x+\tan x-x+C.$$

Transition 5 to 6

One division remains to isolate $$y.$$

Station 6: Final Seal

Lock $$y=1+\frac{C-x}{\sec x+\tan x}.$$
