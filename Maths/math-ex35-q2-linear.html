Exercise 3.5 Question 2

This one looks different because it is given in differential form, but after rearrangement it becomes a linear equation in $$y.$$ Then the integrating-factor method works exactly the same way.

$$(2y-3x)\,dx+x\,dy=0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x^2y=x^3+C$$
Quick Data
Original Form
$$(2y-3x)\,dx+x\,dy=0$$
Standard Form
$$y'+\frac{2}{x}y=3$$
Here
$$P(x)=\frac{2}{x},\quad Q(x)=3$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=e^{\int 2/x\,dx}=x^2$$
Textbook Form
$$yx^2=x^3+C$$
Rooms

Room 1: Rearrange into $$dy/dx$$ Form

  • Start from
  • $$(2y-3x)\,dx+x\,dy=0.$$
  • Move the differential form into derivative form:
  • $$x\frac{dy}{dx}=3x-2y.$$
  • Now divide by $$x:$$
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{2}{x}y=3.$$

Room 2: Read Off the Integrating Factor

  • This is linear with
  • $$P(x)=\frac{2}{x}.$$
  • So
  • $$\mu(x)=e^{\int \frac{2}{x}\,dx}=e^{2\ln|x|}=x^2.$$

Room 3: Multiply Through by $$x^2$$

  • Multiply the whole equation by $$x^2:$$
  • $$x^2\frac{dy}{dx}+2xy=3x^2.$$
  • The left side is
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}(x^2y).$$

Room 4: Integrate

  • So we get
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}(x^2y)=3x^2.$$
  • Integrate both sides:
  • $$x^2y=\int 3x^2\,dx=x^3+C.$$

Room 5: Final Answer

  • The textbook answer is
  • $$\boxed{x^2y=x^3+C}.$$
  • Equivalently, $$y=x+\dfrac{C}{x^2}.$$

Key Shortcut

When an equation is given as $$M\,dx+N\,dy=0,$$ first see whether it can be rearranged into $$y'+P(x)y=Q(x).$$ Once that happens, it becomes a standard linear-equation problem.

Memory Palace Path

This route begins with rearrangement, then becomes a normal integrating-factor problem.

Station 1: Differential Gate

Convert $$(2y-3x)\,dx+x\,dy=0$$ into $$dy/dx$$ form.

Transition 1 to 2

Once $$y'$$ is isolated, the coefficient of $$y$$ becomes visible.

Station 2: IF Desk

Read $$P(x)=2/x$$ and store $$\mu=x^2.$$

Transition 2 to 3

Multiply by $$x^2$$ so the left side fuses into one derivative.

Station 3: Product Hall

Recognize $$x^2y'+2xy=\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^2y).$$

Transition 3 to 4

Only one elementary integral remains.

Station 4: Integral Vault

Integrate $$3x^2$$ to get $$x^3+C.$$

Transition 4 to 5

The result is already in textbook form.

Station 5: Final Seal

Lock $$x^2y=x^3+C.$$
