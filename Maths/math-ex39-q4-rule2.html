Exercise 3.9 Question 4

This larger expression still fits Rule II. The ratio becomes $$-4/y,$$ so the integrating factor is $$1/y^4.$$ After that, the exact finish gives a clean compact answer.

$$(2xy^4e^y+2xy^3+y)\,dx+(x^2y^4e^y-x^2y^2-3x)\,dy=0$$

$$x^2e^y+\frac{x^2}{y}+\frac{x}{y^3}=C$$
Ex 3.9 GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
Rule II Ratio
$$\frac{N_x-M_y}{M}=-\frac4y$$
Integrating Factor
$$e^{\int -4/y\,dy}=\frac1{y^4}$$
Formula Used
$$\text{I.F.}=e^{\int f(y)\,dy}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Check Rule II

  • Here $$M=2xy^4e^y+2xy^3+y$$ and $$N=x^2y^4e^y-x^2y^2-3x.$$
  • Compute $$M_y=2xe^y(y^4+4y^3)+6xy^2+1$$ and $$N_x=2xy^4e^y-2xy^2-3.$$
  • This gives $$\dfrac{N_x-M_y}{M}=-\dfrac4y.$$

Room 2: Build the I.F.

  • Rule II gives $$\text{I.F.}=e^{\int -4/y\,dy}=y^{-4}.$$

Room 3: Multiply Through

  • The exact equation becomes
  • $$\left(2xe^y+\frac{2x}{y}+\frac1{y^3}\right)dx+\left(x^2e^y-\frac{x^2}{y^2}-\frac{3x}{y^4}\right)dy=0.$$

Room 4: Solve the Exact Equation

  • Integrate the new $$M$$ with respect to $$x$$:
  • $$F=x^2e^y+\frac{x^2}{y}+\frac{x}{y^3}+g(y).$$
  • Matching $$F_y$$ with the new $$N$$ gives $$g'(y)=0.$$

Room 5: Final Answer

  • Hence $$x^2e^y+\dfrac{x^2}{y}+\dfrac{x}{y^3}=C.$$
Solution Flow

Even though the expression looks large, the route stays the same: isolate the Rule II ratio, multiply by $$y^{-4},$$ and finish with one exact-equation potential.

Differentiate $$M,N$$→Get $$-4/y$$→Use I.F. $$=1/y^4$$→Make it exact→Write final potential
