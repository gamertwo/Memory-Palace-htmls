Exercise 3.13 Question 6

This last one is a higher-degree first-order equation. It is easiest to treat it as solvable for $$y$$ in terms of $$x$$ and $$p,$$ then take the parameter $$p=c$$ for the complete integral family.

$$x^2(y-px)=yp^2$$

$$y=\frac{cx^3}{x^2-c^2}$$

Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Solve for $$y$$

  • Start from $$x^2(y-px)=yp^2.$$
  • Expand and collect the $$y$$ terms:
  • $$x^2y-x^3p=yp^2 \Rightarrow y(x^2-p^2)=px^3.$$

Room 2: Write the Complete Integral

  • So $$y=\dfrac{px^3}{x^2-p^2}.$$
  • Taking $$p=c$$ gives the complete integral
  • $$y=\frac{cx^3}{x^2-c^2}.$$

Room 3: Final Family

  • The solution family used on this page is
  • $$\boxed{y=\frac{cx^3}{x^2-c^2}}.$$
Solution Flow

Collect the $$y$$ terms, solve explicitly for $$y,$$ and then replace $$p$$ by a constant for the complete integral family.

Expand→Collect $$y$$→Solve for $$y$$→Set $$p=c$$
