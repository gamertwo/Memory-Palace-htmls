Exercise 3.6 Question 4

The initial condition lets us determine the constant after solving the Bernoulli equation. The reciprocal substitution again reduces everything to a linear trig ODE.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=2y\tan x+y^2\tan^2x,\quad y(0)=1\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\frac{1}{y}\sec^2x=1-\frac{\tan^3x}{3}}$$
Quick Data
Bernoulli Form
$$y'-2\tan x\,y=\tan^2x\,y^2$$
Substitution
$$z=\frac{1}{y}$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=\sec^2x$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Standard Form

  • Write it as $$\dfrac{dy}{dx}-2\tan x\,y=\tan^2x\,y^2.$$

Room 2: Reciprocal Substitution

  • Let $$z=\dfrac{1}{y}.$$ Then $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}=-\dfrac{1}{y^2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}.$$
  • To make the substitution appear across the full equation, multiply by $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}.$$

Room 3: Linear Equation

  • Multiplying $$\dfrac{dy}{dx}-2\tan x\,y=\tan^2x\,y^2$$ by $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}$$ gives $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}+2\tan x\,\dfrac{1}{y}=-\tan^2x.$$
  • Now replace $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}$$ by $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}.$$
  • Replace $$\dfrac{1}{y}$$ by $$z.$$
  • So the equation becomes $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}+2\tan x\,z=-\tan^2x.$$

Room 4: Integrate

  • Now $$P(x)=2\tan x,$$ so $$\mu(x)=e^{\int 2\tan x\,dx}=\sec^2x.$$
  • Multiply through: $$\dfrac{d}{dx}(z\sec^2x)=-\tan^2x\sec^2x.$$
  • Hence $$z\sec^2x=-\dfrac{\tan^3x}{3}+C.$$

Room 5: Use the Initial Value

  • At $$x=0,$$ we have $$y=1,$$ so $$z=1.$$ Also $$\sec0=1$$ and $$\tan0=0,$$ hence $$C=1.$$
  • Thus $$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{y}\sec^2x=1-\dfrac{\tan^3x}{3}}.$$

Key Shortcut

Use the initial condition only after you have integrated. That keeps the transformed equation cleaner and avoids mixing old and new variables too early.
