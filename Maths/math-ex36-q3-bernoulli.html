Exercise 3.6 Question 3

The nonlinear term is $$y^2,$$ so the reciprocal substitution works again. After that, it becomes a linear trig equation in $$z=\dfrac{1}{y}.$$

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=y\tan x-y^2\sec x\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\sec x=(\tan x+C)y}$$
Quick Data
Bernoulli Form
$$y'-\tan x\,y=-\sec x\,y^2$$
Substitution
$$z=\frac{1}{y}$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=\sec x$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Standard Bernoulli Shape

  • Move the linear term left:
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}-\tan x\,y=-\sec x\,y^2.$$

Room 2: Reciprocal Substitution

  • Let $$z=\dfrac{1}{y}.$$ Then $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}=-\dfrac{1}{y^2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}.$$
  • To make the substitution show up across the full equation, multiply by $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}.$$

Room 3: Linear Equation

  • Multiplying $$\dfrac{dy}{dx}-\tan x\,y=-\sec x\,y^2$$ by $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}$$ gives $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\tan x\,\dfrac{1}{y}=\sec x.$$
  • Now replace $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}$$ by $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}.$$
  • Replace $$\dfrac{1}{y}$$ by $$z.$$
  • So the equation becomes $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}+\tan x\,z=\sec x.$$

Room 4: Integrating Factor

  • Since $$P(x)=\tan x,$$ $$\mu(x)=e^{\int \tan x\,dx}=\sec x.$$
  • Multiply through to get $$\dfrac{d}{dx}(z\sec x)=\sec^2 x.$$
  • Integrating gives $$z\sec x=\tan x+C.$$

Room 5: Final Answer

  • Put back $$z=\dfrac{1}{y}:$$
  • $$\frac{\sec x}{y}=\tan x+C.$$
  • So $$\boxed{\sec x=(\tan x+C)y}.$$

Key Shortcut

If a Bernoulli equation has power $$2,$$ the substitution collapses to the simple reciprocal $$z=1/y.$$
