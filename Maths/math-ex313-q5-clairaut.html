Exercise 3.13 Odd Route

Question 5

This is the clean Clairaut room of the route. The recognition step does most of the work, but the page still explains why the constant replacement is valid and how the singular envelope is produced if you want the full picture.

$$y=px+p^3 \qquad \text{where } p=\frac{dy}{dx}$$

$$y=ax+a^3$$

Quick Map
Problem
$$y=px+p^3$$
Type / Recognition
True Clairaut equation $$y=px+f(p)$$ with $$f(p)=p^3.$$

Linear Review

This page is short mathematically, but the reason it is short matters. We are not skipping work. We are using the special Clairaut structure directly and then showing the optional singular envelope afterward.

Room 1 Actual Explanation: Identify The Exact Equation Type

Problem: Solve $$y=px+p^3$$ where $$p=\dfrac{dy}{dx}.$$

Type / Recognition: This is exactly of the form

$$y=px+f(p)$$

with

$$f(p)=p^3$$

This matters because Clairaut equations have a standard general solution family. Once the structure is recognized, we do not need to solve a new separable equation from scratch.

Room 2 Actual Explanation: Why Replacing $$p$$ By A Constant Works

For Clairaut's equation, the general solution is the family of straight lines obtained by setting

$$p=c$$

where $$c$$ is an arbitrary constant. The reason is that each member of the family has constant slope, and for a constant slope line, $$p=\dfrac{dy}{dx}$$ stays constant.

Substitute $$p=c$$ into the equation:

$$y=cx+c^3$$

This is already the complete general solution family.

Room 3 Actual Explanation: Match The Book Constant Style

The textbook answer writes the arbitrary constant as $$a$$ instead of $$c.$$ That is just a rename of the constant, so the answer becomes

$$y=ax+a^3$$

Changing the symbol for the constant does not change the family of solutions.

Room 4 Actual Explanation: Optional Singular Solution

If you also want the envelope of the family, differentiate the Clairaut expression with respect to $$p$$ while treating $$x$$ as the line parameter relation:

$$x+3p^2=0$$

So

$$x=-3p^2$$

Substitute into $$y=px+p^3$$:

$$y=p(-3p^2)+p^3=-2p^3$$

Now eliminate $$p.$$ From $$x=-3p^2,$$ we get $$p^2=-x/3.$$ Squaring $$y=-2p^3$$ gives

$$y^2=4p^6=4(p^2)^3=4\left(-\frac{x}{3}\right)^3=-\frac{4x^3}{27}$$

Therefore the singular curve is

$$27y^2+4x^3=0$$

But if the exercise asks only for the ordinary solution family, the expected answer is still just $$y=ax+a^3.$$

Memory Palace Rooms

Room 1: Enter The Clairaut Terrace

After the branch forge, the route opens onto a calm terrace. A polished stone reads $$y=px+p^3.$$ Unlike the earlier rooms, nothing is tangled here. Ichigo immediately recognizes the terrace emblem $$y=px+f(p).$$ The side sculpture $$p^3$$ confirms that $$f(p)=p^3.$$

Room 2: Constant Seal

A glowing seal drops onto the slope spear $$p$$ and freezes it into a constant crystal $$a.$$ The whole terrace line immediately becomes the final family image:

$$y=ax+a^3$$

This room memorizes the central Clairaut idea: once the slope is frozen, the line family appears at once.

Room 3: Optional Envelope Chamber

Behind the terrace, a hidden chamber shows the envelope. A derivative blade scratches $$x+3p^2=0$$ into the wall. The line family folds inward until the envelope freezes as

$$27y^2+4x^3=0$$

The chamber is optional, but it helps connect this page to the broader Clairaut system.
