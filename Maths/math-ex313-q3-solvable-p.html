Exercise 3.13 Odd Route

Question 3

This page enters the branch-forge zone. The equation is quadratic in $$p$$ and factors cleanly, so the main idea is to split the problem into two first-order branches and solve each one completely.

$$x^2p^2+xyp-6y^2=0 \qquad \text{where } p=\frac{dy}{dx}$$

$$y=\frac{c}{x^3}, \qquad y=c_1x^2$$

Quick Map
Problem
$$x^2p^2+xyp-6y^2=0$$
Type / Recognition
Quadratic in $$p$$ that factors directly.
Main Idea
Factor into two linear branch equations in $$p,$$ then solve each branch as a separable differential equation.
Linear Review

Here the key is not substitution first. The correct move is algebra first: factor the quadratic in $$p$$, split into branches, and solve each branch without skipping the logarithm steps.

Room 1 Actual Explanation: Recognize The Quadratic In $$p$$

Problem: Solve $$x^2p^2+xyp-6y^2=0$$ where $$p=\dfrac{dy}{dx}.$$

Type / Recognition: The equation is quadratic in $$p.$$ The powers are $$p^2,$$ $$p,$$ and a constant term in the quadratic sense, so we try to factor it just as an algebraic quadratic.

Write it in factor form by looking for two expressions whose product gives $$x^2p^2-6y^2$$ and whose middle term adds to $$xyp.$$

$$x^2p^2+xyp-6y^2=(xp-2y)(xp+3y)$$

Check it explicitly:

$$xp\cdot xp=x^2p^2$$ $$xp\cdot 3y=3xyp$$ $$-2y\cdot xp=-2xyp$$ $$-2y\cdot 3y=-6y^2$$

The middle terms combine to $$3xyp-2xyp=xyp,$$ so the factorization is correct.

Room 2 Actual Explanation: Split Into Two Branches

Since the product is zero, at least one factor must be zero:

$$xp-2y=0 \qquad \text{or} \qquad xp+3y=0$$

Now replace $$p$$ by $$\dfrac{dy}{dx}$$ in each branch. That gives two separate first-order equations.

First branch:

$$x\frac{dy}{dx}-2y=0$$

Second branch:

$$x\frac{dy}{dx}+3y=0$$

Room 3 Actual Explanation: Solve Branch One Completely

Start from

$$x\frac{dy}{dx}-2y=0$$

Move $$2y$$ to the other side:

$$x\frac{dy}{dx}=2y$$

Divide by $$x$$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{2y}{x}$$

To separate variables, divide by $$y$$ and multiply by $$dx$$:

$$\frac{dy}{y}=2\frac{dx}{x}$$

Integrate both sides:

$$\int\frac{dy}{y}=2\int\frac{dx}{x}$$

So

$$\ln|y|=2\ln|x|+C$$

Exponentiate:

$$|y|=e^C|x|^2$$

Absorb the sign and $$e^C$$ into one constant:

$$y=c_1x^2$$

Room 4 Actual Explanation: Solve Branch Two Completely

Start from

$$x\frac{dy}{dx}+3y=0$$

Move $$3y$$ to the other side:

$$x\frac{dy}{dx}=-3y$$

Divide by $$x$$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{3y}{x}$$

Separate variables:

$$\frac{dy}{y}=-3\frac{dx}{x}$$

Integrate:

$$\int\frac{dy}{y}=-3\int\frac{dx}{x}$$

So

$$\ln|y|=-3\ln|x|+C$$

Exponentiate and absorb the constant:

$$y=\frac{c_2}{x^3}$$

Room 5 Actual Explanation: Collect The Full Answer

Because the original equation split into two valid branches, both families belong to the final answer set:

$$y=c_1x^2,\qquad y=\frac{c_2}{x^3}$$

The textbook answer may rename the constants differently, but the two families are exactly these two branches.

Memory Palace Rooms

Room 1: Enter The Branch Forge

The next connected zone is a forge where the equation wall $$x^2p^2+xyp-6y^2=0$$ is struck by a factoring hammer. The wall cracks into two doors: $$(xp-2y)$$ and $$(xp+3y).$$ Both doors glow because a zero-product law demands that at least one door must vanish.

Room 2: First Branch Corridor

Ichigo enters the first door labeled $$xp-2y=0.$$ The slope spear $$p$$ turns into $$dy/dx,$$ so the corridor reads $$x\,dy/dx=2y.$$ He pulls $$y$$ to the left and $$x$$ to the right, making the separated rails $$dy/y=2\,dx/x.$$ The rails climb together and freeze into the growth banner $$y=c_1x^2.$$

Room 3: Second Branch Corridor

The second door reads $$xp+3y=0.$$ This time the slope spear is dragged downward into $$x\,dy/dx=-3y.$$ After separation, the right corridor slopes downward as $$dy/y=-3\,dx/x.$$ The final ice sculpture here is a decaying tower wrapped around the base rail:

$$y=\frac{c_2}{x^3}$$

Room 4: Twin Final Images

At the end of the forge, both solved branches stand side by side as twin frozen images. One rises upward like $$c_1x^2$$ and one falls backward like $$c_2/x^3.$$ The room teaches that factoring a differential equation in $$p$$ can legitimately create multiple complete solution families.
