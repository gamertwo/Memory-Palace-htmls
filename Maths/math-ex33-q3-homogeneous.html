Exercise 3.3 Question 3

This equation is solved by the homogeneous method. First rewrite it as $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, then use $y=vx$ and separate the variables.

$$x(y-x)\frac{dy}{dx}=y(y+x)$$
Rooms

Room 1: Convert to Ratio Form

  • First isolate $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$:
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{y(y+x)}{x(y-x)}.$$
  • Write it as a ratio of $\dfrac{y}{x}$:
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)\left(\frac{y}{x}+1\right)}{\left(\frac{y}{x}-1\right)}.$$

Room 2: Use the Homogeneous Substitution

  • Since the right side depends only on $\dfrac{y}{x}$, put
  • $$y=vx \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \frac{dy}{dx}=v+x\frac{dv}{dx}.$$
  • Then the equation becomes
  • $$v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{v(v+1)}{v-1}.$$

Room 3: Simplify the v-Equation

  • Move $v$ to the right side:
  • $$x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{v(v+1)}{v-1}-v.$$
  • Simplify:
  • $$x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{v(v+1)-v(v-1)}{v-1}=\frac{2v}{v-1}.$$

Room 4: Separate Variables

  • Bring $v$ terms to the left and $x$ terms to the right:
  • $$\frac{v-1}{2v}\,dv=\frac{dx}{x}.$$
  • That is
  • $$\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{v}\right)dv=\frac{dx}{x}.$$

Room 5: Integrate

  • Integrate both sides:
  • $$\int \frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{v}\right)dv=\int \frac{dx}{x}.$$
  • $$\frac{v}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\ln|v|=\ln|x|+C.$$
  • Multiply through by $2$:
  • $$v-\ln|v|=2\ln|x|+C.$$

Room 6: Back-Substitute and Compress Logs

  • Put back $$v=\frac{y}{x}:$$
  • $$\frac{y}{x}-\ln\left|\frac{y}{x}\right|=2\ln|x|+C.$$
  • Use $$-\ln\left|\frac{y}{x}\right|=-\ln|y|+\ln|x|,$$ so
  • $$\frac{y}{x}-\ln|y|+\ln|x|=2\ln|x|+C.$$
  • Hence
  • $$\frac{y}{x}-\ln|y|-\ln|x|=C.$$
  • Combine the logs:
  • $$\boxed{\frac{y}{x}-\ln|xy|=C}.$$
  • In textbook shorthand, this is written as $$\boxed{\frac{y}{x}-\log(xy)=a}$$ when $x,y>0$.
Final Answer

Using $y=vx$, the homogeneous equation reduces to a separable form and gives $$\boxed{\frac{y}{x}-\ln|xy|=C}.$$ In the same textbook style for positive variables, this becomes $$\boxed{\frac{y}{x}-\log(xy)=a}.$$
