Exercise 3.5 Question 5

The coefficient of $$y'$$ is not 1, so first divide by $$\cos^2 x.$$ After that, the equation becomes linear, the integrating factor is $$e^{\tan x},$$ and the remaining integral is handled by the substitution $$u=\tan x.$$

$$\cos^2 x\frac{dy}{dx}+y=\tan x\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad y=\tan x-1+Ce^{-\tan x}$$
Quick Data
First Step
Divide by $$\cos^2 x$$
Standard Form
$$y'+(\sec^2 x)y=\tan x\sec^2 x$$
Here
$$P(x)=\sec^2 x,\quad Q(x)=\tan x\sec^2 x$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=e^{\int \sec^2 x\,dx}=e^{\tan x}$$
Substitution
$$u=\tan x,\quad du=\sec^2 x\,dx$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Put the Equation in Standard Form

  • Start with
  • $$\cos^2 x\frac{dy}{dx}+y=\tan x.$$
  • Divide through by $$\cos^2 x$$ to make the coefficient of $$y'$$ equal to 1:
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}+\sec^2 x\,y=\tan x\sec^2 x.$$
  • Now this is a standard linear equation.

Room 2: Compute the Integrating Factor

  • Here
  • $$P(x)=\sec^2 x.$$
  • So
  • $$\mu(x)=e^{\int \sec^2 x\,dx}=e^{\tan x}.$$

Room 3: Multiply Through by $$e^{\tan x}$$

  • Multiply the whole equation:
  • $$e^{\tan x}\frac{dy}{dx}+e^{\tan x}\sec^2 x\,y=\tan x\sec^2 x\,e^{\tan x}.$$
  • The left side is
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}\left(ye^{\tan x}\right).$$

Room 4: Integrate the Right Side

  • So we have
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}\left(ye^{\tan x}\right)=\tan x\sec^2 x\,e^{\tan x}.$$
  • Integrate both sides:
  • $$ye^{\tan x}=\int \tan x\sec^2 x\,e^{\tan x}\,dx.$$
  • Let $$u=\tan x,$$ then $$du=\sec^2 x\,dx.$$
  • This gives
  • $$ye^{\tan x}=\int ue^u\,du.$$

Room 5: Finish the Integral

  • Use the standard result
  • $$\int ue^u\,du=e^u(u-1)+C.$$
  • So
  • $$ye^{\tan x}=e^{\tan x}(\tan x-1)+C.$$

Room 6: Final Answer

  • Divide through by $$e^{\tan x}:$$
  • $$y=\tan x-1+Ce^{-\tan x}.$$
  • Hence
  • $$\boxed{y=\tan x-1+Ce^{-\tan x}}.$$

Key Shortcut

If the equation starts with a coefficient on $$y',$$ normalize it first. After division, look for whether $$P(x)$$ integrates to a familiar function and whether the right side suggests a direct substitution after multiplying by the integrating factor.
