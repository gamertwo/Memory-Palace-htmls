Exercise 3.8 Question 3

Here the Rule I ratio becomes $$-2/x,$$ so the integrating factor is $$x^{-2}.$$ Once multiplied through, the equation becomes a clean exact form with a quadratic finish in $$y.$$

$$(y-2x^3)\,dx-x(1-xy)\,dy=0$$

$$-\frac{y}{x}-x^2+\frac{y^2}{2}=C$$
Ex 3.8 GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
M(x,y)
$$M=y-2x^3$$
N(x,y)
$$N=-x+x^2y$$
Rule Test
$$\frac{M_y-N_x}{N}=\frac{1-(2xy-1)}{-x+x^2y}=-\frac2x$$
Integrating Factor
$$\text{I.F.}=e^{\int -2/x\,dx}=\frac1{x^2}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Identify $M$ and $N$

  • Take $$M=y-2x^3$$ and $$N=-x+x^2y.$$

Room 2: Show It Is Not Exact

  • Compute $$M_y=1$$ and $$N_x=2xy-1.$$
  • They do not match, so the equation is not exact.

Room 3: Apply Rule I

  • Use $$\dfrac{M_y-N_x}{N}$$ and simplify.
  • The result is $$-\dfrac2x,$$ a function of $$x$$ alone.
  • So $$\text{I.F.}=e^{\int -2/x\,dx}=\dfrac1{x^2}.$$

Room 4: Multiply Through

  • Multiplying by $$x^{-2}$$ gives
  • $$\left(\frac{y}{x^2}-2x\right)dx+\left(y-\frac1x\right)dy=0,$$ which is exact.

Room 5: Solve the Exact Equation

  • Integrate the new $$M$$ with respect to $$x$$:
  • $$F=-\frac{y}{x}-x^2+g(y).$$
  • Then $$F_y=-\dfrac1x+g'(y).$$ Matching with $$N=y-\dfrac1x$$ gives $$g'(y)=y,$$ so $$g(y)=\dfrac{y^2}{2}.$$

Room 6: Final Answer

  • The potential function is $$F=-\dfrac{y}{x}-x^2+\dfrac{y^2}{2}.$$
  • Hence $$-\dfrac{y}{x}-x^2+\dfrac{y^2}{2}=C.$$

Key Shortcut

If you can factor the numerator and denominator by $(1-xy)$, the Rule I simplification becomes immediate.

Why This Flow Matters

This is a strong example of how a negative power integrating factor can cleanly expose the exact structure.

Solution Flow

The whole route pivots on recognizing the repeated $$1-xy$$ factor inside the Rule I ratio.

Identify→Not exact→Rule I gives $$-2/x$$→Multiply by $$x^{-2}$$→Solve exact form
