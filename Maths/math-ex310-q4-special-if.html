Exercise 3.10 Question 4

This last question is the trig version. The integrating factor is still simple, but the fastest finish is to spot a direct total derivative after multiplying by $$1/x^2.$$

$$(xy\sin xy+\cos xy)y\,dx+(xy\sin xy-\cos xy)x\,dy=0$$

$$y\cos(xy)=Cx$$

Quick Data
M(x,y)
$$M=y(xy\sin xy+\cos xy)$$
N(x,y)
$$N=x(xy\sin xy-\cos xy)$$
Formula Used
Recognize $$d\!\left(\dfrac{y\cos(xy)}{x}\right)$$ after multiplying by $$1/x^2.$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu=\frac1{x^2}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Multiply by the I.F.

  • Use $$\mu=1/x^2.$$
  • The new equation becomes
  • $$\left(\frac{y^2\sin(xy)}{x}+\frac{y\cos(xy)}{x^2}\right)dx+\left(y\sin(xy)-\frac{\cos(xy)}{x}\right)dy=0.$$

Room 2: Spot the Differential Formula

  • Differentiate $$F=-\dfrac{y\cos(xy)}{x}.$$
  • Then
  • $$dF=\left(\frac{y^2\sin(xy)}{x}+\frac{y\cos(xy)}{x^2}\right)dx+\left(y\sin(xy)-\frac{\cos(xy)}{x}\right)dy.$$

Room 3: Collapse to Exact Form

  • So the multiplied equation is simply $$dF=0.$$
  • Hence $$-\dfrac{y\cos(xy)}{x}=C.$$

Room 4: Final Answer

  • Absorb the minus sign into the constant.
  • The final answer is $$\dfrac{y\cos(xy)}{x}=C,$$ or equivalently $$y\cos(xy)=Cx.$$
