Exercise 3.6 Question 9

This is a Bernoulli equation with power $$3.$$ The helper variable absorbs the cubic power cleanly, and the last line becomes a simple reciprocal-square answer.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}+y=3e^xy^3\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\frac{1}{y^2}=6e^x+Ce^{2x}}$$
Major Guide
Quick Data
Bernoulli Form
$$y'+y=3e^xy^3$$
Substitution
$$z=y^{-2}$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=e^{-2x}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Bernoulli Shape

  • The equation is already in Bernoulli form: $$\dfrac{dy}{dx}+y=3e^xy^3.$$
  • The power is $$3,$$ so use the standard helper $$z=y^{1-3}=y^{-2}.$$

Room 2: Power Substitution

  • Let $$z=y^{-2}.$$ Then $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}=-2y^{-3}\dfrac{dy}{dx}.$$
  • Multiply the Bernoulli equation by $$-2y^{-3}$$ so the substitution appears everywhere.

Room 3: Linear Equation

  • Multiplying gives $$-2y^{-3}y'-2y^{-2}=-6e^x.$$
  • Now replace $$-2y^{-3}y'$$ by $$z'$$ and $$y^{-2}$$ by $$z.$$
  • So the linear form is $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}-2z=-6e^x.$$

Room 4: Integrating Factor

  • Here $$P(x)=-2,$$ so $$\mu(x)=e^{\int -2\,dx}=e^{-2x}.$$
  • Multiply through: $$\dfrac{d}{dx}(ze^{-2x})=-6e^{-x}.$$
  • Integrating gives $$ze^{-2x}=6e^{-x}+C.$$

Room 5: Back-Substitute

  • Multiply by $$e^{2x}:$$ $$z=6e^x+Ce^{2x}.$$
  • Put back $$z=y^{-2}$$ to get $$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{y^2}=6e^x+Ce^{2x}}.$$

Key Shortcut

For Bernoulli power $$n=3,$$ the helper is always $$y^{-2}.$$ That one power drop is the fast trigger for the whole page.
