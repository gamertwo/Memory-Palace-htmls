Exercise 3.9 Question 3

This exponential-algebraic form still collapses to the simple Rule II ratio $$-2/y.$$ The integrating factor is again $$1/y^2.$$

$$y(x^2y+e^x)\,dx-e^x\,dy=0$$

$$\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{e^x}{y}=C$$
Ex 3.9 GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
Rule II Ratio
$$\frac{N_x-M_y}{M}=-\frac2y$$
Integrating Factor
$$e^{\int -2/y\,dy}=\frac1{y^2}$$
Formula Used
$$\text{I.F.}=e^{\int f(y)\,dy}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Check Rule II

  • Here $$M=x^2y^2+ye^x$$ and $$N=-e^x.$$
  • Compute $$M_y=2x^2y+e^x$$ and $$N_x=-e^x.$$
  • So $$\dfrac{N_x-M_y}{M}=-\dfrac2y.$$

Room 2: Build the I.F.

  • Rule II gives $$\text{I.F.}=e^{\int -2/y\,dy}=y^{-2}.$$

Room 3: Multiply Through

  • The exact equation becomes
  • $$\left(x^2+\frac{e^x}{y}\right)dx-\frac{e^x}{y^2}dy=0.$$

Room 4: Solve the Exact Equation

  • Integrate the new $$M$$ with respect to $$x$$:
  • $$F=\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{e^x}{y}+g(y).$$
  • Matching $$F_y$$ with the new $$N$$ gives $$g'(y)=0.$$

Room 5: Final Answer

  • So $$\dfrac{x^3}{3}+\dfrac{e^x}{y}=C.$$
Solution Flow

The exponential term looks different at first, but the Rule II ratio still drops to $$-2/y,$$ and then the exact-equation finish is short.

Expand $$M,N$$→Get $$-2/y$$→Use I.F. $$=1/y^2$$→Multiply through→Recover potential
