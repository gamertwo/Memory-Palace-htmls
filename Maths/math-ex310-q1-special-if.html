Exercise 3.10 Question 1

This is the cleanest two-block Rule IV-style pattern. We try an integrating factor of the form $$x^h y^k,$$ solve for $$h$$ and $$k,$$ and then finish as an exact equation.

$$(y-xy^2)\,dx-(x+x^2y)\,dy=0$$

$$\log\!\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)-xy=C$$
Ex 3.10 GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
M(x,y)
$$M=y-xy^2$$
N(x,y)
$$N=-x-x^2y$$
Formula Used
Assume $$\mu=x^h y^k$$ and force $$\partial_y(\mu M)=\partial_x(\mu N).$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu=x^{-1}y^{-1}=\frac1{xy}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Spot the Pattern

  • Split the equation as $$\left(y\,dx-x\,dy\right)-xy\left(y\,dx+x\,dy\right)=0.$$
  • This suggests the special integrating factor form $$x^h y^k.$$

Room 2: Solve for $h$ and $k$

  • Take $$\mu=x^h y^k.$$ After multiplying, compare powers in $$\partial_y(\mu M)=\partial_x(\mu N).$$
  • The coefficient relations give $$h=k=-1.$$
  • So $$\mu=\dfrac1{xy}.$$

Room 3: Multiply Through

  • Multiplying by $$1/(xy)$$ gives
  • $$\left(\frac1x-y\right)dx+\left(-\frac1y-x\right)dy=0,$$ which is exact.

Room 4: Solve the Exact Equation

  • Integrate the new $$M$$ with respect to $$x$$:
  • $$F=\log x-xy+g(y).$$
  • Then $$F_y=-x+g'(y).$$ Matching with $$N=-\dfrac1y-x$$ gives $$g'(y)=-\dfrac1y,$$ so $$g(y)=-\log y.$$

Room 5: Final Answer

  • Hence $$F=\log x-\log y-xy.$$
  • So $$\log\!\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)-xy=C.$$
