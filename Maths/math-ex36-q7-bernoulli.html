Exercise 3.6 Question 7

This one becomes a Bernoulli equation after treating $$x$$ as a function of $$y.$$ The reciprocal swap is now built from $$x,$$ not $$y,$$ but the same net-knife-fire-mat-case-coat chain still works.

$$y\,dx+(x+x^2y)\,dy=0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\frac{1}{xy}=\log(Cy)}$$
Major Guide
Quick Data
Dependent Switch
Solve for $$x(y)$$
Substitution
$$z=\frac{1}{x}$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(y)=\frac{1}{y}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Rewrite in $$x(y)$$ Form

  • From $$y\,dx+(x+x^2y)\,dy=0,$$ divide by $$dy$$ to get $$y\frac{dx}{dy}+x+x^2y=0.$$
  • Then divide by $$y$$: $$\frac{dx}{dy}+\frac{x}{y}=-x^2.$$

Room 2: Reciprocal Substitution

  • Let $$z=\dfrac{1}{x}.$$ Then $$\dfrac{dz}{dy}=-\dfrac{1}{x^2}\dfrac{dx}{dy}.$$
  • Multiply the Bernoulli line by $$-\dfrac{1}{x^2}$$ so the helper appears everywhere.

Room 3: Linear Equation

  • Multiplying $$\dfrac{dx}{dy}+\dfrac{x}{y}=-x^2$$ by $$-\dfrac{1}{x^2}$$ gives $$-\dfrac{1}{x^2}\dfrac{dx}{dy}-\frac{1}{y}\frac{1}{x}=1.$$
  • Now replace $$-\dfrac{1}{x^2}\dfrac{dx}{dy}$$ by $$z'$$ and $$\dfrac{1}{x}$$ by $$z.$$
  • So the linear equation is $$\dfrac{dz}{dy}-\frac{1}{y}z=1.$$

Room 4: Integrating Factor

  • Here $$P(y)=-1/y,$$ so $$\mu(y)=e^{\int -1/y\,dy}=1/y.$$
  • Multiply through: $$\dfrac{d}{dy}\left(\frac{z}{y}\right)=\frac{1}{y}.$$
  • Integrating gives $$\frac{z}{y}=\log y + C=\log(Cy).$$

Room 5: Back-Substitute

  • Replace $$z=\dfrac{1}{x}$$ to get $$\dfrac{1}{xy}=\log(Cy).$$
  • That is the required final answer: $$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{xy}=\log(Cy)}.$$

Key Shortcut

If the equation becomes cleaner in $$dx/dy$$ than in $$dy/dx,$$ switch the dependent variable first. The same Bernoulli logic can still run in the new direction.
