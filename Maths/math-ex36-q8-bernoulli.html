Exercise 3.6 Question 8

This one is easiest after a square swap. Once $$y^2$$ is treated as the helper variable, the equation becomes first-order linear and the same integrating-factor pegs carry the full solution.

$$(x^2+y^2+x)\,dx+xy\,dy=0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{x^2y^2=-\frac{x^4}{2}-\frac{2x^3}{3}+C}$$
Major Guide
Quick Data
Helper Variable
$$z=y^2$$
Linear Form
$$z'+\frac{2}{x}z=-2x-2$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=x^2$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Rewrite the Differential Form

  • From $$(x^2+y^2+x)\,dx+xy\,dy=0,$$ divide by $$dx$$ to get $$x^2+y^2+x+xy\frac{dy}{dx}=0.$$
  • So $$y\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{y^2}{x}=-x-1.$$

Room 2: Square Substitution

  • Let $$z=y^2.$$ Then $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}=2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}.$$
  • Multiply the previous line by $$2$$ so the helper derivative appears cleanly.

Room 3: Linear Equation

  • Doubling $$y\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\dfrac{y^2}{x}=-x-1$$ gives $$2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\frac{2}{x}y^2=-2x-2.$$
  • Replace $$2yy'$$ by $$z'$$ and $$y^2$$ by $$z.$$
  • So the linear equation becomes $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}+\frac{2}{x}z=-2x-2.$$

Room 4: Integrating Factor

  • Here $$P(x)=2/x,$$ so $$\mu(x)=e^{\int 2/x\,dx}=x^2.$$
  • Multiply through: $$\dfrac{d}{dx}(zx^2)=-2x^3-2x^2.$$
  • Integrating gives $$zx^2=-\dfrac{x^4}{2}-\dfrac{2x^3}{3}+C.$$

Room 5: Back-Substitute

  • Replace $$z=y^2$$ to get $$x^2y^2=-\dfrac{x^4}{2}-\dfrac{2x^3}{3}+C.$$
  • That is the final answer: $$\boxed{x^2y^2=-\dfrac{x^4}{2}-\dfrac{2x^3}{3}+C}.$$

Key Shortcut

Whenever you see $$yy'$$ hiding inside the equation, test a square helper immediately. It often saves a full Bernoulli-style power substitution.
