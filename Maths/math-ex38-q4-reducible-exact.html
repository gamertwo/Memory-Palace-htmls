Exercise 3.8 Question 4

This trig-polynomial mix still follows the same Rule I pattern. The ratio again becomes $$-2/x,$$ so multiplying by $$x^{-2}$$ reveals an exact equation.

$$(x\sec^2 y-x^2\cos y)\,dy=(\tan y-3x^4)\,dx$$

$$x^3+\frac{\tan y}{x}-\sin y=C$$
Ex 3.8 GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
M(x,y)
$$M=3x^4-\tan y$$
N(x,y)
$$N=x\sec^2 y-x^2\cos y$$
Rule Test
$$\frac{M_y-N_x}{N}=\frac{-\sec^2 y-(\sec^2 y-2x\cos y)}{x\sec^2 y-x^2\cos y}=-\frac2x$$
Integrating Factor
$$\text{I.F.}=e^{\int -2/x\,dx}=\frac1{x^2}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Standard Form

  • Move everything to one side first:
  • $$\left(3x^4-\tan y\right)dx+\left(x\sec^2 y-x^2\cos y\right)dy=0.$$

Room 2: Show It Is Not Exact

  • Compute $$M_y=-\sec^2 y$$ and $$N_x=\sec^2 y-2x\cos y.$$
  • These are not equal, so the equation is not exact yet.

Room 3: Apply Rule I

  • Use $$\dfrac{M_y-N_x}{N}.$$
  • After simplification, it becomes $$-\dfrac2x.$$
  • Hence $$\text{I.F.}=e^{\int -2/x\,dx}=\dfrac1{x^2}.$$

Room 4: Multiply Through

  • After multiplying by $$x^{-2},$$ the exact equation is
  • $$\left(3x^2-\frac{\tan y}{x^2}\right)dx+\left(\frac{\sec^2 y}{x}-\cos y\right)dy=0.$$

Room 5: Solve the Exact Equation

  • Integrate the new $$M$$ with respect to $$x$$:
  • $$F=x^3+\frac{\tan y}{x}+g(y).$$
  • Then $$F_y=\dfrac{\sec^2 y}{x}+g'(y).$$ Matching with $$N=\dfrac{\sec^2 y}{x}-\cos y$$ gives $$g'(y)=-\cos y,$$ so $$g(y)=-\sin y.$$

Room 6: Final Answer

  • The potential function is $$F=x^3+\dfrac{\tan y}{x}-\sin y.$$
  • So $$x^3+\dfrac{\tan y}{x}-\sin y=C.$$

Key Shortcut

Always rewrite the equation into $Mdx+Ndy=0$ before testing the integrating-factor rule.

Why This Flow Matters

This problem proves the rule still works even when trig pieces and polynomial pieces are mixed together.

Solution Flow

The important shift is not the trig. It is seeing that the Rule I ratio still collapses to an x-only function.

Rearrange→Not exact→Rule I gives $$-2/x$$→Multiply by $$x^{-2}$$→Solve exact form
