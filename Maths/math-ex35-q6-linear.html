Exercise 3.5 Question 6

First divide by $$x+a$$. The equation becomes linear, the integrating factor is a negative power, and the right side collapses to a simple polynomial.

$$(x+a)\frac{dy}{dx}-3y=(x+a)^5\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{2y=(x+a)^5+2C(x+a)^3}$$
Quick Data
Standard Form
$$y'-\frac{3}{x+a}y=(x+a)^4$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=e^{\int -3/(x+a)\,dx}=(x+a)^{-3}$$
Product Form
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[y(x+a)^{-3}\right]=x+a$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Normalize

  • Divide by $$x+a$$:
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}-\frac{3}{x+a}y=(x+a)^4.$$

Room 2: Integrating Factor

  • $$P(x)=-\frac{3}{x+a}$$ so
  • $$\mu(x)=e^{\int -3/(x+a)\,dx}=(x+a)^{-3}.$$

Room 3: Multiply Through

  • After multiplying by $$(x+a)^{-3},$$
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[y(x+a)^{-3}\right]=x+a.$$

Room 4: Integrate

  • $$y(x+a)^{-3}=\int (x+a)\,dx=\frac{(x+a)^2}{2}+C.$$

Room 5: Final Answer

  • Multiply by $$(x+a)^3:$$
  • $$y=\frac{(x+a)^5}{2}+C(x+a)^3.$$
  • Hence $$\boxed{2y=(x+a)^5+2C(x+a)^3}.$$

Key Shortcut

Here the integrating factor cancels most of the forcing power immediately. Always check that before expanding anything.
