Exercise 3.6 Question 2

This is a Bernoulli equation with power $$y^4.$$ The standard substitution $$z=y^{-3}$$ converts it into a first-order linear equation.

$$3\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{3}{x}y=2x^4y^4\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{\frac{1}{y^3}=Cx^3-x^5}$$
Quick Data
Standard Form
$$y'+\frac{1}{x}y=\frac{2}{3}x^4y^4$$
Substitution
$$z=y^{-3}$$
Linear Form
$$z'-\frac{3}{x}z=-2x^4$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Normalize

  • Divide through by $$3$$:
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{1}{x}y=\frac{2}{3}x^4y^4.$$

Room 2: Bernoulli Swap

  • Since the power is $$4,$$ let $$z=y^{1-4}=y^{-3}.$$
  • Then $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}=-3y^{-4}\dfrac{dy}{dx}.$$
  • To make both substitutions appear at once, multiply the whole normalized equation by $$-3y^{-4}.$$

Room 3: Linear Equation

  • Multiplying by $$-3y^{-4}$$ gives $$-3y^{-4}\dfrac{dy}{dx}-\dfrac{3}{x}y^{-3}=-2x^4.$$
  • Now replace $$-3y^{-4}\dfrac{dy}{dx}$$ by $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}.$$
  • Also replace $$y^{-3}$$ by $$z.$$
  • So the equation becomes $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}-\dfrac{3}{x}z=-2x^4,$$ which is linear in $$z.$$

Room 4: Solve for $$z$$

  • The integrating factor is $$\mu(x)=e^{\int -3/x\,dx}=x^{-3}.$$
  • So $$\dfrac{d}{dx}(zx^{-3})=-2x.$$
  • Integrating gives $$zx^{-3}=-x^2+C.$$

Room 5: Final Answer

  • Multiply by $$x^3$$:
  • $$z=Cx^3-x^5.$$
  • Since $$z=\dfrac{1}{y^3},$$ $$\boxed{\dfrac{1}{y^3}=Cx^3-x^5}.$$

Key Shortcut

For Bernoulli form $$y'+P(x)y=Q(x)y^n,$$ the substitution is always $$z=y^{1-n}.$$ Here that means $$z=y^{-3}.$$
