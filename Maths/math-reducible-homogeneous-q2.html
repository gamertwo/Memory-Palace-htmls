Reducible to Homogeneous - Question 2

Again, constants prevent direct homogeneity. Translate $(x,y)$ first, then solve the reduced equation by $Y=vX$.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{y-x+1}{y+x+5}$$
Rooms

Room 1: Shift to Remove Constants

  • Put $$x=X+h,\quad y=Y+k.$$
  • From constants:
  • $$k-h+1=0,\qquad h+k+5=0.$$
  • So $$h=-2,\ k=-3.$$
  • Hence use
  • $$X=x+2,\qquad Y=y+3,\qquad \frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dY}{dX}.$$

Room 2: Reduced Homogeneous Form

  • Equation becomes
  • $$\frac{dY}{dX}=\frac{Y-X}{Y+X}.$$
  • This is homogeneous in $$X,Y.$$

Room 3: Use $$Y=vX$$

  • $$Y=vX\Rightarrow \frac{dY}{dX}=v+X\frac{dv}{dX}.$$
  • Then
  • $$v+X\frac{dv}{dX}=\frac{v-1}{v+1}.$$
  • So
  • $$X\frac{dv}{dX}=-\frac{v^2+1}{v+1}.$$

Room 4: Separate and Integrate

  • Separate:
  • $$\frac{v+1}{v^2+1}\,dv=-\frac{dX}{X}.$$
  • Integrate:
  • $$\int\frac{v+1}{v^2+1}\,dv=-\int\frac{dX}{X}.$$
  • $$\frac12\ln(1+v^2)+\tan^{-1}v=-\ln|X|+C.$$
  • Multiply by 2 and combine logs:
  • $$\ln(1+v^2)+2\tan^{-1}v=-2\ln|X|+C.$$
  • $$\ln\!\big((1+v^2)X^2\big)+2\tan^{-1}v=C.$$

Room 5: Back-Substitute

  • Since $$v=\dfrac{Y}{X},\quad (1+v^2)X^2=X^2+Y^2.$$
  • So
  • $$\ln(X^2+Y^2)+2\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{Y}{X}\right)=C.$$
  • Replace $$X=x+2,\ Y=y+3:$$
  • $$\boxed{\log\!\big[(y+3)^2+(x+2)^2\big]+2\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{y+3}{x+2}\right)=a}.$$
Final Answer

After translation and homogeneous substitution, the solution is $$\boxed{\log\!\big[(y+3)^2+(x+2)^2\big]+2\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{y+3}{x+2}\right)=a}.$$
