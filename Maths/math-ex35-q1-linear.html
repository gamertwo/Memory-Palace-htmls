Exercise 3.5 Question 1

This is a first-order linear differential equation. The only job is to recognize the coefficient of $$y$$, build the integrating factor, turn the left side into one exact derivative, and integrate once.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{1}{x}y=x^3-3\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad xy=\frac{x^5}{5}-\frac{3x^2}{2}+C$$
Quick Data
Method
First-order linear ODE
Standard Form
$$y'+P(x)y=Q(x)$$
Here
$$P(x)=\frac1x,\quad Q(x)=x^3-3$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=e^{\int (1/x)\,dx}=x$$
Domain Note
Solve on intervals with $$x\neq0$$
Rooms

Room 1: Recognize the Linear Pattern

  • The equation is already in standard linear form:
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{1}{x}y=x^3-3.$$
  • That means the integrating-factor method applies directly.

Room 2: Compute the Integrating Factor

  • Use $$\mu(x)=e^{\int P(x)\,dx}.$$
  • Here
  • $$\mu(x)=e^{\int \frac1x\,dx}=e^{\ln|x|}.$$
  • On a fixed interval, absorb the sign and write
  • $$\mu(x)=x.$$

Room 3: Multiply Through by the Integrating Factor

  • Multiply the whole differential equation by $$x:$$
  • $$x\frac{dy}{dx}+y=x^4-3x.$$
  • The left side is now the derivative of a product:
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}(xy)=x\frac{dy}{dx}+y.$$

Room 4: Integrate Once

  • So the equation becomes
  • $$\frac{d}{dx}(xy)=x^4-3x.$$
  • Integrate both sides:
  • $$xy=\int(x^4-3x)\,dx.$$
  • $$xy=\frac{x^5}{5}-\frac{3x^2}{2}+C.$$

Room 5: Final Answer

  • The textbook form is already clean:
  • $$\boxed{xy=\frac{x^5}{5}-\frac{3x^2}{2}+C}.$$
  • If desired, divide by $$x$$ to write $$y$$ explicitly, but the boxed implicit form is standard.

Key Shortcut

When the equation already looks like $$y'+P(x)y=Q(x),$$ do not rearrange unnecessarily. Read off $$P(x),$$ build the integrating factor, and look for the exact derivative of $$\mu(x)y.$$ That is the whole method.

Lock $$xy=\frac{x^5}{5}-\frac{3x^2}{2}+C.$$
