Exercise 3.6 Question 6

This is another Bernoulli equation with power $$2.$$ The reciprocal swap turns the nonlinear term into a linear trig equation, and the final answer collapses to a compact product with $$y.$$

$$\frac{dy}{dx}+y\tan x=y^2\sec x\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{y(x+C)+\cos x=0}$$
Major Guide
Quick Data
Bernoulli Form
$$y'+\tan x\,y=\sec x\,y^2$$
Substitution
$$z=\frac{1}{y}$$
Integrating Factor
$$\mu(x)=\cos x$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Standard Bernoulli Shape

  • Write the equation as $$\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\tan x\,y=\sec x\,y^2.$$
  • The power on $$y$$ is $$2,$$ so the reciprocal helper is the natural swap.

Room 2: Reciprocal Substitution

  • Let $$z=\dfrac{1}{y}.$$ Then $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}=-\dfrac{1}{y^2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}.$$
  • Multiply the Bernoulli line by $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}$$ so the swap appears across the whole equation.

Room 3: Linear Equation

  • After multiplying by $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2},$$ we get $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}-\tan x\,\dfrac{1}{y}=-\sec x.$$
  • Replace $$-\dfrac{1}{y^2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}$$ by $$z'$$ and $$\dfrac{1}{y}$$ by $$z.$$
  • So the linear form is $$\dfrac{dz}{dx}-\tan x\,z=-\sec x.$$

Room 4: Integrating Factor

  • Here $$P(x)=-\tan x,$$ so $$\mu(x)=e^{\int -\tan x\,dx}=\cos x.$$
  • Multiply through: $$\dfrac{d}{dx}(z\cos x)=-1.$$
  • Integrating gives $$z\cos x=-x+C.$$

Room 5: Back-Substitute

  • Replace $$z=\dfrac{1}{y}$$ to get $$\dfrac{\cos x}{y}=-x+C.$$
  • Rearrange and absorb the sign into the constant: $$\boxed{y(x+C)+\cos x=0}.$$

Key Shortcut

When the coefficient beside $$y$$ is $$\tan x,$$ the integrating factor often simplifies to a secant or cosine partner after the exponential rule.
