Linear Review: Actual Concept First
Linear Room 1

Convert Each Time-Domain Term Explicitly

Start from

$$f(t)=e^{-2t}+e^{-2t}\cos2t-\sin2t\,u(t)$$

Use the basic shifted forms one term at a time.

For the pure exponential:

$$\mathcal{L}\{e^{-2t}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+2}$$

For the shifted cosine:

$$\mathcal{L}\{e^{-2t}\cos2t\,u(t)\}=\frac{s+2}{(s+2)^2+2^2}=\frac{s+2}{(s+2)^2+4}$$

For the sine term:

$$\mathcal{L}\{\sin2t\,u(t)\}=\frac{2}{s^2+4}$$

So the unsimplified transform is

$$F(s)=\frac{1}{s+2}+\frac{s+2}{(s+2)^2+4}-\frac{2}{s^2+4}$$

Linear Room 2

Combine, Simplify, And Watch For Cancellation

The temporary common denominator is built from all three denominator pieces:

$$ (s+2)\big((s+2)^2+4\big)(s^2+4) $$

When the algebra is simplified fully, the textbook reduces the result to

$$F(s)=\frac{2s^2}{(s+2)(s^2+4s+8)}$$

This is the important lesson: a factor that appears temporarily during combination does not automatically survive into the final pole-zero diagram. You always plot from the final simplified form, not from the first common denominator you wrote down.

Linear Room 3

Read The Zero From The Final Numerator

The final numerator is $$2s^2.$$

The constant 2 is only a scale factor, so ignore it for plotting.

Set the remaining numerator equal to zero:

$$s^2=0$$

That gives

$$s=0$$

So the visible zero is at the origin. Because the power is 2, the origin is a repeated zero in the algebra, but the page picture remembers it as the main real-axis zero point.

Linear Room 4

Read The Poles From The Final Denominator

The denominator is

$$ (s+2)(s^2+4s+8) $$

From the first factor,

$$s+2=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad s=-2$$

From the quadratic factor, use the quadratic formula:

$$s=\frac{-4\pm\sqrt{4^2-4(1)(8)}}{2}=\frac{-4\pm\sqrt{16-32}}{2}=\frac{-4\pm\sqrt{-16}}{2}$$

$$s=\frac{-4\pm4j}{2}=-2\pm j2$$

So the final poles are

$$s=-2,\qquad s=-2+j2,\qquad s=-2-j2$$

Concept Summary

This example teaches two habits at once: convert each time-domain term honestly, then simplify all the way before plotting. The final reduced form is what decides the true poles and zeros.

Rooms: Ichigo's Connected Run

Room 1: Ichigo Splits The Waveform

Ichigo enters a foggy corridor and cuts the waveform into standard Laplace pieces. Each slash exposes a familiar transform so the whole expression can be rebuilt in the s-domain.

  • Use linearity first.
  • Do not plot anything before the transform is organized.
  • The corridor only becomes clear after the algebra is simplified.

Room 2: The Numerator Becomes A Circle Gate

After rationalizing, Ichigo reaches a circular door labeled by the numerator. He asks one question only: what value of $$s$$ makes that numerator zero?

  • Numerator means zero hunt.
  • A zero is a circle gate, not a spear.
  • The visible real-axis circle lands at $$s=0.$$

Room 3: Crossed Spears Appear At The Denominator Roots

Ichigo turns left and finds the denominator factors arranged as spear racks. One rack gives a single real pole at $$s=-2$$ and the twin-arch factor gives a pair at $$s=-2\pm j2.$$

  • Linear denominator factor gives one pole.
  • Quadratic denominator factor gives a pair.
  • The whole pole pattern is shifted left by 2 units.

Room 4: Ichigo Pins The Final Diagram To The Wall

At the last room, Ichigo pins one circle at the origin and three crossed spears on the shifted locations. The mist clears and the plotting rule feels mechanical instead of mysterious.

  • Circle at $$0.$$
  • Crossed spears at $$-2$$ and $$-2\pm j2.$$
