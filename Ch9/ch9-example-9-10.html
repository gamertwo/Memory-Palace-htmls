Example 9-10

Renji Follows The Origin Pole And The Imaginary Pair

Renji's scene is a forge walkway. One heavy spear is hammered into the origin while a twin-arch cosine gate throws two matching points up and down the imaginary axis.

$$f(t)=(4-3\cos500t)u(t)$$
Shape Guide Open In Builder
Quick Result
Pole at origin
A pole appears at $$s=0.$$ The constant term causes it.
Imaginary pair
Two poles land at $$s=\pm j500.$$ The cosine term causes the symmetric pair.

The zeros are not practical to plot here from the simplified expression, so the memory focus is on the pole pattern.
Linear Review: Actual Concept First
Linear Room 1

Transform The Constant And The Cosine Separately

Start from

$$f(t)=(4-3\cos500t)u(t)$$

Use linearity:

$$F(s)=4\mathcal{L}\{u(t)\}-3\mathcal{L}\{\cos500t\,u(t)\}$$

Now apply the basic table entries:

$$\mathcal{L}\{u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s},\qquad \mathcal{L}\{\cos500t\,u(t)\}=\frac{s}{s^2+500^2}$$

So the transform becomes

$$F(s)=\frac{4}{s}-\frac{3s}{s^2+500^2}$$

Linear Room 2

Combine Into One Rational Expression

The common denominator is $$s(s^2+500^2).$$

Rewrite each term over that denominator:

$$\frac{4}{s}=\frac{4(s^2+500^2)}{s(s^2+500^2)}$$

$$\frac{3s}{s^2+500^2}=\frac{3s^2}{s(s^2+500^2)}$$

Subtract the numerators:

$$F(s)=\frac{4(s^2+500^2)-3s^2}{s(s^2+500^2)}$$

Expand the top:

$$4(s^2+500^2)-3s^2=4s^2+1000000-3s^2=s^2+1000000$$

So

$$F(s)=\frac{s^2+1000000}{s(s^2+250000)}$$

Linear Room 3

Read The Denominator Poles Explicitly

The denominator is

$$s(s^2+250000)$$

Set it equal to zero factor by factor.

From $$s=0,$$ we get the origin pole.

From $$s^2+250000=0,$$ we get

$$s^2=-250000,\qquad s=\pm j500$$

So the pole set is

$$s=0,\quad s=+j500,\quad s=-j500$$

Linear Room 4

Understand Why The Diagram Focuses On Three Pole Points

The denominator already gives the main pole geometry: one center pole and one symmetric imaginary-axis pair.

That is the picture the page is trying to lock in:

pole at $$0,$$ pole at $$+j500,$$ and pole at $$-j500.$$

If you keep tracking the numerator too, $$s^2+1000000=0$$ gives zeros at $$\pm j1000.$$ But the teaching focus of this example is the three-pole frame caused by the constant-plus-cosine denominator structure.

Concept Summary

Example 9-10 is the clean “origin pole plus pure imaginary pair” example. The algebra leads to $$F(s)=\frac{s^2+1000000}{s(s^2+250000)},$$ so the pole pattern is read directly from $$s(s^2+250000).$$
