Example 9-3

The Zero Checkpoint Leaves Only One Bright Token

The chapter now zooms all the way into the instant around zero. The impulse does not care about the long road to infinity. It acts only at the checkpoint between $$0^-$$ and $$0^+$$ and samples the exponential at exactly zero.

$$f(t)=\delta(t), \qquad F(s)=\mathcal{L}\{\delta(t)\}=1$$

Linear Review: Actual Concept First
Linear Room 1

Start With The Impulse Definition Setup

Write the transform as

$$F(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty}\delta(t)e^{-st}\,dt$$

The lower limit is written as $$0^-$$ because the impulse acts at exactly zero.

Linear Room 2

Reduce The Interval To The Instant Around Zero

Since $$\delta(t)=0$$ everywhere except at $$t=0,$$ nothing away from zero contributes.

So the integral reduces to

$$F(s)=\int_{0^-}^{0^+}\delta(t)e^{-st}\,dt$$

Linear Room 3

Sample The Exponential At Zero

At the impulse location, $$t=0,$$ so

$$e^{-st}=e^0=1$$

That makes the integral

$$F(s)=\int_{0^-}^{0^+}\delta(t)\,dt$$

Linear Room 4

Use The Unit-Area Property

The impulse has unit area over that tiny interval, so

$$\int_{0^-}^{0^+}\delta(t)\,dt=1$$

Therefore,

$$F(s)=1$$

Concept Summary

The impulse transform is a sampling story, not an infinity story. The spike only cares about the exact instant at zero, where the exponential equals 1.

Rooms: The Zero Checkpoint

Room 1: Only The Gates Around Zero Matter

The long time road disappears and only one checkpoint remains. A gate marked $$0^-$$ stands just before zero, and a gate marked $$0^+$$ stands just after it.

  • This scene zooms into the instant around zero.
  • The rest of the timeline does not matter.
  • The integration interval shrinks around the checkpoint.

Room 2: A Razor-Thin Spear Spike Appears At The Marker

Exactly at the zero marker, a needle-thin spear spike appears. That spike is $$\delta(t),$$ meaning all the action happens at one instant and nowhere else.

  • The spike is not wide.
  • All action is concentrated at one point.
  • Everything off the point contributes zero.

Room 3: The Orb Gets Sampled Before It Can Decay

A glowing exponential orb tries to move along the road, but the impulse hits at exactly zero before any motion happens. Since the hit happens instantly, the orb is sampled at $$t=0,$$ where it equals 1.

  • No time passes before sampling.
  • That is why the exponential becomes 1.
  • The far road and infinity are irrelevant here.

Room 4: One Bright Token Is Left Behind

After the zero-point strike, only one bright token remains. There is no denominator structure here, just the pure unit result $$1.$$

  • The impulse leaves unit area.
  • The sampled value is 1.
  • The final answer is simply $$1.$$
Memory Hook

Impulse only acts at zero, so it samples the exponential at zero and leaves 1.
