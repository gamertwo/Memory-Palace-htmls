Chapter 9 Memory Guide

Shape Language For Laplace Formulas

Use one fixed visual language through the whole chapter so every new transform drops into the same palace instead of forcing you to memorize a brand-new picture each time.

Section 9-1 Core Symbols
Curved ODM Blade

$$\int$$

The long curved slash means integration: sweeping across time and accumulating the whole interval into one result.

Glowing Energy Orb

$$e$$

The bright orb stores exponential behavior. It can fade, be sampled, or be fused with other objects depending on the transform.

Downward Slash

$$-$$

A downward cut mark means decay, reduction, or a negative sign pulling the result down.

Spiral Vortex

$$s$$

The dark spinning vortex is the Laplace-domain pull. When it sits in the denominator, it becomes the base of the fraction image.

Timeline Road

$$t$$

The long straight road is the time axis. Motion along the road means the signal evolving over time.

Outer Gate Horizon

$$\infty$$

The giant wall gate at the horizon means the upper integration limit and the end of the time interval.

White Pillar

1

The upright white pillar stores a unit value or constant 1, especially when the exponential orb is evaluated at zero.

Flat White Ground

$$u(t)$$

The unit step is a flat platform that turns on and then stays unchanged for $$t\ge 0.$$

Gold Decay Ring

$$\alpha$$

The collar or ring attached to the vortex adds extra decay and changes the denominator from $$s$$ to $$s+\alpha.$$

Needle Spear Spike

$$\delta(t)$$

The razor-thin spike at one exact point stores the impulse: all the action happening at one instant.

Zero Gates

$$0^-$$ and $$0^+$$

Two small gates around zero show the tiny interval that matters for impulse integration.

Half Coin

0.5

A half-sized glowing coin stores the scaled impulse result when the transform collapses to a constant like 0.5.

Example 9-5 To Exercise 9-12 Shape Language
Derivative Slash

$$d/dt$$

A sharp acceleration cut means time differentiation: inject an $$s$$ vortex into the numerator and then check the value at $$0^-.$$

Oscillation Ring

$$\beta$$

A rotating frequency ring stores sinusoidal frequency. It becomes the top core in sine transforms and the paired base term in cosine and sine denominators.

Blue Rotation Aura

$$j$$

The blue imaginary rotation glow marks complex rotation and conjugate directions in Euler-form sinusoid splits.

Rising Ramp Road

$$tu(t)$$

A road that rises as you walk means the ramp. It is the step ground after one full integration sweep.

Collared Vortex

$$s+\alpha$$

A vortex wearing a decay collar stores a shifted first-order denominator caused by exponential decay.

Clockwise Complex Vortex

$$s-j\beta$$

One complex factor in the Euler split. It pairs with the opposite rotation to rebuild the sinusoid denominator.

Counterclockwise Vortex

$$s+j\beta$$

The conjugate partner of $$s-j\beta.$$ Adding and subtracting these two factors gives cosine and sine transform structures.

Locked Twin Base

$$s^2+\beta^2$$

Two opposite rotational systems locked together. This is the base image reused for all basic sine and cosine transform pages.

Stacked Structure

Fraction

The numerator object rests over the denominator base. Big mixed-signal pages work by building several structures and fusing them into one stacked final form.

20000 Core

$$20000$$

A giant compressed glow core used for the numeric sine example after multiplying 100 by 200.

40000 Base

$$40000$$

A heavy oscillation base that stores $$200^2$$ in the denominator.

6000 Crystal

$$6000$$

A large amplified energy crystal used on the integrated exponential page after the scale factor is applied.

Shape Legend
Circle

Zero

If a factor belongs to the numerator, imagine a bright white circle gate. Wherever it lands on the s-plane, that is a zero.

Crossed Spear

Pole

If a factor belongs to the denominator, imagine a black crossed spear. Wherever it lands, the response is pulled hard there.

Square Seal

Scale Factor

The constant in front, like $$K$$ or a numeric multiplier, becomes a stamped square seal at the palace entrance.

Twin Arch

Quadratic Pair

A quadratic term like $$s^2+\omega^2$$ becomes twin arches, telling you to expect a pair at $$+j\omega$$ and $$-j\omega.$$

Arrow

Real Shift

A term like $$s+a$$ means move left to $$-a.$$ The arrow always points from the symbol toward the actual plotted location.

Axis Ladder

s-Plane

The horizontal hallway is the real axis and the vertical ladder is the imaginary axis.

How To Read A Transform Fast

1. Identify the base signal

Ask whether the page is really a step story, a decaying step story, an impulse story, or a pole-zero plot.

2. Lock the main object

Use the flat ground, collared vortex, or zero spike to decide what family of transform you are in.

3. Add the modifier

Scale factors become chains, glowing bands, or smaller tokens before you worry about anything else.

4. Read the final frozen image

Always collapse the whole page to one last picture such as $$1/s,$$ $$1/(s+\alpha),$$ or a constant token.

5. If integrating

Drop the whole old structure onto one extra plain $$s$$ layer underneath it.

6. If differentiating

Inject an $$s$$ vortex into the numerator, then subtract the checkpoint value at $$0^-.$$

7. If adding signals

Build each known transform as its own structure first, then fuse them into one common denominator platform.

8. If sinusoidal

Reuse the locked twin-rotation base for $$s^2+\beta^2.$$ Sine leaves $$\beta$$ on top and cosine leaves $$s$$ on top.

Later Ch9 Pole-Zero Legend

1. Stamp the square

Separate the scale factor first so it does not get mistaken for a pole or zero.

2. Circle the numerator

Ask what values make the numerator zero, then place circle gates there.

3. Spear the denominator

Ask what values make the denominator zero, then place crossed spears there.

4. Split quadratics

If you see a twin arch, crack it into two symmetric points.
