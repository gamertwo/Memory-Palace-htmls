Example 9-8: Transform Each Known Signal Separately Before Combining

Start from

$$f(t)=2u(t)-5e^{-2t}u(t)+3\cos2t\,u(t)+3\sin2t\,u(t)$$

Apply linearity:

$$F(s)=2\mathcal{L}\{u(t)\}-5\mathcal{L}\{e^{-2t}u(t)\}+3\mathcal{L}\{\cos2t\,u(t)\}+3\mathcal{L}\{\sin2t\,u(t)\}$$

Transform each term:

$$F(s)=\frac{2}{s}-\frac{5}{s+2}+\frac{3s}{s^2+4}+\frac{6}{s^2+4}$$

Combine the last two first:

$$F(s)=\frac{2}{s}-\frac{5}{s+2}+\frac{3s+6}{s^2+4}$$

After rationalization, the final single-fraction form is

$$F(s)=\frac{16(s^2+1)}{s(s+2)(s^2+4)}$$

Exercise 9-11: Practice Mixed Addition On Two Smaller Cases

Part (a):

$$f(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)+4tu(t)-u(t)$$

Transform term by term:

$$F(s)=\frac{1}{s+2}+\frac{4}{s^2}-\frac{1}{s}$$

Combine the first and third terms:

$$\frac{1}{s+2}-\frac{1}{s}=\frac{s-(s+2)}{s(s+2)}=-\frac{2}{s(s+2)}$$

Then

$$F(s)=\frac{4}{s^2}-\frac{2}{s(s+2)}=\frac{4(s+2)-2s}{s^2(s+2)}=\frac{2(s+4)}{s^2(s+2)}$$

Part (b):

$$f(t)=[2+2\sin2t-2\cos2t]u(t)$$

So

$$F(s)=\frac{2}{s}+\frac{4}{s^2+4}-\frac{2s}{s^2+4}=\frac{2}{s}+\frac{4-2s}{s^2+4}$$

Use a common denominator:

$$F(s)=\frac{2(s^2+4)+s(4-2s)}{s(s^2+4)}=\frac{4s+8}{s(s^2+4)}=\frac{4(s+2)}{s(s^2+4)}$$

Exercise 9-12: Combine Integration And Differentiation With Known Bases

Part (a):

$$f(t)=e^{-4t}u(t)+5\int_0^t\sin4x\,dx$$

Transform the decay term:

$$\mathcal{L}\{e^{-4t}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+4}$$

For the integral term, first use $$\mathcal{L}\{\sin4t\}=\frac{4}{s^2+16}$$ and then divide by $$s$$:

$$5\mathcal{L}\left\{\int_0^t\sin4x\,dx\right\}=5\cdot\frac{1}{s}\cdot\frac{4}{s^2+16}=\frac{20}{s(s^2+16)}$$

So

$$F(s)=\frac{1}{s+4}+\frac{20}{s(s^2+16)}=\frac{s^3+36s+80}{s(s+4)(s^2+16)}$$

Part (b):

$$f(t)=5e^{-40t}u(t)+\frac{d}{dt}[5te^{-40t}u(t)]$$

The first term gives

$$\frac{5}{s+40}$$

The damped ramp has transform

$$\mathcal{L}\{5te^{-40t}u(t)\}=\frac{5}{(s+40)^2}$$

Differentiating injects $$s$$ and the checkpoint contributes zero:

$$\mathcal{L}\left\{\frac{d}{dt}[5te^{-40t}u(t)]\right\}=s\frac{5}{(s+40)^2}$$

So

$$F(s)=\frac{5}{s+40}+\frac{5s}{(s+40)^2}=\frac{5(s+40)+5s}{(s+40)^2}=\frac{10s+200}{(s+40)^2}$$

Example 9-8: Four Transform Warriors Fuse Into One Triple Base

A step tower, a decay tower, a cosine tower, and a sine tower appear separately. The sine and cosine towers already share the same oscillation base. Then all four structures move inward and fuse into one common denominator platform made of $$s,$$ $$s+2,$$ and $$s^2+4.$$ The top compresses into a bright $$16(s^2+1)$$ core.

  • Transform each known signal first.
  • Only after that do you fuse the structures.
  • The final image is $$16(s^2+1)$$ over a triple base.

Exercise 9-11: Small Mixed Workshops

In the first workshop, decay plus ramp minus step compresses into a base made of $$s^2$$ and $$s+2,$$ leaving $$2(s+4)$$ on top. In the second workshop, a step tower fuses with sine and negative cosine on the oscillation base and leaves $$4(s+2)$$ above $$s(s^2+4).$$

  • Part (a) is a decay-ramp-step workshop.
  • Part (b) is a step-sine-cosine workshop.
  • Both are practice in common-denominator fusion.

Exercise 9-12: Integration And Differentiation Towers Merge With Known Bases

One chamber combines a decay tower with an integrated sine tower, producing a triple denominator platform. The next chamber combines a decay tower with a differentiated damped-ramp tower, both sharing the squared collared base $$(s+40)^2.$$

  • Integrated sine means add one $$s$$ layer under the oscillation base.
  • Differentiated damped ramp means inject $$s$$ on top but keep the squared decay base.
  • The final memory images are the compressed numerators over the shared platforms.
