$$f(t)=\left(2e^{-t}-1\right)u(t)$$

Linear Review: Actual Concept First
Linear Room 1

Convert Each Time-Domain Piece

Do not jump straight to the answer. Convert the two visible parts one by one.

First part:

$$\mathcal{L}\{2e^{-t}u(t)\}=2\cdot\frac{1}{s+1}=\frac{2}{s+1}$$

Second part:

$$\mathcal{L}\{-u(t)\}=-\frac{1}{s}$$

So before any simplification, the transform is

$$F(s)=\frac{2}{s+1}-\frac{1}{s}$$

Linear Room 2

Combine The Two Fractions Carefully

The common denominator of $$\frac{2}{s+1}$$ and $$\frac{1}{s}$$ is $$s(s+1).$$

Rewrite both terms over that denominator:

$$\frac{2}{s+1}=\frac{2s}{s(s+1)},\qquad \frac{1}{s}=\frac{s+1}{s(s+1)}$$

Now subtract:

$$F(s)=\frac{2s}{s(s+1)}-\frac{s+1}{s(s+1)}=\frac{2s-(s+1)}{s(s+1)}$$

Then simplify the numerator:

$$2s-(s+1)=s-1$$

So the final rational form is

$$F(s)=\frac{s-1}{s(s+1)}$$

Linear Room 3

Find The Zero From The Numerator

The zero comes only from the numerator, so set the numerator equal to zero:

$$s-1=0$$

That gives

$$s=1$$

This is why the page has one real-axis zero at $$+1.$$ It does not matter that the denominator also contains $$s$$ and $$s+1$$. Those belong to the pole hunt, not the zero hunt.

Linear Room 4

Find The Poles From The Denominator

The poles come only from the denominator:

$$s(s+1)=0$$

That product is zero when either factor is zero.

From $$s=0,$$ we get a pole at the origin.

From $$s+1=0,$$ we get

$$s=-1$$

So the final plot has one circle at $$+1$$ and two poles at $$0$$ and $$-1.$$ That is the exact reason the picture looks like right-circle, center-pole, left-pole.

Concept Summary

Exercise 9-15 is the cleanest possible numerator-versus-denominator exercise. The algebra reduces to $$F(s)=\frac{s-1}{s(s+1)},$$ so the zero is read from $$s-1$$ and the poles are read from $$s(s+1).$$

Rooms: Rukia's Frozen Gallery

Room 1: Rukia Freezes The Transform Into One Fraction

Rukia freezes the moving terms into a single glass fraction. She refuses to plot until the numerator and denominator are visible as separate shapes.

  • First clean the transform.
  • Separate square seal from circle and spear shapes.
  • Readable algebra makes plotting faster than guessing.

Room 2: A Circle Gate Forms At $$s=1$$

The numerator crystallizes into a circle gate on the positive real side. Rukia reminds you that a zero can sit to the right or left depending on the factor; the page is about reading the algebra, not assuming stability from the word zero.

  • Zero comes only from the numerator.
  • Here the real-axis circle sits at $$s=1.$$
  • The scale factor is still not plotted.

Room 3: Two Spears Lock The Denominator

Rukia plants one spear at the origin and another at $$s=-1.$$ The frozen symmetry is gone here, so the memory image is simple: one spear at center, one spear one step left.

  • $$s=0$$ gives an origin pole.
  • $$s=-1$$ gives a negative real pole.
  • The denominator decides both spear positions.

Room 4: Rukia Shows The Clean Contrast

At the final ice wall, Rukia draws one circle at $$+1$$ and two spears at $$0$$ and $$-1.$$ This page exists to burn in the difference between numerator circles and denominator spears with no extra clutter.

  • One circle, two spears.
  • Positive real zero is allowed.
