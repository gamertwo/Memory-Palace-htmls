Linear Review: Actual Concept First
Linear Room 1

Separate The Three Jobs In The Formula

When you look at

$$F(s)=K\frac{(s-z_1)(s-z_2)\cdots}{(s-p_1)(s-p_2)\cdots}$$

do not treat the whole expression as one blur. It has three different jobs.

The constant $$K$$ is only a scale factor.

The numerator factors decide the zeros.

The denominator factors decide the poles.

That is why the constant is not plotted as a point, while numerator and denominator roots are.

Linear Room 2

Find Zeros By Killing The Numerator

Take a numerator factor like $$s-z_1.$$

To find its plotted location, ask when it becomes zero:

$$s-z_1=0$$

That gives

$$s=z_1$$

So the point $$z_1$$ is a zero.

If there are several numerator factors, repeat that same step for each one. Every root of the numerator becomes a zero on the pole-zero diagram.

Linear Room 3

Find Poles By Killing The Denominator

Now take a denominator factor like $$s-p_1.$$

Ask when that factor becomes zero:

$$s-p_1=0$$

That gives

$$s=p_1$$

So the point $$p_1$$ is a pole.

Again, if there are several denominator factors, repeat the same process for every factor. Every denominator root becomes a pole.

Linear Room 4

Handle Quadratic Factors Without Guessing

If a factor is quadratic, solve it explicitly instead of hoping the answer is obvious.

For example, if the denominator contains

$$s^2+\omega^2$$

set it equal to zero:

$$s^2+\omega^2=0$$

Move the constant term:

$$s^2=-\omega^2$$

Take the square root:

$$s=\pm j\omega$$

So one quadratic factor usually creates two symmetric plotted points, not one.

Concept Summary

A pole-zero diagram is a root map. Numerator roots become zeros, denominator roots become poles, and quadratic factors must be solved into pairs before plotting.

Rooms: One Connected Scenario

Room 1: Urahara Opens The Axis Hall

Urahara slides open a long hallway. The floor line is the real axis and a glowing ladder rises through the center as the imaginary axis. He tells you the whole chapter happens on this one map.

  • Horizontal hall = real part.
  • Vertical ladder = imaginary part.
  • Every future point must be placed relative to these two guides.

Room 2: Circle Gates Mark The Zeros

Urahara hangs bright circular gates over positions that make the numerator vanish. When the numerator becomes zero, the transfer function is zero there too.

  • Numerator factor means circle shape.
  • If $$s=z_1$$ makes the numerator zero, plot a circle at $$z_1.$$
  • The circles are openings, not attacks.

Room 3: Crossed Spears Mark The Poles

In the next chamber he drives black crossed spears into the floor. These mark values that make the denominator zero, so the response is pulled sharply there.

  • Denominator factor means pole.
  • If $$s=p_1$$ makes the denominator zero, plot a crossed spear at $$p_1.$$
  • Pole locations dominate behavior, stability, and waveform style.

Room 4: Twin Arches Split Into Conjugate Pairs

At the final chamber, a twin stone arch labeled $$s^2+\omega^2$$ cracks into two symmetric doors: one at $$+j\omega$$ and one at $$-j\omega.$$ Urahara says symmetry is the clue that prevents plotting only one point.

  • Quadratic terms often hide two points.
  • Pure imaginary pairs sit directly on the imaginary axis.
  • This room leads directly to Ichigo's first full plotting mission.
