Exercise 8-18

Find the Input Impedance and Cancel Its Reactance

This page is a reduction-and-cancellation movie. You reduce the network in Figure 8-27 to one rectangular impedance, convert it to polar form to read the size and angle cleanly, then choose one series element whose reactance kills the remaining imaginary lane. The memory emphasis is that the cancelling element must have equal magnitude and opposite sign.

$$Z=256+j195\,\Omega=322.7\angle37.3^\circ\,\Omega,\qquad C=0.513\,\mu ext{F}$$

Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Reduce the Figure to One Rectangular Impedance

  • Treat the network as a series-parallel reduction problem and collapse the branches to one equivalent input impedance.
  • For this figure, the reduced input is $$Z=256+j195\,\Omega.$$
  • The memory point is that all the internal structure is now packed into one real lane and one imaginary lane.

Room 2: Read the Meaning of the Two Lanes

  • The real part is $$256\,\Omega.$$
  • The imaginary part is positive, $$+j195\,\Omega,$$ so the input is net inductive.
  • That sign is the whole reason the cancelling element must be capacitive.

Room 3: Convert the Result to Polar Form

  • Use the magnitude formula $$|Z|=\sqrt{256^2+195^2}=322.7\,\Omega.$$
  • Use the angle formula $$\theta=\tan^{-1}(195/256)=37.3^\circ.$$
  • So $$Z=322.7\angle37.3^\circ\,\Omega.$$

Room 4: Choose the Series Element that Makes the Reactance Zero

  • To make the total reactance zero, add an equal and opposite reactance in series.
  • Since the network currently has $$+j195\,\Omega,$$ the new element must provide $$-j195\,\Omega.$$
  • A negative reactance means a capacitor.

Room 5: Convert the Needed Reactance into a Capacitor Value

  • Use $$X_C=\frac{1}{\omega C}$$ with $$\omega=10 ext{ krad/s}. $$
  • So $$C=\frac{1}{\omega X_C}=\frac{1}{(10^4)(195)}=0.513\times10^{-6}\text{ F}. $$
  • Therefore the needed series element is a capacitor of about $$0.513\,\mu ext{F}. $$

Key Shortcut

Reduce first, read the sign of the imaginary lane second, and choose the equal-and-opposite reactance third.

Why This Flow Matters

This page reinforces a powerful Chapter 8 habit: once the whole network is reduced to one complex impedance, the design decision often becomes a one-line sign check on the imaginary part.
