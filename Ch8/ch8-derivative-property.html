Derivative Property of Phasors

Differentiating a sinusoid in the time domain becomes a multiplication by $$j\omega$$ in phasor form. This is one of the key shortcuts that makes AC circuit analysis fast.

$$v(t)=\Re\{Ve^{j\omega t}\}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{dv}{dt}=\Re\{j\omega Ve^{j\omega t}\}$$
Quick Data
Multiplier
Differentiate by multiplying the phasor by $$j\omega.$$
Magnitude Change
The amplitude gets multiplied by $$\omega.$$
Angle Shift
The phase angle increases by $$90^\circ.$$
Why
Because $$d(e^{j\omega t})/dt=j\omega e^{j\omega t}.$$
Rooms

Room 1: Start From the Phasor Form

  • Write the sinusoid as $$v(t)=\Re\{Ve^{j\omega t}\}.$$
  • The phasor $$V$$ is constant with respect to time.

Room 2: Differentiate

  • Differentiate inside the real-part operator.
  • Then $$\frac{dv}{dt}=\Re\left\{V\frac{d}{dt}e^{j\omega t}\right\}=\Re\{j\omega Ve^{j\omega t}\}.$$

Room 3: Identify the New Phasor

  • Comparing with the standard phasor form, the derivative phasor is $$j\omega V.$$
  • Since $$j=e^{j90^\circ},$$ multiplying by $$j$$ rotates the phasor by $$+90^\circ.$$

Room 4: Read the Physical Meaning

  • The derivative has a magnitude multiplied by $$\omega.$$
  • The derivative leads the original waveform by $$90^\circ.$$
  • This is the same pattern behind capacitor and inductor impedance relations later in AC analysis.
