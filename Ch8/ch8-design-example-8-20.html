Design Example 8-20

Use the Thevenin Equivalent to Choose a Load

This page turns Thevenin into a design shortcut. First remove the load and find the Thevenin voltage and look-back impedance. Then put the load back as $$Z_L=R_L+jX_L$$ and force the divider equation to deliver the required output phasor.

$$V_T=35.35\angle-45^\circ\text{ V},\quad Z_T=500-j500~\Omega,\quad Z_L=235.3-j58.8~\Omega$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
What You Are Asked To Find
Given
Use the circuit of Fig. 8-42 in the phasor domain.
Find
Find the Thevenin equivalent seen by the load $$Z_L,$$ then choose $$Z_L$$ so that $$V_L=10\angle-90^\circ\text{ V}.$$
Design Pattern
Reduce the fixed network first, then design only the load.
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Open the Load and Find the Thevenin Voltage

  • With the load removed, the output node is set by a simple divider across the fixed network.
  • Using the open-circuit divider gives $$V_T=35.35\angle-45^\circ\text{ V}.$$
  • This becomes the source for the Thevenin model.

Why This Step

The load must not distort the Thevenin source while we are finding it. That is why we remove the load before computing the open-circuit voltage.

Room 2: Turn Off the Source and Look Back for $$Z_T$$

  • Deactivate the independent source and compute the impedance seen from the load terminals.
  • The look-back reduction gives $$Z_T=500-j500~\Omega.$$
  • Now the whole left side of the circuit has been compressed into one Thevenin pair.

Why This Step

Thevenin works because everything fixed in the network can be replaced by one source and one impedance as seen by the load.

Room 3: Write the Unknown Load as $$R_L+jX_L$$

  • Let $$Z_L=R_L+jX_L.$$
  • Use the voltage-divider relation $$V_L=\dfrac{Z_L}{Z_L+Z_T}V_T.$$
  • Substitute the target $$V_L=10\angle-90^\circ\text{ V}$$ and the known $$V_T$$ and $$Z_T.$$

Why This Step

Once the network has been reduced to Thevenin form, the design problem becomes a one-load divider problem instead of a messy full-circuit problem.

Room 4: Separate Real and Imaginary Parts

  • Cross-multiply the divider equation and expand in rectangular form.
  • Then match the real and imaginary parts as two simultaneous equations in $$R_L$$ and $$X_L.$$
  • Solving gives $$R_L=235.3~\Omega$$ and $$X_L=-58.8~\Omega.$$

Why This Step

Designing a complex load means solving for two unknowns, so the rectangular form is the cleanest place to separate the problem into two real equations.

Room 5: State the Final Load Clearly

  • The required load is $$Z_L=235.3-j58.8~\Omega.$$
  • This load makes the divider produce the demanded $$10\angle-90^\circ\text{ V}.$$
  • The whole design move depended on compressing the source network first.

Why This Step

This is the real memory target of the page: Thevenin is not only for analysis, it is a design shortcut for choosing a load that creates a required output.
