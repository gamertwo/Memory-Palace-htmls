Exercise 8-8

Find the Impedance of an Element

This page trains phasor division. Convert the voltage and current waveforms into phasors, divide to get the impedance in polar form, then if needed cut that polar result into rectangular form.

$$v(t)=50\cos(500t)\text{ V},\qquad i(t)=4\cos(500t-60^\circ)\text{ A}$$ $$\text{Find the impedance }Z\text{ of the element.}$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
Voltage Phasor
$$V=50\angle0^\circ$$
Current Phasor
$$I=4\angle-60^\circ$$
Core Rule
$$Z=\dfrac{V}{I},$$ so divide magnitudes and subtract angles.
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Convert the Waveforms to Phasors

  • The voltage waveform is $$v(t)=50\cos(500t),$$ so its phasor is $$V=50\angle0^\circ.$$
  • The current waveform is $$i(t)=4\cos(500t-60^\circ),$$ so its phasor is $$I=4\angle-60^\circ.$$
  • Both use the same $$\omega=500,$$ which is exactly what you want when comparing voltage and current of one element.

Room 2: Divide to Get Impedance in Polar Form

  • Use the impedance definition $$Z=\dfrac{V}{I}.$$ In polar form, division is especially clean.
  • Divide the magnitudes: $$50/4=12.5.$$
  • Subtract the angles: $$0^\circ-(-60^\circ)=60^\circ.$$
  • So $$Z=12.5\angle60^\circ\,\Omega.$$

Room 3: Cut the Polar Form into Rectangular Form

  • Use $$M\angle\theta=M(\cos\theta+j\sin\theta).$$
  • So $$Z=12.5(\cos60^\circ+j\sin60^\circ).$$
  • With $$\cos60^\circ=\frac12$$ and $$\sin60^\circ=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\approx0.866,$$ the real part is $$12.5(0.5)=6.25$$ and the imaginary part is $$12.5(0.866)=10.8.$$

Room 4: Write the Rectangular Answer

  • Therefore $$Z=6.25+j10.8\,\Omega.$$
  • The positive imaginary part tells you the element behaves inductively rather than capacitively.
  • So the answer may be boxed in either form: $$12.5\angle60^\circ\,\Omega$$ or $$6.25+j10.8\,\Omega.$$

Room 5: Lock the Interpretation

  • Because the voltage angle is ahead of the current angle by $$60^\circ,$$ the impedance angle is positive.
  • A positive impedance angle means the voltage leads the current, which is the signature of an inductive element.
  • So $$\boxed{Z=6.25+j10.8\,\Omega}$$ is the final rectangular answer.

Key Shortcut

For multiplication, add angles. For division, subtract angles. That one rule makes phasor impedance problems much faster.

Why The Angle Becomes Positive

The voltage phasor sits at $$0^\circ,$$ while the current phasor sits at $$-60^\circ.$$ When you divide $$V$$ by $$I,$$ you subtract the current angle, so the minus sign flips and the impedance angle becomes $$+60^\circ.$$

Why The Answer Looks Inductive

In rectangular form, a positive imaginary part means inductive behavior. If the imaginary part had been negative, the element would have looked capacitive instead.
