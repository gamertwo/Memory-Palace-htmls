Exercise 8-30

Determine the Thevenin Equivalent by Inspection

The textbook labels this one an inspection problem, but inspection only feels easy after the hidden logic becomes visible. This page spells out exactly what the shortcut is seeing in Figure 8-30: remove the capacitor, read the open-circuit divider voltage, turn off the source, and spot two identical series branches in parallel.

$$V_T=5\angle0^\circ\text{ V},\qquad Z_T=25+j50~\Omega$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
What You Are Asked To Find
Given
Use Figure 8-30 with $$V_S=10\angle0^\circ\text{ V}.$$ The capacitor branch is the load branch whose Thevenin equivalent is required.
Known Topology
From Example 8-15, the node where the capacitor connects sits between a left branch $$50+j100\ \Omega$$ and a right branch $$j100+50\ \Omega.$$
Goal
Find the Thevenin voltage and Thevenin impedance seen by the capacitor.
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Remove the Capacitor Because It Is the Load

  • The capacitor is the element connected at the interface of interest, so remove it first.
  • With the capacitor branch open, the left source and the remaining passive elements form one series path: $$(50+j100)\ \Omega\quad\text{followed by}\quad(j100+50)\ \Omega.$$
  • The terminal where the capacitor used to connect is the midpoint between those two series halves.

Why This Is the Correct Port

Thevenin is always built from the load's point of view. If we want the equivalent seen by the capacitor, then the capacitor must be removed and the rest of the circuit must be viewed from those exact terminals.

Room 2: Find the Open-Circuit Thevenin Voltage $$V_T$$

  • With the capacitor removed, the two remaining series sections are $$Z_{left}=50+j100,\qquad Z_{right}=50+j100.$$
  • Because they are equal, the source voltage divides equally between them.
  • The open-circuit voltage at the capacitor terminals is the voltage across the right-hand half, so $$V_T=V_S\frac{Z_{right}}{Z_{left}+Z_{right}}=10\angle0^\circ\frac{50+j100}{(50+j100)+(50+j100)}.$$

Why the Fraction Is Exactly One-Half

Since $$Z_{left}=Z_{right},$$ the numerator and one of the two equal denominator terms are the same. That makes the divider ratio $$\frac{Z_{right}}{Z_{left}+Z_{right}}=\frac{1}{2}. $$ So the midpoint voltage is exactly half the source voltage.

Final Thevenin Voltage

$$V_T=10\angle0^\circ\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=5\angle0^\circ\text{ V}. $$ The magnitude $$5$$ comes from halving the 10-V source, and the angle stays $$0^\circ$$ because the divider ratio is a positive real number with zero phase shift.

Room 3: Turn Off the Source Before Finding $$Z_T$$

  • To find Thevenin impedance, turn off the independent voltage source. A turned-off voltage source becomes a short circuit.
  • After that short is placed, the capacitor terminals see two branches to ground.
  • The left branch is the original left-side series impedance: $$50+j100~\Omega.$$
  • The right branch is the original right-side series impedance: $$j100+50=50+j100~\Omega.$$

Inspection Shortcut

At this point the port is looking into two identical branches in parallel. Once you notice that symmetry, the rest is immediate.

Room 4: Combine the Two Identical Branches in Parallel

  • The Thevenin impedance is $$Z_T=(50+j100)\parallel(50+j100).$$
  • When two equal impedances are in parallel, the result is half of either one:
  • $$Z_T=\frac{50+j100}{2}=25+j50~\Omega.$$

Where the Real Part $$25$$ Comes From

Each branch contributes $$50\ \Omega$$ of resistance. Two equal 50-ohm resistive parts in parallel reduce to $$25\ \Omega.$$

Where the Imaginary Part $$+50$$ Comes From

Each branch contributes $$+j100\ \Omega$$ of inductive reactance. Two equal inductive reactances in parallel halve in the same way, so $$j100\parallel j100=j50.$$

Room 5: State the Final Thevenin Equivalent and Explain Why Inspection Works

  • The Thevenin voltage seen by the capacitor is $$V_T=5\angle0^\circ\text{ V}. $$
  • The Thevenin impedance seen by the capacitor is $$Z_T=25+j50~\Omega. $$
  • The whole reason this can be done by inspection is that the circuit has obvious symmetry once the capacitor is removed and the source is turned off.

Compact Exam-Style Working

$$V_T=10\angle0^\circ\frac{50+j100}{(50+j100)+(50+j100)}=5\angle0^\circ\text{ V}$$ $$Z_T=(50+j100)\parallel(50+j100)=\frac{50+j100}{2}=25+j50~\Omega$$

What the Shortcut Is Really Doing

Inspection is not magic. It is just recognizing, without a long algebra run, that the open-circuit case is a 50-50 divider and the look-back case is two equal series branches in parallel.
