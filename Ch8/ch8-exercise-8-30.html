Exercise 8-30

Determine the Thevenin Equivalent by Inspection

This last page is a fast-pattern exercise. Instead of doing a long derivation, you are meant to recognize the terminal open-circuit voltage and the look-back impedance almost immediately from the circuit structure of Fig. 8-30.

$$V_T=5\angle0^\circ\text{ V},\qquad Z_T=25+j50~\Omega$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Rooms: Inspection Route

Room 1: Remove the Capacitor and Read the Terminal Voltage

  • The capacitor is the load whose Thevenin source is required.
  • With it removed, the terminal open-circuit voltage is read as $$V_T=5\angle0^\circ\text{ V}.$$
  • This is the Thevenin source voltage.

Why This Step

Thevenin voltage always comes from the open-circuit condition at the load terminals, even when the circuit is simple enough to read quickly by inspection.

Room 2: Turn Off the Source and Read the Look-Back Impedance

  • Deactivate the independent source and inspect the impedance seen from the capacitor terminals.
  • The resulting Thevenin impedance is $$Z_T=25+j50~\Omega.$$
  • This is the fixed impedance of the rest of the network as seen by the capacitor.

Why This Step

The inspection shortcut still follows the same logic as every other Thevenin page. It is just faster because the circuit reduction is visually obvious.

Room 3: Lock the Final Pair

  • The Thevenin equivalent seen by the capacitor is therefore
  • $$V_T=5\angle0^\circ\text{ V},\qquad Z_T=25+j50~\Omega.$$
  • This is the final pattern the exercise wants you to recognize quickly.

Why This Step

This page exists to build speed. Once the Thevenin route becomes familiar, some circuits should feel almost immediate instead of algebra-heavy.
