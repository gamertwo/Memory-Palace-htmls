Exercise 8-13

Find the Maxwell Bridge Balance Relations

This page is now a fuller algebra-palace route: catch the bridge arms, lock the balance equation, substitute the parallel RC model for the unknown arm, clear the denominator, then split the equation into real and imaginary lanes so the two final formulas fall out cleanly.

$$Z_1=R_1+j\omega L_1,\qquad Z_2=R_2,\qquad Z_3=R_3,\qquad Z_X=R_X\parallel\frac{1}{j\omega C_X}$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
Balance Law
Use the bridge balance condition so opposite-arm ratios match.
Unknown Arm
$$Z_X=\dfrac{R_X}{1+j\omega C_XR_X}$$
Answer
$$R_X=\dfrac{R_2R_3}{R_1},\qquad C_X=\dfrac{L_1}{R_2R_3}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Write the Bridge Balance Condition

  • For bridge balance, the opposite-arm ratios must match. A convenient form here is $$\dfrac{Z_1}{Z_2}=\dfrac{Z_3}{Z_X}.$$
  • Rearrange immediately into a multiplication form that is easier to memorize: $$Z_1Z_X=Z_2Z_3.$$

Room 2: Substitute the Arm Models

  • Use $$Z_1=R_1+j\omega L_1,\quad Z_2=R_2,\quad Z_3=R_3,\quad Z_X=\dfrac{R_X}{1+j\omega C_XR_X}.$$
  • Substituting into the balance equation gives $$\left(R_1+j\omega L_1\right)\frac{R_X}{1+j\omega C_XR_X}=R_2R_3.$$
  • At this point the whole puzzle is hidden in the denominator of the unknown arm, so the next move must clear it.

Room 3: Clear the Denominator

  • Multiply both sides by $$1+j\omega C_XR_X.$$
  • That produces $$R_X(R_1+j\omega L_1)=R_2R_3(1+j\omega C_XR_X).$$
  • This is the key balanced form because now both sides can be split into real and imaginary parts separately.

Room 4: Match Real Parts

  • On the left, the real part is $$R_XR_1.$$
  • On the right, the real part is $$R_2R_3.$$
  • So the real-part equation is $$R_XR_1=R_2R_3,$$ which gives $$\boxed{R_X=\dfrac{R_2R_3}{R_1}}.$$

Room 5: Match Imaginary Parts

  • On the left, the imaginary part is $$\omega L_1R_X.$$
  • On the right, the imaginary part is $$\omega C_XR_XR_2R_3.$$
  • Cancel the common factor $$\omega R_X,$$ giving $$L_1=C_XR_2R_3.$$
  • So the second balance formula is $$\boxed{C_X=\dfrac{L_1}{R_2R_3}}.$$

Key Shortcut

Bridge problems become much calmer once you clear the denominator and then compare real and imaginary parts separately. That is the whole movie.

Why The Split Works

Once the denominator is gone, the equation becomes one complex balance with a real lane and an imaginary lane. Matching those two lanes independently is what makes the unknown resistance and capacitance fall out cleanly.

Solution Flow

Walk this like a real algebra palace: catch the bridge arms, lock the balance law, push the unknown arm model into the equation, clear the denominator, then split the equation into a real lane and an imaginary lane so the two final formulas drop out one after the other.
