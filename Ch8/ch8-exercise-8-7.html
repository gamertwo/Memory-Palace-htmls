Exercise 8-7

Find Frequencies That Match Impedance Magnitudes

This page trains the impedance-comparison reflex. Instead of building full phasor sums, you write the magnitude formulas for the resistor, inductor, and capacitor, then solve the matching condition for $$\omega.$$ The last part reveals the resonance condition.

$$R=1\,\text{k}\Omega,\qquad C=1\,\mu\text{F},\qquad L=100\,\text{mH}$$ $$\text{Find the frequencies where }|Z_L|=|Z_R|,\; |Z_C|=|Z_R|,\; |Z_L|=|Z_C|.$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
Magnitude Rules
$$|Z_R|=R,\qquad |Z_L|=\omega L,\qquad |Z_C|=\frac{1}{\omega C}$$
Element Values
$$R=1000\,\Omega,\qquad L=0.1\text{ H},\qquad C=10^{-6}\text{ F}$$
Big Idea
Each part is just an equality of magnitudes. Solve that equality for $$\omega.$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Write the Impedance Magnitudes

  • The resistor magnitude is constant: $$|Z_R|=R=1000\,\Omega.$$
  • The inductor magnitude grows with frequency: $$|Z_L|=\omega L=\omega(0.1).$$
  • The capacitor magnitude shrinks with frequency: $$|Z_C|=\dfrac{1}{\omega C}=\dfrac{1}{\omega(10^{-6})}.$$
  • These three formulas are the whole engine of the problem.

Room 2: Part (a) Match Inductor to Resistor

  • Set the magnitudes equal: $$|Z_L|=|Z_R|.$$
  • So $$\omega L=R,$$ which becomes $$\omega(0.1)=1000.$$
  • Solve for $$\omega:$$ $$\omega=\frac{1000}{0.1}=10{,}000\text{ rad/s}=10\text{ krad/s}.$$
  • Therefore the answer to part (a) is $$\boxed{10\text{ krad/s}}.$$

Room 3: Part (b) Match Capacitor to Resistor

  • Now set $$|Z_C|=|Z_R|.$$
  • That gives $$\frac{1}{\omega C}=R,$$ or $$\frac{1}{\omega(10^{-6})}=1000.$$
  • Rearrange: $$1=1000\omega(10^{-6})=10^{-3}\omega,$$ so $$\omega=1000\text{ rad/s}=1\text{ krad/s}.$$
  • Therefore part (b) gives $$\boxed{1\text{ krad/s}}.$$

Room 4: Part (c) Match Inductor to Capacitor

  • Now set $$|Z_L|=|Z_C|.$$
  • That means $$\omega L=\frac{1}{\omega C}.$$ Multiply through by $$\omega C$$ to get $$\omega^2LC=1.$$
  • So $$\omega=\frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(0.1)(10^{-6})}}=3162\text{ rad/s}=3.162\text{ krad/s}.$$
  • Therefore the part (c) frequency is $$\boxed{3.162\text{ krad/s}}.$$

Room 5: Name the Special Frequency

  • The condition $$|Z_L|=|Z_C|$$ is the resonance condition for the series circuit.
  • So $$\boxed{\omega_0=3.162\text{ krad/s}}$$ is called the resonant frequency.
  • This is the frequency where the inductive and capacitive reactance magnitudes balance each other.

Key Shortcut

Do not memorize three separate worked solutions. Memorize the three magnitude formulas first. Then each part becomes a one-line equality and solve step.

Why Resonance Comes From $$|Z_L|=|Z_C|$$

The inductor reactance grows with $$\omega,$$ while the capacitor reactance falls with $$\omega.$$ The resonance frequency is exactly where those two magnitudes become equal. That crossing point is why the formula $$\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$$ matters so much in circuits.

How The Frequency Trends Help

If you increase frequency, $$|Z_L|$$ gets larger and $$|Z_C|$$ gets smaller. That is why the inductor matches the resistor at a high frequency here, while the capacitor matches the resistor at a lower one.
