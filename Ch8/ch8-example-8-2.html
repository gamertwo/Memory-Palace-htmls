Example 8-2

Balanced Three-Phasor Sum

This example is the chapter's balance pattern. Three equal-amplitude phasors, separated by $$120^\circ,$$ cancel exactly when you add them in rectangular form.

$$i_A(t)=5\cos(377t+50^\circ)\text{ A}\qquad i_B(t)=5\cos(377t+170^\circ)\text{ A}$$ $$i_C(t)=5\cos(377t-70^\circ)\text{ A}$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
Phasors
$$I_A=5\angle50^\circ,\quad I_B=5\angle170^\circ,\quad I_C=5\angle-70^\circ$$
Spacing
Each phasor is displaced by $$120^\circ,$$ which is the balanced three-phase pattern.
Goal
Show the phasor sum and waveform sum collapse to zero.
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Convert $$I_A$$

  • Start from the polar phasor $$I_A=5\angle50^\circ.$$ To move into rectangular form, use $$M\angle\theta=M(\cos\theta+j\sin\theta).$$
  • So $$I_A=5(\cos50^\circ+j\sin50^\circ).$$
  • Using $$\cos50^\circ\approx0.643$$ and $$\sin50^\circ\approx0.766,$$ the real part becomes $$5(0.643)=3.21$$ and the imaginary part becomes $$5(0.766)=3.83.$$
  • Therefore $$I_A=3.21+j3.83\text{ A}.$$

Room 2: Convert $$I_B$$

  • Now convert $$I_B=5\angle170^\circ$$ in the same way: $$I_B=5(\cos170^\circ+j\sin170^\circ).$$
  • At $$170^\circ,$$ the phasor is almost on the negative real axis, so you should expect a large negative real part and a small positive imaginary part.
  • Numerically, $$\cos170^\circ\approx-0.985$$ and $$\sin170^\circ\approx0.174.$$
  • That gives $$I_B=-4.92+j0.87\text{ A}.$$

Room 3: Convert $$I_C$$

  • For the third phasor, $$I_C=5\angle-70^\circ=5(\cos-70^\circ+j\sin-70^\circ).$$
  • Because the angle is negative, the arrow points below the real axis. So you should expect a positive real part and a negative imaginary part.
  • Using $$\cos-70^\circ\approx0.342$$ and $$\sin-70^\circ\approx-0.940,$$ you get $$I_C=1.71-j4.70\text{ A}.$$

Room 4: Add on the Mat

  • Add the three real parts together and the three imaginary parts together:
  • $$I_A+I_B+I_C=(3.21-4.92+1.71)+j(3.83+0.87-4.70).$$
  • The real components cancel to $$0,$$ and the imaginary components also cancel to $$0.$$
  • Therefore $$\boxed{I_A+I_B+I_C=0+j0\text{ A}}.$$
  • This cancellation is the rectangular proof that the three vectors close into a loop.

Room 5: Interpret the Balance

  • Equal magnitudes with $$120^\circ$$ spacing form a closed triangle in the phasor plane. No side is left over, so the vector sum is zero.
  • That geometric balance is exactly what the component arithmetic just confirmed.
  • So the current waveform sum is also zero: $$\boxed{i_A(t)+i_B(t)+i_C(t)=0.}$$

Key Shortcut

When you see equal magnitudes separated by $$120^\circ,$$ start expecting cancellation. The rectangular sum confirms it cleanly.

Why The Sum Is Zero

Each phasor has the same length, but they point in directions spaced evenly around the phasor plane. When converted to rectangular form, the positive and negative horizontal parts cancel, and the positive and negative vertical parts cancel too. The zero answer is not magic; it is the component form of a perfectly balanced three-phase picture.

What The $$120^\circ$$ Pattern Is Doing

Once one phasor is known, the other two are one-third of a turn away on either side. That symmetry means every component that points one way is matched by components from the other phasors pointing back. The rectangular arithmetic is simply the numerical shadow of that symmetry.
