Additive Property of Phasors

When multiple sinusoids share the same frequency, their phasors add directly. This page turns the proof into a short path you can replay fast during review.

$$v(t)=v_1(t)+v_2(t)+\cdots+v_N(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V=V_1+V_2+\cdots+V_N$$
Quick Data
Condition
All sinusoids must have the same angular frequency.
Best Form
Rectangular form makes phasor addition easiest.
Output
Convert the final sum back to polar for the final waveform.
Picture
It is just vector addition in the complex plane.
Rooms

Room 1: Write Every Signal With the Same Rotating Factor

  • For same-frequency signals, $$v_k(t)=\Re\{V_ke^{j\omega t}\}.$$
  • Each signal has its own phasor $$V_k$$ but the same factor $$e^{j\omega t}.$$

Room 2: Add the Waveforms

  • Take $$v(t)=\sum v_k(t).$$
  • Because the real-part operator is additive, $$v(t)=\Re\{(V_1+V_2+\cdots+V_N)e^{j\omega t}\}.$$

Room 3: Read the Resulting Phasor

  • Compare the result to the standard phasor form $$v(t)=\Re\{Ve^{j\omega t}\}.$$
  • Therefore the new phasor is $$V=V_1+V_2+\cdots+V_N.$$

Room 4: Practical Workflow

  • Convert each sinusoid to a phasor.
  • Change to rectangular form if needed.
  • Add real parts and imaginary parts.
  • Convert the sum to polar form and return to the time domain.
