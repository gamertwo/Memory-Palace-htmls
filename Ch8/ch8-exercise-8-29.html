Exercise 8-29

Find the Thevenin Circuit Seen by the Inductor and Then Find $$I_x$$

This is a complete remove-reduce-restore Thevenin problem. The inductor is treated as the load, the rest of the circuit is compressed into $$V_T$$ and $$Z_T,$$ and then the inductor is reattached so its current can be found from one final phasor division.

$$V_T=0.08925+j1.3839\text{ V},\quad Z_T=230.8-j153.8~\Omega,\quad I_x=5.852\angle99.44^\circ\text{ mA}$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
What You Are Asked To Find
Circuit Data
The source is $$V_s=5\angle120^\circ\text{ V}.$$ The fixed network connected to the inductor terminals consists of a $$1\text{ k}\Omega$$ resistor feeding a node with a $$500\ \Omega$$ resistor and a $$-j500\ \Omega$$ capacitor branch.
Find
Find the Thevenin equivalent seen by the inductor $$j100\ \Omega,$$ then use it to compute the inductor current $$I_x.$$
Pattern
Remove the load, find $$V_T,$$ turn off the source and find $$Z_T,$$ then put the load back and solve one reduced circuit.
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Remove the Inductor and Define the Thevenin Port

  • The inductor is the load of interest, so it must be removed first.
  • Once the inductor is open-circuited, the remaining network seen from its two terminals is what we must replace by a Thevenin equivalent.
  • With the inductor removed, the only branches remaining from the port node to ground are the $$500\ \Omega$$ resistor and the $$-j500\ \Omega$$ capacitor.

Why Removal Matters

Thevenin voltage is an open-circuit voltage. If the inductor stayed connected, its current would change the terminal voltage and we would no longer be measuring the source network by itself.

Room 2: Combine the Two Shunt Branches to Prepare for $$V_T$$

  • The open-circuit port node sees $$500\ \Omega$$ in parallel with $$-j500\ \Omega.$$ Call that equivalent impedance $$Z_p.$$
  • Use the parallel formula: $$Z_p=\frac{(500)(-j500)}{500-j500}. $$
  • Simplifying gives $$Z_p=250-j250~\Omega.$$

Where $$250-j250$$ Comes From

Because the two branch magnitudes are equal, their parallel combination lands halfway in both the real and imaginary directions. You can also verify it by admittance: $$\frac{1}{Z_p}=\frac{1}{500}+\frac{1}{-j500}=0.002+j0.002,$$ so inverting gives $$Z_p=250-j250~\Omega.$$

Room 3: Use Voltage Division to Find the Open-Circuit Thevenin Voltage

  • With the inductor removed, the source sees a series path made of the $$1\text{ k}\Omega$$ resistor and the equivalent shunt impedance $$Z_p=250-j250\ \Omega.$$
  • The open-circuit terminal voltage is the voltage across $$Z_p,$$ so $$V_T=V_s\frac{Z_p}{1000+Z_p}.$$
  • Substitute the known values: $$V_T=5\angle120^\circ\frac{250-j250}{1250-j250}. $$

Work the Divider Ratio First

$$\frac{250-j250}{1250-j250}=0.23077-j0.15385.$$ This ratio has magnitude $$\sqrt{0.23077^2+(-0.15385)^2}=0.27735$$ and angle $$\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{-0.15385}{0.23077}\right)=-33.69^\circ.$$

Now Apply the Source Magnitude and Phase

The source magnitude is $$5$$ and the divider magnitude is $$0.27735,$$ so $$|V_T|=5(0.27735)=1.38675\text{ V}. $$ The source angle is $$120^\circ$$ and the divider angle is $$-33.69^\circ,$$ so $$\angle V_T=120^\circ-33.69^\circ=86.31^\circ.$$ Therefore, $$V_T=1.38675\angle86.31^\circ\text{ V}. $$

Rectangular Form of $$V_T$$

$$V_T=1.38675(\cos86.31^\circ+j\sin86.31^\circ)=0.08925+j1.3839\text{ V}. $$ That is exactly the answer reported in the text.

Room 4: Turn Off the Source and Find the Look-Back Impedance $$Z_T$$

  • To find Thevenin impedance, deactivate the independent voltage source. A voltage source turned off becomes a short circuit.
  • That means the port node now sees three branches to ground in parallel: $$1000~\Omega,\quad 500~\Omega,\quad -j500~\Omega.$$
  • So $$\frac{1}{Z_T}=\frac{1}{1000}+\frac{1}{500}+\frac{1}{-j500}=0.001+0.002+j0.002=0.003+j0.002.$$

Invert the Admittance

$$Z_T=\frac{1}{0.003+j0.002}.$$ Multiply numerator and denominator by the complex conjugate: $$Z_T=\frac{0.003-j0.002}{(0.003)^2+(0.002)^2}=\frac{0.003-j0.002}{0.000013}.$$ Therefore, $$Z_T=230.8-j153.8~\Omega.$$

Meaning of the Signs

The real part $$230.8\ \Omega$$ is the equivalent resistance of the fixed network seen by the inductor. The negative imaginary part $$-153.8\ \Omega$$ means the fixed network looks net capacitive at the port.

Room 5: Reattach the Inductor as the Load

  • The load is the original inductor, so $$Z_L=j100~\Omega.$$
  • After Thevenin reduction, the inductor current is simply the load current of one source driving one series impedance path:
  • $$I_x=\frac{V_T}{Z_T+Z_L}. $$
  • First add the series impedances: $$Z_T+Z_L=(230.8-j153.8)+j100=230.8-j53.8~\Omega.$$

Why the Imaginary Part Changes from $$-153.8$$ to $$-53.8$$

The inductor contributes $$+j100\ \Omega.$$ It partially cancels the capacitive part of the Thevenin impedance, so the total reactance becomes less negative.

Room 6: Divide to Get the Inductor Current

  • Use the rectangular values directly: $$I_x=\frac{0.08925+j1.3839}{230.8-j53.8}. $$
  • It is easier to see the final magnitude and angle if we first convert the denominator to polar form.

Magnitude and Phase of the Denominator

$$|230.8-j53.8|=\sqrt{230.8^2+(-53.8)^2}=236.99\ \Omega$$ $$\angle(230.8-j53.8)=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{-53.8}{230.8}\right)=-13.12^\circ$$ So, $$Z_T+Z_L=236.99\angle-13.12^\circ~\Omega. $$

Magnitude and Phase of the Numerator

$$|V_T|=\sqrt{0.08925^2+1.3839^2}=1.38675\text{ V}$$ $$\angle V_T=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1.3839}{0.08925}\right)=86.31^\circ$$ so $$V_T=1.38675\angle86.31^\circ\text{ V}. $$

Final Current Magnitude and Phase

$$|I_x|=\frac{1.38675}{236.99}=0.005852\text{ A}=5.852\text{ mA}$$ $$\angle I_x=86.31^\circ-(-13.12^\circ)=99.43^\circ$$ Therefore, $$I_x=5.852\angle99.44^\circ\text{ mA}. $$

Room 7: State the Final Thevenin Pair and the Final Current

  • The open-circuit Thevenin voltage is $$V_T=0.08925+j1.3839\text{ V}. $$
  • The look-back Thevenin impedance is $$Z_T=230.8-j153.8~\Omega. $$
  • After reconnecting the inductor $$j100\ \Omega,$$ the load current is $$I_x=5.852\angle99.44^\circ\text{ mA}. $$

Compact Exam-Style Working

$$500\parallel(-j500)=250-j250$$ $$V_T=5\angle120^\circ\frac{250-j250}{1000+250-j250}=0.08925+j1.3839\text{ V}$$ $$Z_T=\left(\frac{1}{1000}+\frac{1}{500}+\frac{1}{-j500}\right)^{-1}=230.8-j153.8~\Omega$$ $$I_x=\frac{V_T}{Z_T+j100}=\frac{0.08925+j1.3839}{230.8-j53.8}=5.852\angle99.44^\circ\text{ mA}$$
