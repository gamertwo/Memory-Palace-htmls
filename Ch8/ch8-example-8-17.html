Example 8-17

Use Superposition When the Sources Operate at Different Frequencies

This page is the contrast partner to Example 8-16. You still turn off one source at a time and solve two reduced circuits, but now the two sources operate at different frequencies, so the responses cannot be added as phasors. The memory emphasis is the stopping rule: different frequencies break the common phasor floor, so the final answer must be written as a sum of time-domain sinusoids.

$$i(t)=2.23\cos(20{,}000t-21.8^\circ)+0.512\cos(60{,}000t-20.2^\circ)\text{ mA}$$

What You Are Asked To Find
Given
Use superposition on Figure 8-35 for $$R=10\text{ k}\Omega,\ L=200\text{ mH}.$$
Sources
$$v_{s1}=24\cos(20{,}000t)\text{ V},\qquad v_{s2}=8\cos(60{,}000t+30^\circ)\text{ V}$$
Find
Find the steady-state current $$i(t)$$ and validate with Multisim.
Quick Data
First Frequency
$$\omega_1=20{,}000,\qquad Z_{EQ1}=R+j\omega_1L=(10+j4)\text{ k}\Omega$$
Second Frequency
$$\omega_2=60{,}000,\qquad Z_{EQ2}=R+j\omega_2L=(10+j12)\text{ k}\Omega$$
Partial Currents
$$I_1=2.23\angle-21.8^\circ\text{ mA},\qquad I_2=0.512\angle-20.2^\circ\text{ mA}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Notice That the Two Sources Do Not Share a Frequency

  • The first source operates at $$\omega_1=20{,}000\text{ rad/s},$$ while the second operates at $$\omega_2=60{,}000\text{ rad/s}.$$
  • This destroys the common phasor floor, so the final answer cannot be one single phasor current.
  • Superposition still works, but the last step must happen in the time domain.

Room 2: Solve the Response to Source 1 Alone

  • Turn off source 2 and analyze the circuit at $$\omega_1=20{,}000.$$
  • The equivalent impedance is $$Z_{EQ1}=R+j\omega_1L=(10+j4)\text{ k}\Omega.$$
  • So the phasor current due to source 1 is $$I_1=\frac{V_{s1}}{Z_{EQ1}}=\frac{24\angle0^\circ}{10{,}000+j4000}=2.23\angle-21.8^\circ\text{ mA}.$$

Room 3: Solve the Response to Source 2 Alone

  • Turn off source 1 and analyze the same physical circuit at $$\omega_2=60{,}000.$$
  • The equivalent impedance is now $$Z_{EQ2}=R+j\omega_2L=(10+j12)\text{ k}\Omega.$$
  • So the phasor current due to source 2 is $$I_2=\frac{V_{s2}}{Z_{EQ2}}=\frac{8\angle30^\circ}{10{,}000+j12{,}000}=0.512\angle-20.2^\circ\text{ mA}.$$

Room 4: Refuse to Add the Phasors and Move Back to Time Domain

  • Do not add $$I_1$$ and $$I_2$$ as phasors. They belong to different frequencies, so that sum would be meaningless.
  • Instead write each phasor response back as its own sinusoid.
  • That gives $$i(t)=2.23\cos(20{,}000t-21.8^\circ)+0.512\cos(60{,}000t-20.2^\circ)\text{ mA}.$$

Room 5: Validate with Two Single-Frequency Multisim Runs

  • Multisim can still validate the answer, but it must be used in two separate single-frequency analyses.
  • Convert the angular frequencies to hertz: $$20{,}000\text{ rad/s}\to3183\text{ Hz},\qquad 60{,}000\text{ rad/s}\to9549\text{ Hz}.$$
  • The simulated magnitude-phase results match the hand values closely, which confirms the final time-domain sum.

Key Shortcut

Superposition still means one source at a time, but the final combination rule changes: same frequency means phasor addition, different frequencies mean time-domain addition.

Why This Flow Matters

This page protects you from one of the easiest Chapter 8 mistakes: adding phasors that should never be added. The stopping rule is just as important as the solving rule.
