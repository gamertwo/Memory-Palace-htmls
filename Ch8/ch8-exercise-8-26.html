Exercise 8-26

Convert a Norton Circuit into an Equivalent Thevenin Circuit

This page is the mirror of Example 8-19. The current source and parallel impedance are given, and you reverse the move by multiplying the Norton current by the Norton impedance. The impedance itself stays unchanged. The real work is understanding how the rectangular forms become polar forms and how that multiplication produces the final magnitude and angle.

$$V_T=70.71\angle-8.13^\circ\text{ V},\qquad Z_T=141.4\angle45^\circ~\Omega$$
Major Guide Open in Memory Builder
What You Are Asked To Find
Given
A Norton circuit with $$I_N=300-j400\text{ mA}$$ and $$Z_N=100+j100~\Omega.$$
Find
Find the equivalent Thevenin circuit.
Key Rule
Use $$V_T=I_N Z_N$$ and keep the same impedance.
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Reverse the Source-Transformation Rule

  • A Norton source becomes a Thevenin source by multiplication.
  • The source-conversion rule is: $$V_T=I_NZ_N$$
  • The impedance does not change during the conversion.
  • So once $$V_T$$ is found, the job is almost done.

What This Rule Means

This page is the exact reverse of Thevenin-to-Norton conversion. There, you divided voltage by impedance to get current. Here, you multiply current by impedance to get voltage.

Memory Pair

$$\text{Thevenin}\to\text{Norton}: \quad I_N=\frac{V_T}{Z_T}$$ $$\text{Norton}\to\text{Thevenin}: \quad V_T=I_NZ_N$$

Room 2: Read the Given Quantities Carefully

  • The given Norton current is in rectangular form: $$I_N=300-j400\text{ mA}$$
  • The given impedance is also in rectangular form: $$Z_N=100+j100~\Omega$$
  • Because multiplication is easiest in polar form, convert both quantities before multiplying.

Why Polar Form Is Better Here

In polar form, multiplication becomes:

multiply the magnitudes,
add the angles.

That is much cleaner than multiplying rectangular forms directly.

Room 3: Convert the Norton Current into Polar Form

  • Start from: $$I_N=300-j400\text{ mA}$$
  • Convert mA to A if you want standard units: $$300-j400\text{ mA}=0.3-j0.4\text{ A}$$
  • Now find the magnitude: $$|I_N|=\sqrt{0.3^2+(-0.4)^2}=\sqrt{0.09+0.16}=\sqrt{0.25}=0.5\text{ A}$$

Where Does the Angle -53.1° Come From?

Use: $$\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)$$ with $$a=0.3,\qquad b=-0.4$$ so: $$\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{-0.4}{0.3}\right)=\tan^{-1}(-1.333)\approx-53.1^\circ$$

Why the Angle Is Negative

The current has positive real part and negative imaginary part, so it lies below the real axis. That puts it in the fourth quadrant, which is why the angle is negative.

Final Polar Form of the Current

Therefore: $$I_N=0.5\angle-53.1^\circ\text{ A}$$

Room 4: Convert the Norton Impedance into Polar Form

  • Start from: $$Z_N=100+j100~\Omega$$
  • Find the magnitude: $$|Z_N|=\sqrt{100^2+100^2}=\sqrt{20000}=141.4~\Omega$$

Where Does 141.4 Come From?

It comes directly from the Pythagorean magnitude formula: $$|a+jb|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$$ so for $$100+j100,$$ the magnitude is $$\sqrt{100^2+100^2}=141.4.$$

Where Does the Angle 45° Come From?

Use: $$\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)$$ with $$a=100,\qquad b=100$$ so: $$\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{100}{100}\right)=\tan^{-1}(1)=45^\circ$$

Why the Angle Is Positive

Both the real and imaginary parts are positive, so the impedance lies in the first quadrant. That is why the angle is positive.

Final Polar Form of the Impedance

Therefore: $$Z_N=141.4\angle45^\circ~\Omega$$

Room 5: Multiply to Get the Thevenin Voltage

  • Now apply the conversion rule: $$V_T=I_NZ_N$$
  • Substitute the polar forms: $$V_T=(0.5\angle-53.1^\circ)(141.4\angle45^\circ)$$

What Happens During Polar Multiplication?

When multiplying in polar form: $$\left(A\angle\alpha\right)\left(B\angle\beta\right)=AB\angle(\alpha+\beta)$$ So there are two separate jobs:

Magnitude job: $$0.5\times141.4=70.7$$

Angle job: $$-53.1^\circ+45^\circ=-8.1^\circ$$

Where Does 70.71 Come From?

It comes only from multiplying the magnitudes: $$0.5\times141.4=70.7\approx70.71$$ So the final voltage magnitude is the product of the current magnitude and the impedance magnitude.

Where Does -8.13° Come From?

It comes only from adding the angles: $$-53.1^\circ+45^\circ=-8.1^\circ\approx-8.13^\circ$$ So the final voltage angle is just the current angle plus the impedance angle.

Room 6: Write the Thevenin Voltage Clearly

  • After multiplying magnitudes and adding angles: $$V_T=70.71\angle-8.13^\circ\text{ V}$$
  • This is the equivalent Thevenin source voltage.

What This Answer Is Saying

The Thevenin equivalent has a voltage source whose magnitude is $$70.71\text{ V}$$ and whose phase is $$-8.13^\circ.$$ Nothing mysterious happened. The number came from magnitude multiplication, and the angle came from angle addition.

Room 7: Keep the Same Impedance

  • The Thevenin impedance equals the Norton impedance.
  • So: $$Z_T=Z_N=100+j100~\Omega$$
  • Or in polar form: $$Z_T=141.4\angle45^\circ~\Omega$$

Why the Impedance Stays the Same

Source transformation changes only the source representation. It does not change the impedance seen from the load terminals. That is why the impedance is preserved exactly.

Room 8: State the Final Thevenin Circuit

  • The equivalent Thevenin source voltage is: $$V_T=70.71\angle-8.13^\circ\text{ V}$$
  • The equivalent Thevenin impedance is: $$Z_T=100+j100=141.4\angle45^\circ~\Omega$$
  • So the final Thevenin circuit is a voltage source in series with this same impedance.

Final Answer

$$V_T=70.71\angle-8.13^\circ\text{ V},\qquad Z_T=141.4\angle45^\circ~\Omega$$

Compact Exam-Style Working

$$I_N=300-j400\text{ mA}=0.3-j0.4\text{ A}=0.5\angle-53.1^\circ\text{ A}$$ $$Z_N=100+j100=141.4\angle45^\circ~\Omega$$ $$V_T=I_NZ_N=(0.5\angle-53.1^\circ)(141.4\angle45^\circ)$$ $$V_T=70.71\angle-8.13^\circ\text{ V}$$ $$Z_T=Z_N=141.4\angle45^\circ~\Omega$$
