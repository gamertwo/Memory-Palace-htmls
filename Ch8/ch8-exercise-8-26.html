Exercise 8-26

Convert a Norton Circuit into an Equivalent Thevenin Circuit

This page is the mirror of Example 8-19. The current source and parallel impedance are given, and you reverse the move by multiplying the Norton current by the Norton impedance. The impedance itself stays unchanged.

$$V_T=70.71\angle-8.13^\circ\text{ V},\qquad Z_T=141.4\angle45^\circ~\Omega$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
What You Are Asked To Find
Given
A Norton circuit with $$I_N=300-j400\text{ mA}$$ and $$Z_N=100+j100~\Omega.$$
Find
Find the equivalent Thevenin circuit.
Key Rule
Use $$V_T=I_N Z_N$$ and keep the same impedance.
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Reverse the Source-Transformation Rule

  • A Norton source becomes a Thevenin source through multiplication.
  • The new voltage is $$V_T=I_N Z_N.$$
  • The impedance stays the same during the conversion.

Why This Step

This keeps the two source-transformation directions paired in memory: divide to go Thevenin to Norton, multiply to go Norton to Thevenin.

Room 2: Put the Given Quantities into Convenient Form

  • The impedance $$100+j100$$ becomes $$141.4\angle45^\circ~\Omega.$$
  • The current $$300-j400\text{ mA}$$ has magnitude-angle form $$0.5\angle-53.1^\circ\text{ A}.$$
  • Now the multiplication becomes clean.

Why This Step

Complex multiplication is fastest in polar form. This is the exact companion move to the polar division used on the previous page.

Room 3: Multiply to Get the Thevenin Voltage

  • $$V_T=(0.5\angle-53.1^\circ)(141.4\angle45^\circ).$$
  • This gives $$V_T=70.71\angle-8.13^\circ\text{ V}.$$
  • That is the equivalent source voltage.

Why This Step

Once current and impedance are in polar form, the source voltage falls out directly from magnitude multiplication and angle addition.

Room 4: Keep the Same Impedance

  • The equivalent Thevenin impedance equals the Norton impedance.
  • So $$Z_T=Z_N=100+j100=141.4\angle45^\circ~\Omega.$$
  • The final Thevenin circuit is a voltage source in series with this impedance.

Why This Step

The source representation changes, but the port seen by the load must not change. That is why the impedance is preserved.
