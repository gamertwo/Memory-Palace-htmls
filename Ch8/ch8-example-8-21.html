Example 8-21

Use Source Transformations When Both Sources Share One Frequency

This example solves the same-frequency superposition idea in a different style. Instead of turning one source off at a time, you transform each series source into a Norton form, combine the parallel current sources, reduce the passive parallel network, and then recover the output voltage in one clean phasor step.

$$V_R=227\angle-47.9^\circ\text{ V},\qquad v_R(t)=227\cos(5000t-47.9^\circ)\text{ V}$$
Major Guide Open in Memory Builder
What You Are Asked To Find
Goal
Find the resistor voltage $$V_R$$ by transforming the sources and reducing the same-frequency phasor network.
Key Rule
Because both sources operate at the same angular frequency, they can be reduced into one final phasor answer.
Shortcut Idea
Transform both series sources into Norton sources, combine them, reduce the parallel network, then use $$V=IZ.$$
Quick Data
Source Currents
$$I_{eq1}=-j10\text{ A},\qquad I_{eq2}=2-j3.46\text{ A}$$
Combined Source
$$I_{eq}=I_{eq1}+I_{eq2}=2-j13.46=13.6\angle-81.5^\circ\text{ A}$$
Equivalent Impedance
$$Z_{eq}=16.6\angle33.7^\circ~\Omega$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Why Use Source Transformations Here?

  • The circuit has two sources, but both operate at the same frequency.
  • That means the whole network can remain in one phasor world.
  • Instead of turning one source off at a time, we choose a smarter reduction route.
  • Each series source is converted into a Norton source, so both sources become parallel current sources.

Why This Method Is Attractive

Parallel current sources add directly. So source transformation is not just a trick here; it reorganizes the circuit into an easier structure.

Big Comparison

Different-frequency case:
solve separately and add in time domain.

Same-frequency case:
keep everything in phasors and reduce to one final answer.

Room 2: Transform the Left Series Source into Norton Form

  • The left branch is converted from a voltage-source series form into a current-source parallel form.
  • The transformation rule is: $$I_{eq1}=\frac{V_{s1}}{Z_{s1}}$$
  • The book’s transformed result is: $$I_{eq1}=-j10\text{ A}$$

What -j10 Means

$$-j10$$ is already in rectangular phasor form. Its magnitude is: $$|-j10|=10$$ and its angle is: $$\angle(-j10)=-90^\circ$$ So: $$I_{eq1}=10\angle-90^\circ\text{ A}$$

Why the Angle Is -90°

A pure negative imaginary phasor lies straight downward on the complex plane. That is why its phase is exactly $$-90^\circ.$$

Room 3: Transform the Right Series Source into Norton Form

  • The right branch is transformed the same way: $$I_{eq2}=\frac{V_{s2}}{Z_{s2}}$$
  • The book gives: $$I_{eq2}=2-j3.46\text{ A}$$

Rectangular Meaning

The current has:
real part $$=2$$
imaginary part $$=-3.46$$

Convert It to Polar Form

Magnitude: $$|I_{eq2}|=\sqrt{2^2+(-3.46)^2}=\sqrt{4+11.97}\approx4$$ Angle: $$\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{-3.46}{2}\right)\approx-60^\circ$$ So: $$I_{eq2}\approx4\angle-60^\circ\text{ A}$$

Why This Helps

Even if the current is first given in rectangular form, it still represents an ordinary phasor. Rectangular form is helpful for addition, while polar form is helpful for interpretation.

Room 4: Combine the Parallel Current Sources

  • Now both sources are current sources in parallel, so they add directly.
  • Use: $$I_{eq}=I_{eq1}+I_{eq2}$$
  • Substitute the rectangular forms: $$I_{eq}=(-j10)+(2-j3.46)$$
  • So: $$I_{eq}=2-j13.46\text{ A}$$

Why Rectangular Form Is Used Here

In rectangular form, currents add component by component:

real parts add,
imaginary parts add.

That is why rectangular form is the easiest place to combine parallel phasor currents.

Now Convert the Combined Current to Polar Form

Magnitude: $$|I_{eq}|=\sqrt{2^2+(-13.46)^2}\approx13.6$$ Angle: $$\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{-13.46}{2}\right)\approx-81.5^\circ$$ Therefore: $$I_{eq}=13.6\angle-81.5^\circ\text{ A}$$

Where Did 13.6∠-81.5° Come From?

It is just the polar form of: $$2-j13.46$$ So:
$$13.6$$ came from Pythagorean magnitude,
and $$-81.5^\circ$$ came from the arctangent angle.

Room 5: Reduce the Four Passive Elements in Parallel

  • After transforming the sources, the passive branches are all in one parallel network.
  • That full parallel reduction gives: $$Z_{eq}=16.6\angle33.7^\circ~\Omega$$

What This Equivalent Means

Instead of carrying the resistor, capacitor, and inductive branches separately, we replace them with one equivalent impedance that behaves the same at the terminals.

Why the Angle Is Positive

A positive impedance angle means the equivalent network behaves overall like an inductive-leaning impedance at this frequency.

Structural Win

At this point the whole circuit has become:

one current source
in parallel with
one equivalent impedance.

Room 6: Recover the Output Voltage

  • The voltage across the equivalent impedance is the same as the resistor voltage $$V_R.$$
  • Use the phasor Ohm’s law relation: $$V_R=I_{eq}Z_{eq}$$
  • Substitute: $$V_R=(13.6\angle-81.5^\circ)(16.6\angle33.7^\circ)$$

Multiply Magnitudes

$$13.6\times16.6\approx225.8\approx227$$

Add Angles

$$-81.5^\circ+33.7^\circ=-47.8^\circ\approx-47.9^\circ$$

Final Phasor Voltage

$$V_R=227\angle-47.9^\circ\text{ V}$$

Where Did 227∠-47.9° Come From?

It came directly from multiplying the combined source current phasor by the equivalent impedance phasor:

magnitude from multiplication,
angle from addition.

Room 7: Convert the Final Phasor Back to Time Domain

  • The phasor output is: $$V_R=227\angle-47.9^\circ\text{ V}$$
  • The common source frequency is: $$\omega=5000\text{ rad/s}$$
  • So the time-domain waveform is: $$v_R(t)=227\cos(5000t-47.9^\circ)\text{ V}$$

Why One Final Cosine Is Allowed

Both sources share the same angular frequency. That means the whole circuit response also lives at that same one frequency. So one phasor answer is valid all the way to the end.

Big Rule

Same frequency ⇒ one final phasor.

Different frequencies ⇒ separate waveform terms.

Room 8: Compact Exam-Style Working

  • $$I_{eq1}=-j10\text{ A}$$
  • $$I_{eq2}=2-j3.46\text{ A}$$
  • $$I_{eq}=I_{eq1}+I_{eq2}=2-j13.46=13.6\angle-81.5^\circ\text{ A}$$
  • $$Z_{eq}=16.6\angle33.7^\circ~\Omega$$
  • $$V_R=I_{eq}Z_{eq}=(13.6\angle-81.5^\circ)(16.6\angle33.7^\circ)=227\angle-47.9^\circ\text{ V}$$
  • $$v_R(t)=227\cos(5000t-47.9^\circ)\text{ V}$$
