Example 8-22

Use Thevenin Theorem to Find the Bridge Current $$I_x$$

This page shows the classic bridge-circuit Thevenin move. First remove the bridge element and compute the open-circuit voltage between its terminals. Then turn off the source, find the look-back impedance, reconnect the element, and solve the resulting one-loop current.

$$V_T=23.0-j26.6\text{ V},\qquad Z_T=69.9-j17.8~\Omega,\qquad I_x=0.180\angle-113^\circ\text{ A}$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Remove the Bridge Impedance and Find $$V_T$$

  • Disconnect the $$j200~\Omega$$ branch so the bridge terminals become an open circuit.
  • Use voltage division on the left and right sides to get the terminal node voltages.
  • The open-circuit Thevenin voltage is $$V_T=V_A-V_B=23.0-j26.6\text{ V}.$$

Why This Step

Thevenin voltage is always an open-circuit terminal voltage. The bridge element must be removed first so it cannot affect the voltage being measured.

Room 2: Turn Off the Source and Look Back for $$Z_T$$

  • Deactivate the independent voltage source by replacing it with a short.
  • The remaining network is a series connection of two parallel combinations.
  • This gives $$Z_T=69.9-j17.8~\Omega.$$

Why This Step

Thevenin impedance is a passive look-back quantity. That is why the independent source is turned off before the impedance reduction is done.

Room 3: Reconnect the Bridge Branch as a Load

  • Put the $$j200~\Omega$$ branch back across the Thevenin terminals.
  • The current of interest is now just the load current of a reduced Thevenin circuit.
  • Use $$I_x=\dfrac{V_T}{Z_T+j200}. $$

Why This Step

This is the whole reason for the reduction. The original bridge is awkward, but the Thevenin version turns the problem into one source driving one series path.

Room 4: Compute the Final Current

  • Substituting the Thevenin quantities gives
  • $$I_x=\frac{23.0-j26.6}{69.9+j182}=0.180\angle-113^\circ\text{ A}. $$
  • This is the bridge current through the reconnected element.

Why This Step

All the complexity of the bridge has been compressed into one final division. That is the clean payoff of the Thevenin method.
