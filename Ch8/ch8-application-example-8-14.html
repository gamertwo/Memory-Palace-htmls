Application Example 8-14

Build the RC Low-Pass Filter Ratio as a Memory Movie

This page is a divider-ratio filter movie. You write the output-to-input phasor ratio, simplify it into a clean transfer function, take its magnitude, inspect the key frequency landmarks, and then interpret why low frequencies pass while high frequencies shrink. The memory emphasis is on the cutoff idea: the filter bends around one special frequency.

$$left|\frac{V_C}{V_S}\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}},\qquad \omega_c=\frac{1}{RC}$$

What You Are Asked To Find
Given
Use the RC divider of Figure 8-29 with the output taken across the capacitor.
Find
Find the ratio $V_C/V_S$ and then find its magnitude as a function of frequency.
Target
You are also asked to explain the filter behavior and the cutoff idea.
Quick Data
Divider Ratio
$$\frac{V_C}{V_S}=\frac{1}{1+j\omega RC}$$
Magnitude
$$\left|\frac{V_C}{V_S}\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$$
Cutoff
$$\omega_c=1/RC$$ where the magnitude is $$1/\sqrt2$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Write the Voltage Divider for the Capacitor Output

  • The output is taken across the capacitor, so use the divider relation $$V_C=\frac{1/j\omega C}{R+1/j\omega C}V_S.$$
  • Multiplying numerator and denominator by $$j\omega C$$ simplifies the ratio to $$\frac{V_C}{V_S}=\frac{1}{1+j\omega RC}. $$
  • This compact ratio is the main formula to memorize.

Room 2: Take the Magnitude of the Ratio

  • To study filtering, take the magnitude of the transfer ratio.
  • That gives $$\left|\frac{V_C}{V_S}\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}.$$
  • This formula is the passband picture in one line.

Room 3: Check the Landmark Frequencies

  • At $$\omega=0,$$ the magnitude is $$1,$$ so low-frequency signals pass almost unchanged.
  • At $$\omega=1/RC,$$ the magnitude is $$1/\sqrt2approx0.707.$$
  • At very high frequency, the magnitude approaches $$0.$$
  • These three landmarks tell the whole story of the curve.

Room 4: Interpret the Filter Behavior

  • As frequency rises, the capacitor impedance drops.
  • That means less of the source voltage appears across the capacitor itself.
  • So low frequencies pass, but high frequencies are attenuated.
  • This is why the circuit is called a low-pass filter.

Room 5: Lock the Cutoff Memory

  • The boundary between the easy-pass and strong-attenuation regions is the cutoff frequency.
  • For this RC low-pass filter, $$\omega_c=1/RC.$$
  • At cutoff, the output magnitude is down to about $$70.7%$$ of the input magnitude.

Key Shortcut

For the RC low-pass filter, memorize the clean chain: divider ratio, magnitude, landmarks, interpretation, cutoff.
