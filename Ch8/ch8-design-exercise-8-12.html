Design Exercise 8-12

Design a Load with Standard Parts

This page turns one target impedance into a parts-selection movie. First confirm the polar load and frequency, cut the load into rectangular form, interpret the negative imaginary part as a capacitor, solve the exact resistor and capacitor values, then round to the nearest standard values without losing the logic of the target.

$$\omega=100\text{ krad/s},\qquad Z_L=1500\angle-57.5^\circ\,\Omega$$

Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Confirm the Target Load and Frequency

  • The desired load is $$Z_L=1500\angle-57.5^\circ\,\Omega.$$
  • The negative angle already tells you the load must look capacitive, because its imaginary part must come out negative.
  • The operating frequency is fixed at $$\omega=100000\text{ rad/s}.$$ You need that value later when the reactance is converted into a capacitor.

Room 2: Cut the Polar Load into Rectangular Form

  • Use $$Z=|Z|(\cos\theta+j\sin\theta).$$
  • The real part becomes $$R=1500\cos57.5^\circ\approx806\,\Omega.$$
  • The imaginary part becomes $$X=1500\sin(-57.5^\circ)\approx-1265\,\Omega.$$
  • So the target load in rectangular form is approximately $$806-j1265\,\Omega.$$ This is the key design split: resistor part plus capacitive reactance part.

Room 3: Interpret the Negative Imaginary Part as a Capacitor

  • A negative reactance means a capacitor, so match the imaginary part to $$X_C=-\frac{1}{\omega C}.$$
  • That gives $$-\frac{1}{\omega C}=-1265,$$ so $$\frac{1}{\omega C}=1265.$$
  • Substitute $$\omega=100000$$ and solve: $$C=\frac{1}{(100000)(1265)}\approx7.91\times10^{-9}\text{ F}=7910\text{ pF}.$$

Room 4: State the Exact Design

  • The real part has already pulled out the resistor value: $$R\approx806\,\Omega.$$
  • The negative imaginary part has been converted into the capacitor value: $$C\approx7910\text{ pF}.$$
  • So the exact design target is an $$806\,\Omega$$ resistor in series with a $$7910\text{ pF}$$ capacitor.

Room 5: Choose Nearby Standard Parts

  • Real hardware uses standard values, so now round to practical nearby parts.
  • A good standard choice is about $$820\,\Omega$$ and $$8200\text{ pF}.$$
  • This keeps the realized load close to the target while using values you can actually buy and build with.

Key Shortcut

Design-to-parts problems start by cutting the target impedance into rectangular form. The real part becomes the resistor. The negative imaginary part becomes the capacitor reactance.

Why The Angle Matters So Much

The angle tells you the sign of the imaginary part before you calculate anything. A negative angle warns you that the reactance must be capacitive, so the movie already knows which component formula to use before the arithmetic begins.
