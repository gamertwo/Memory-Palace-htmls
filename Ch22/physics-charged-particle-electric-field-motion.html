Motion of a Charged Particle in an Electric Field

This sample problem treats the drop like horizontal motion plus vertical acceleration. The electric field gives the vertical force, so the deflection is found with basic kinematics after the travel time between the plates is computed.

$$y=\frac{QEL^2}{2mv_x^2}=6.4\times10^{-4}\text{ m}=0.64\text{ mm}$$
Given Data
Mass
$$m=1.3\times10^{-10}\,\text{kg}$$
Charge Magnitude
$$Q=1.5\times10^{-13}\,\text{C}$$
Plate Length
$$L=1.6\,\text{cm}=1.6\times10^{-2}\,\text{m}$$
Horizontal Speed
$$v_x=18\,\text{m/s}$$
Electric Field
$$E=1.4\times10^6\,\text{N/C}$$
Ignore
Gravitational force is negligible.
Rooms

Room 1: Use the Electric Force

  • The vertical force comes from the electric field:
  • $$F=QE.$$
  • So the vertical acceleration is
  • $$a_y=\frac{F}{m}=\frac{QE}{m}.$$

Room 2: Find the Time Between the Plates

  • The particle moves horizontally with constant speed.
  • So
  • $$L=v_xt \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad t=\frac{L}{v_x}.$$

Room 3: Use Vertical Kinematics

  • The initial vertical velocity is zero.
  • So the vertical deflection is
  • $$y=\frac{1}{2}a_yt^2.$$

Room 4: Substitute $a_y$ and $t$

  • Insert $$a_y=\frac{QE}{m}$$ and $$t=\frac{L}{v_x}:$$
  • $$y=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{QE}{m}\right)\left(\frac{L}{v_x}\right)^2.$$
  • So
  • $$y=\frac{QEL^2}{2mv_x^2}.$$

Room 5: Insert the Numerical Values

  • $$y=\frac{(1.5\times10^{-13})(1.4\times10^6)(1.6\times10^{-2})^2}{2(1.3\times10^{-10})(18)^2}.$$

Room 6: Final Answer

  • Evaluating gives
  • $$y=6.4\times10^{-4}\,\text{m}.$$
  • Therefore
  • $$\boxed{y=0.64\,\text{mm}}.$$
Key Physics Shortcut

Split the Motion

The easiest way to remember this is to split the problem into two independent parts: horizontal motion only gives the travel time, and vertical motion only gives the deflection. The electric field affects the vertical direction, not the horizontal speed.
