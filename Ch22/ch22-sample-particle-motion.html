Worked Example

Charged Particle Motion in an Electric Field

This example shows how Chapter 22 field formulas become kinematics. A particle with mass $$m=1.3\times10^{-10}\text{ kg}$$ and charge magnitude $$Q=1.5\times10^{-13}\text{ C}$$ enters a region of uniform electric field $$E=1.4\times10^6\text{ N/C}$$ with horizontal speed $$v_x=18\text{ m/s}.$$ The plate length is $$L=1.6\text{ cm}.$$ Find the vertical deflection.

$$y=\frac{QEL^2}{2mv_x^2}=6.4\times10^{-4}\text{ m}=0.64\text{ mm}$$

Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Turn Field into Force

  • The electric field exerts force $$F=QE.$$
  • That force acts vertically while the horizontal motion stays uniform.
  • So the field changes only the vertical motion here.

Why This Step

This is the bridge from electrostatics to mechanics. We first convert the field into an actual force because acceleration comes from force, not directly from field.

Room 2: Convert Force into Vertical Acceleration

  • Using Newton's second law, $$a_y=F/m.$$
  • So $$a_y=QE/m.$$
  • The vertical acceleration is constant because the field is uniform.

Why This Step

Uniform field means uniform force, which means constant acceleration. That lets us use simple constant-acceleration kinematics instead of something more complicated.

Room 3: Use the Horizontal Motion Only to Find Time

  • The particle crosses the plate region with constant horizontal speed.
  • So $$t=L/v_x.$$
  • This gives the time during which the field has a chance to deflect the particle.

Why This Step

The vertical deflection depends on how long the force acts. Horizontal motion is not there to confuse us; it is there to tell us the interaction time.

Room 4: Use Vertical Kinematics

  • The initial vertical velocity is zero.
  • So the deflection is $$y=\frac{1}{2}a_y t^2.$$
  • Substituting gives $$y=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{QE}{m}\right)\left(\frac{L}{v_x}\right)^2.$$

Why This Step

Once acceleration and time are known, ordinary kinematics finishes the problem. This is where the electric-field part and the motion part finally lock together.

Room 5: Compress to One Final Formula and Evaluate

  • The compressed result is $$y=\dfrac{QEL^2}{2mv_x^2}.$$
  • Substituting the data gives $$y=6.4\times10^{-4}\text{ m}.$$
  • So the final deflection is $$0.64\text{ mm}.$$

Why This Step

This compressed expression is worth remembering because it summarizes the whole logic chain in one line: more charge, more field, or more travel distance means more deflection; more mass or speed means less.
