Worked Example

Field of a Uniformly Charged Rod at an Off-End Point

This sample problem carries the full Chapter 22 method on one finite rod. A rod of length $$L=8.15\text{ cm}$$ carries total charge $$Q_{\text{rod}}=-4.23\text{ fC}$$ uniformly. Find the field at a point located $$a=12.0\text{ cm}$$ from the rod end, then compare the exact result with the far-field point-charge form.

$$E_x=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\lambda\left(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{a+L}\right)=-1.57\times10^{-3}\text{ N/C}$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Given Data
Rod Length
$$L=8.15\text{ cm}=0.0815\text{ m}$$
Total Charge
$$Q_{\text{rod}}=-4.23\times10^{-15}\text{ C}$$
Point Distance
$$a=0.120\text{ m}$$ from the rod end
Density
$$\lambda=Q_{\text{rod}}/L=-5.19\times10^{-14}\text{ C/m}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Convert Total Charge into Linear Density

  • The rod is uniform, so its charge density is constant.
  • $$\lambda=\frac{Q_{\text{rod}}}{L}=\frac{-4.23\times10^{-15}}{0.0815}=-5.19\times10^{-14}\text{ C/m}.$$
  • This density is negative, so the field will point toward the rod.

Why This Step

The rod is a continuous source, so we need a charge-per-length description before writing any differential element. This is the bridge from given data to a usable integral.

Room 2: Set Coordinates and Write One Differential Charge

  • Let the rod run from $$x=0$$ to $$x=L,$$ and place the observation point at $$x=L+a.$$
  • One small rod piece has charge $$dq=\lambda\,dx.$$
  • Its distance to the observation point is $$L+a-x.$$

Why This Step

Coordinates turn the picture into algebra. Once the distance from one source piece to the field point is known, the rest is standard Coulomb-law setup.

Room 3: Write the Differential Field

  • All contributions lie along the x-axis, so no component split is needed.
  • $$dE_x=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\lambda\,dx}{(L+a-x)^2}.$$
  • The negative sign of $$\lambda$$ will automatically make the final direction point toward the rod.

Why This Step

This rod geometry is simpler than a ring or disk because every tiny field already points along the same axis. That means the vector problem collapses immediately into one scalar integral.

Room 4: Integrate Over the Whole Rod

  • $$E_x=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\lambda\int_0^L \frac{dx}{(L+a-x)^2}.$$
  • Evaluating gives $$E_x=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\lambda\left(\dfrac{1}{a}-\dfrac{1}{a+L}\right).$$
  • This is the exact finite-rod field at the off-end point.

Why This Step

The integral adds the effect of every rod piece. Because the geometry is one-dimensional, the integration is straightforward once the setup is correct.

Room 5: Insert the Numbers and Interpret the Sign

  • Substituting the data gives $$E_x=-1.57\times10^{-3}\text{ N/C}.$$
  • The magnitude is $$1.57\times10^{-3}\text{ N/C}.$$
  • The negative sign means the field points toward the negatively charged rod.

Why This Step

The sign is not just algebra clutter. It gives the actual field direction along the x-axis, which is part of the physics answer.

Room 6: Check the Far-Field Limit

  • When $$a\gg L,$$ the factor $$a+L$$ is approximately $$a.$$
  • The exact result then reduces to $$E\approx \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{Q_{\text{rod}}}{a^2}.$$
  • So a distant finite rod behaves like a point charge carrying the same total charge.

Why This Step

This limit check connects the exact rod result to a simpler physical picture. Far away, the observation point can no longer resolve the rod's length, only its total charge.
