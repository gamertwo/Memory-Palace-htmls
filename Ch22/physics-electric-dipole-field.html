The Electric Field Due to an Electric Dipole

This page fills the chapter concept that is separate from torque-on-a-dipole. Here the dipole itself is the source, and we derive the field on the dipole axis before taking the far-field limit and rewriting the result with dipole moment.

$$E=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac{q}{(z-d/2)^2}-\frac{q}{(z+d/2)^2}\right],\qquad E\approx\frac{1}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{p}{z^3}$$
Core Definitions
Dipole
Two charges $$+q$$ and $$-q$$ separated by distance $$d.$$
Axis Point
Observation point lies on the dipole axis a distance $$z$$ from the center.
Moment
Dipole moment magnitude is $$p=qd.$$
Far Field
Use $$d/(2z)\ll1$$ when the point is far from the dipole size.
Rooms

Room 1: Place the Point on the Axis

  • Put the dipole center on a straight axis and place the observation point a distance $$z$$ away.
  • The two source charges are not equally far from that point.
  • So their field magnitudes differ.

Room 2: Write the Two Distances

  • The near and far distances are
  • $$r_+=z-\frac d2,\qquad r_-=z+\frac d2.$$
  • These distances control the two inverse-square terms.

Room 3: Subtract the Two Contributions

  • At the observation point the two fields lie along one line.
  • So the exact field is the difference
  • $$E=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac{q}{(z-d/2)^2}-\frac{q}{(z+d/2)^2}\right].$$

Room 4: Expose the Small Ratio

  • Factor out $$q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 z^2).$$
  • The exact expression now contains the small ratio $$d/(2z).$$
  • That prepares the far-field limit cleanly.

Room 5: Use the Far-Field Limit

  • When $$d/(2z)\ll1,$$ the dipole looks tiny compared with the observation distance.
  • The exact field simplifies to
  • $$E\approx\frac{1}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{qd}{z^3}.$$

Room 6: Replace $$qd$$ by Dipole Moment

  • Use the definition $$p=qd.$$
  • The far-axis field becomes
  • $$\boxed{E\approx\frac{1}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{p}{z^3}}.$$

Room 7: Keep the Main Comparison

  • A point-charge field falls as $$1/r^2.$$
  • A dipole field falls faster, as $$1/r^3$$ in the far region.
  • That faster decay is the physical signature of a dipole source.
Detailed Notes

Why the Field Does Not Cancel Completely

The two charges are equal and opposite, but the observation point is closer to one than the other. Because the electric field follows an inverse-square law, the nearer contribution wins slightly and a nonzero net field survives.

Do Not Confuse Two Different Dipole Pages

This page is about the field produced by a dipole. The separate dipole-in-an-electric-field page is about an external field acting on the dipole and producing torque and potential energy.
