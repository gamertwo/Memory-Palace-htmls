Point Charge Field and Superposition

This page adds the chapter's first explicit field formula and the next key move after it: if several charges are present, the total field is the vector sum of the individual fields.

$$\vec E=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}\,\hat r,\qquad \vec E_{\text{net}}=\vec E_1+\vec E_2+\cdots+\vec E_n$$
Quick Facts
Source Law
Point-charge field falls off as $$1/r^2.$$
Direction
Away from positive charges and toward negative charges.
Constant
Use $$k=1/(4\pi\varepsilon_0).$$
Net Field
Add fields as vectors, not just magnitudes.
Rooms

Room 1: Begin with Coulomb's Force

  • A source charge $$q$$ exerts force on a test charge $$q_0$$ at distance $$r.$$
  • The force points along the line connecting the two charges.
  • That is the starting point for the field formula.

Room 2: Convert Force to Field

  • Divide by the test charge using $$\vec E=\vec F/q_0.$$
  • The point-charge field becomes
  • $$\boxed{\vec E=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}\,\hat r}.$$

Room 3: Let the Sign Decide the Arrow

  • If $$q>0$$, the field arrow points outward from the source.
  • If $$q<0$$, the field arrow points inward toward the source.
  • The magnitude remains $$k|q|/r^2.$$

Room 4: Multiple Charges Mean Multiple Fields

  • At any observation point, each source charge contributes its own field vector.
  • The point does not choose one source. It feels all contributions.
  • So the total field must combine every vector.

Room 5: State Superposition Explicitly

  • $$\boxed{\vec E_{\text{net}}=\vec E_1+\vec E_2+\cdots+\vec E_n}.$$
  • This is vector addition, not scalar addition.
  • Draw arrows first, then resolve into components if needed.

Room 6: Use the Sample-Problem Strategy

  • Find each field magnitude from $$k|q|/d^2.$$
  • Use geometry to assign directions at the observation point.
  • Then add x-components with x-components and y-components with y-components.

Room 7: Symmetry Can Do Half the Work

  • Equal-angle components often cancel automatically.
  • The remaining components add to produce the net field.
  • Always check symmetry before long algebra.
Detailed Notes

Direction Before Components

Do not attach signs to magnitudes too early. First decide the physical direction of each field at the observation point. After that, project those arrows onto axes and add signed components.

Distance Scaling

The inverse-square law is the main pattern here. If distance doubles, the field falls by a factor of four. That fact alone solves many comparison questions quickly.

Memory Palace Path

Force first, divide to get the field of one point charge, then stack many such fields by superposition.

Station 1

A point charge throws a radial force arrow toward a test charge across distance $$r.$$

Trigger

The test charge is only a probe, so divide it away.

Station 2

The force arrow collapses into a clean $$1/r^2$$ field arrow.

Trigger

The sign of the source flips the arrow outward or inward.

Station 3

Positive charges spray arrows outward while negative charges pull them inward.

Trigger

Then more charges enter the room, so one arrow is no longer enough.

Station 4

Many field arrows meet at one point and stack head-to-tail as vectors.

Trigger

You split them into components and let symmetry erase the pairs that cancel.

Station 5

The surviving components join into one final net field arrow.
