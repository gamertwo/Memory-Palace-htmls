Worked Example

Superposition and Symmetry

This page trains the field-addition movie on a simple symmetric example. Two equal positive charges sit at $$x=\pm 0.40\text{ m}$$ and point $$P$$ lies at $$y=0.30\text{ m}$$ on the vertical axis. The field from each charge is not vertical by itself, but symmetry kills the horizontal components and lets the vertical pieces add.

$$E_{\text{net}}=2\left(\frac{kq}{r^2}\right)\frac{y}{r}=1.30\times10^5\text{ N/C upward}$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Given Data
Source Charges
$$q_1=q_2=+3.0\,\mu\text{C}$$
Locations
$$q_1=(-0.40,0),\ q_2=(+0.40,0)\text{ m}$$
Observation Point
$$P=(0,0.30)\text{ m}$$
Distance to Each Charge
$$r=\sqrt{0.40^2+0.30^2}=0.50\text{ m}$$
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Draw the Geometry Before Writing Numbers

  • Point $$P$$ is directly above the midpoint of two equal positive charges.
  • So the two source-to-point triangles are mirror images of each other.
  • That symmetry tells us the two field magnitudes will match.

Why This Step

Superposition becomes much easier when we let geometry speak first. The picture tells us where cancellation is possible before we calculate any field components.

Room 2: Find the Field from One Charge

  • For one source charge, $$E_1=\dfrac{kq}{r^2}.$$
  • Using $$k=8.99\times10^9,$$ $$q=3.0\times10^{-6}\text{ C},$$ and $$r=0.50\text{ m},$$
  • $$E_1=\frac{(8.99\times10^9)(3.0\times10^{-6})}{0.50^2}=1.08\times10^5\text{ N/C}.$$

Why This Step

We solve one charge first because superposition says the total field is built from individual contributions. The symmetry will later let us reuse this same value for the second charge.

Room 3: Split Each Field into Components

  • The vertical fraction of each field is $$y/r=0.30/0.50=0.60.$$
  • So each vertical component is $$E_{1y}=E_1(y/r)=1.08\times10^5(0.60)=6.47\times10^4\text{ N/C}.$$
  • The horizontal components have equal size but opposite directions.

Why This Step

Each individual field points along a slanted line from source to observation point, not straight up. We split into components so symmetry can remove the useless part cleanly.

Room 4: Let Symmetry Cancel the x-Components

  • The left charge pushes rightward and upward at $$P.$$
  • The right charge pushes leftward and upward at $$P.$$
  • The x-components cancel, while the y-components add.

Why This Step

This is the payoff for doing the geometry first. Symmetry removes half the work and shows that the final answer must lie along the vertical axis.

Room 5: Add the Surviving Components

  • $$E_{\text{net}}=2E_{1y}=2(6.47\times10^4)\text{ N/C}.$$
  • So $$E_{\text{net}}=1.30\times10^5\text{ N/C}.$$
  • The direction is upward because both surviving components point upward.

Why This Step

Superposition means vector addition, not magnitude addition. Once symmetry shows the surviving direction, the total field becomes a clean sum in that one direction.
