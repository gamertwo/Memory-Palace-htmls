The Electric Field Due to a Charged Disk

This page now uses the same rooms-and-memory-palace structure as the main charged-disk page. It builds the disk from concentric rings, integrates from the center to the edge, and keeps the infinite-sheet limit as the last anchor.

$$E=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\left(1-\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2+R^2}}\right)$$
Rooms

Room 1: Slice the Disk into Rings

  • Treat the disk as many thin concentric rings.
  • A ring of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$ has area
  • $$dA=2\pi r\,dr.$$
  • So the charge on that ring is
  • $$dq=\sigma\,dA=\sigma(2\pi r\,dr).$$

Room 2: Use the Ring Result for One Thin Ring

  • The field on the axis due to a ring is
  • $$dE=\frac{z\,dq}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(z^2+r^2)^{3/2}}.$$
  • Substitute $$dq=\sigma(2\pi r\,dr):$$
  • $$dE=\frac{z\sigma\,2\pi r\,dr}{4\pi\varepsilon_0(z^2+r^2)^{3/2}}.$$

Room 3: Simplify the Differential Field

  • Cancel the factor of $2\pi$ with $4\pi$:
  • $$dE=\frac{\sigma z}{4\varepsilon_0}\frac{2r\,dr}{(z^2+r^2)^{3/2}}.$$

Room 4: Integrate from $r=0$ to $r=R$

  • Add all ring contributions:
  • $$E=\int dE=\frac{\sigma z}{4\varepsilon_0}\int_0^R (z^2+r^2)^{-3/2}(2r)\,dr.$$

Room 5: Use the Power Integral

  • Let $$X=z^2+r^2,$$ so $$dX=2r\,dr.$$
  • Then
  • $$\int X^{-3/2}\,dX=\frac{X^{-1/2}}{-1/2}=-2X^{-1/2}.$$

Room 6: Evaluate the Limits

  • So
  • $$E=\frac{\sigma z}{4\varepsilon_0}\left[-2(z^2+r^2)^{-1/2}\right]_0^R.$$
  • This gives
  • $$E=\frac{\sigma z}{2\varepsilon_0}\left(\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{z^2+R^2}}\right).$$

Room 7: Final Simplification

  • Factor out the $z$:
  • $$\boxed{E=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\left(1-\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2+R^2}}\right)}.$$
  • If $$R\to\infty,$$ then the second term goes to zero, so
  • $$E=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$$
  • for an infinite sheet.

Memory Palace Path

This is a linked route. Each station creates the need for the next step, so you can walk from disk to ring to integral to final sheet limit without losing the chain.

Station 1: Disk Gate

Do not attack the whole disk at once. Cut it into thin concentric rings.

Transition 1 to 2

Once one thin ring is chosen, its charge must be written from surface density.

Station 2: Area Bridge

Use $$dq=\sigma dA=\sigma(2\pi r\,dr).$$

Transition 2 to 3

Now that the charge of one ring is known, import the ring field formula from the previous problem.

Station 3: Ring Lift

Treat each tiny ring as a charged ring on the axis.

Transition 3 to 4

After substitution, the only variable left is the ring radius $$r$$, so the next move is a radial integral.

Station 4: Radius Walk

Integrate from the center $$r=0$$ to the outer edge $$r=R.$$

Transition 4 to 5

The integrand has $$2r\,dr$$ attached to $$z^2+r^2$$, so a direct substitution is forced.

Station 5: Power Tunnel

Set $$X=z^2+r^2$$ so the power integral becomes standard.

Transition 5 to 6

Once the antiderivative is known, the remaining task is just endpoint evaluation.

Station 6: Limit Door

Evaluate at $$r=R$$ and $$r=0$$ to get the finite-disk formula.

Transition 6 to 7

The last natural question is what happens when the disk becomes huge.

Station 7: Infinite Sheet Vault

Let $$R\to\infty$$ and store the special limit: $$E=\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0).$$
