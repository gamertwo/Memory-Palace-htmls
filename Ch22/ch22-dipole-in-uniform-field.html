Derivation + Meaning

Dipole in a Uniform Electric Field

This page answers a question students often find weird at first: how can a dipole feel zero net force but still rotate? The answer is that the two forces are equal and opposite, so they cancel translationally, yet they act at different locations and therefore form a torque. That torque leads naturally to the potential-energy formula.

$$\tau=pE\sin\theta,\qquad U=-pE\cos\theta$$

Quick Data
Dipole Moment
$$\vec p$$ points from the negative charge to the positive charge.
Uniform Field
A uniform field gives equal field magnitude at both charges.
Net Force
Forces cancel translationally in a uniform field.
Torque
Separated equal-and-opposite forces produce rotation instead.
Rooms: Torque and Energy

Room 1: Write the Two Forces on the Dipole Charges

  • The positive charge feels force along $$\vec E,$$ and the negative charge feels force opposite $$\vec E.$$
  • Because the field is uniform, the two force magnitudes are equal: $$F=qE.$$
  • Their directions are opposite.

Why This Step

We begin with the individual forces because torque cannot be understood as a mysterious new law. It is simply the rotational effect of these two ordinary electric forces.

Room 2: Explain Why the Net Force Is Zero

  • The two forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
  • So the vector sum of forces is zero.
  • The dipole does not translate as a whole in a uniform field.

Why This Step

This resolves the first confusion. Students often expect any force pair to create motion, but here translation disappears because the forces cancel exactly.

Room 3: Explain Why the Net Torque Is Not Zero

  • The two equal forces act at different points of the dipole.
  • That separation creates a couple.
  • The dipole therefore rotates toward alignment with the field.

Why This Step

This is the core physical idea of the page: cancellation of net force does not imply cancellation of torque. Location matters as much as magnitude.

Room 4: Write the Torque Magnitude

  • The lever arm geometry gives $$\tau=Fd\sin\theta.$$
  • Using $$F=qE$$ and $$p=qd,$$ this becomes $$\tau=pE\sin\theta.$$
  • The torque tends to reduce $$\theta$$ and align the dipole with the field.

Why This Step

This is where the familiar rotational form emerges. Rewriting the torque using dipole moment makes the result compact and physically interpretable.

Room 5: Turn Torque into Potential Energy

  • Potential energy comes from the work associated with rotating the dipole.
  • Integrating the torque relation gives $$U=-pE\cos\theta.$$
  • The minimum energy occurs at $$\theta=0,$$ where the dipole is aligned.

Why This Step

Energy explains the same alignment behavior from a stability point of view. Torque tells us what happens dynamically; energy tells us which orientations are stable and unstable.
