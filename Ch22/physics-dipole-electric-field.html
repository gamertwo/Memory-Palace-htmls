A Dipole in an Electric Field

This page covers both parts in your screenshot: the torque that tends to align a dipole with a uniform electric field, and the potential energy expression that explains stable and unstable orientation.

$$\tau=pE\sin\theta,\qquad \vec{\tau}=\vec{p}\times\vec{E},\qquad U=-\vec{p}\cdot\vec{E}=-pE\cos\theta$$
Core Definitions
Dipole Charges
Two charges: $$+q$$ and $$-q$$
Separation
Distance between charges: $$d$$
Dipole Moment
$$\vec p=q\vec d$$ from negative to positive
Field
Uniform external field $$\vec E$$
Angle
$$\theta$$ is angle between $$\vec p$$ and $$\vec E$$
Physical Result
Net force is zero, but torque is nonzero unless aligned
Rooms: Torque on a Dipole

Room 1: Forces on Each Charge

  • On $$+q$$, force magnitude is $$F=qE$$ along $$\vec E.$$
  • On $$-q$$, force magnitude is also $$F=qE$$ opposite to $$\vec E.$$
  • These two forces cancel in translation, so net force is zero.

Room 2: Build the Couple

  • The equal and opposite forces act at different points, so they form a couple.
  • Perpendicular moment arm of each force about center is $$\frac{d}{2}\sin\theta.$$
  • Total torque magnitude:
  • $$\tau=F\left(\frac{d}{2}\sin\theta\right)+F\left(\frac{d}{2}\sin\theta\right)=Fd\sin\theta.$$

Room 3: Substitute $$F=qE$$ and $$p=qd$$

  • Replace $$F$$ in $$\tau=Fd\sin\theta$$:
  • $$\tau=(qE)d\sin\theta=(qd)E\sin\theta.$$
  • Using $$p=qd$$ gives
  • $$\boxed{\tau=pE\sin\theta}.$$

Room 4: Vector Form and Direction

  • Vector form is
  • $$\boxed{\vec\tau=\vec p\times\vec E}.$$
  • Direction follows right-hand rule and tends to rotate the dipole toward alignment with $$\vec E.$$
Rooms: Potential Energy of a Dipole

Room 5: Start From Rotational Work

  • Field torque tries to reduce $$\theta.$$
  • For a slow external rotation, potential-energy change is related by
  • $$dU=-\tau\,d\theta=-pE\sin\theta\,d\theta.$$

Room 6: Integrate

  • Integrate from a reference angle to $$\theta$$:
  • $$U(\theta)-U(\theta_0)=-\int_{\theta_0}^{\theta}pE\sin\phi\,d\phi.$$
  • So
  • $$U(\theta)-U(\theta_0)=-pE\left[\cos\theta_0-\cos\theta\right].$$

Room 7: Choose the Common Reference

  • Set $$U=0$$ at $$\theta_0=90^\circ$$ where $$\cos\theta_0=0.$$
  • Then
  • $$\boxed{U(\theta)=-pE\cos\theta}.$$
  • Equivalent vector form:
  • $$\boxed{U=-\vec p\cdot\vec E}.$$

Room 8: Stability Meaning

  • Minimum energy at $$\theta=0$$ (aligned): $$U_{\min}=-pE.$$
  • Maximum energy at $$\theta=\pi$$ (anti-aligned): $$U_{\max}=+pE.$$
  • This is why a dipole tends to align with the field direction.
Key Physics Shortcut

One-Line Memory Rule

If you remember only one chain, keep this: opposite forces make a couple, the couple gives $$\tau=pE\sin\theta,$$ and integrating torque gives $$U=-pE\cos\theta.$$ Alignment means lower energy.

Minimum at $$\theta=0,$$ maximum at $$\theta=\pi.$$
