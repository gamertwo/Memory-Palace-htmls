Derivation

Electric Field on the Axis of a Charged Disk

This page is the natural sequel to the ring derivation. A disk is treated as many thin concentric rings, so the ring formula becomes a building block inside a new radial integral. The physical payoff is large: the final expression also contains the infinite-sheet limit as a built-in check.

$$E_z=\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}\left(1-\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2+R^2}}\right),\qquad R\to\infty\Rightarrow E=\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}$$

Source
Uniform disk of radius $$R$$ and surface density $$\sigma.$$

A very large disk behaves like an infinite sheet.
Rooms: Full Derivation

Room 1: Build the Disk from Thin Rings

  • Do not attack the entire disk at once.
  • Instead cut it into concentric rings of radius $$r$$ and thickness $$dr.$$
  • Each thin ring already has a known axis field formula from the previous page.

Why This Step

We reuse a result we already earned. This is a standard physics move: solve a simpler source first, then stack many copies of it to build a harder source.

Room 2: Write the Charge of One Thin Ring

  • A ring of radius $$r$$ and thickness $$dr$$ has area $$dA=2\pi r\,dr.$$
  • So the ring charge is $$dq=\sigma\,dA=\sigma(2\pi r\,dr).$$
  • This is the disk version of writing the correct $$dq.$$

Why This Step

The disk is a surface object, so the charge on a slice must come from surface density times slice area. This is where the disk's geometry truly enters the derivation.

Room 3: Import the Ring Axis Formula

  • The field on the axis due to one ring is
  • $$dE_z=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{z\,dq}{(z^2+r^2)^{3/2}}.$$
  • Substitute the ring charge $$dq=\sigma(2\pi r\,dr).$$

Why This Step

This is the payoff for learning the ring first. The disk derivation is really a ring derivation inside a radial integral.

Room 4: Set Up the Radial Integral

  • After substitution,
  • $$E_z=\frac{\sigma z}{2\epsilon_0}\int_0^R \frac{r\,dr}{(z^2+r^2)^{3/2}}.$$
  • The only remaining variable is the ring radius $$r.$$

Why This Step

Once the geometry and slice choice are correct, the disk problem becomes a pure accumulation over radius. The math now matches the physical idea of adding rings from center to edge.

Room 5: Evaluate the Integral and Clean the Result

  • The integral gives $$-1/\sqrt{z^2+r^2}.$$
  • Evaluating from $$0$$ to $$R$$ yields
  • $$E_z=\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}\left(1-\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2+R^2}}\right).$$

Why This Step

The final form separates into a simple constant term and a geometric correction term. That structure is exactly what makes the infinite-sheet limit easy to read.

Room 6: Check the Infinite-Sheet Limit

  • If $$R\to\infty,$$ then $$z/\sqrt{z^2+R^2}\to 0.$$
  • So the field becomes $$E=\sigma/(2\epsilon_0).$$
  • This is the known field of an infinite charged sheet.

Why This Step

A limit check is physical proofreading. It confirms that the disk formula smoothly becomes a simpler, famous case when the geometry is pushed to an extreme.
