Example 7-26

What range of source resistance produces an underdamped natural response in a parallel RLC circuit with $$L=200\ \text{mH}$$ and $$C=0.032\ \mu\text{F}?$$ Normalize the parallel characteristic equation, match it to the standard second-order form, and impose $$\zeta<1.$$

$$LCs^2+\frac{L}{R_N}s+1=0$$ $$s^2+\frac{1}{R_NC}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0,\qquad R_N>1250\ \Omega$$
Quick Data
Inductance
$$L=200\ \text{mH}$$
Capacitance
$$C=0.032\ \mu\text{F}$$
Parallel Form
$$s^2+\frac{1}{R_NC}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0$$
Natural Frequency
$$\omega_0=12.5\times10^3\ \text{rad/s}$$
Critical Boundary
$$R_N=1250\ \Omega$$
Underdamped Range
$$R_N>1250\ \Omega$$
Answer Board
Standard Match
$$2\zeta\omega_0=\frac{1}{R_NC}$$
Threshold
$$R_N=\frac{1}{2\omega_0C}=1250\ \Omega$$
Final Answer
$$\boxed{R_N>1250\ \Omega}$$
Rooms

Room 1: Write the Parallel Equation

  • Use the natural-response form $$LCs^2+\frac{L}{R_N}s+1=0.$$
  • Divide by $$LC$$ to get $$s^2+\frac{1}{R_NC}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0.$$

Room 2: Match the Standard Form

  • Compare with $$s^2+2\zeta\omega_0s+\omega_0^2=0.$$
  • So $$2\zeta\omega_0=1/(R_NC)$$ and $$\omega_0^2=1/(LC).$$

Room 3: Compute the Natural Frequency

  • $$\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}=1/\sqrt{(0.2)(32\times10^{-9})}=12.5\times10^3\ \text{rad/s}.$$

Room 4: Use the Critical Boundary

  • Underdamped means $$\zeta<1.$$
  • The boundary occurs at $$\zeta=1,$$ so $$R_N=1/(2\omega_0C).$$

Room 5: Solve the Resistance Range

  • $$R_N=1/(2(12.5\times10^3)(32\times10^{-9}))=1250\ \Omega.$$
  • For a parallel RLC circuit, underdamping lies on the larger-resistance side, so $$\boxed{R_N>1250\ \Omega}.$$

Fast Memory Rule

In a parallel RLC circuit, more resistance means less damping. Normalize first, find the critical threshold, then move upward in resistance for underdamping.
