Sample Problem: Circular Arc Center Field

This worked example turns the straight-wire and arc formulas into a clean filtering problem: decide which wire sections actually contribute at the center and then keep only the surviving term.

$$B_1=0,\qquad B_2=0,\qquad B_3=\frac{\mu_0 i}{8R},\qquad \vec B=\frac{\mu_0 i}{8R}(-\hat k)$$

Core Rooms

Room 1: Split the Wire into Three Sections

  • The sample wire is made of straight section 1, straight section 2, and circular arc 3.
  • Compute the field from each section separately at point $$C$$ and then add the vector results.
  • This keeps the geometry visible instead of hiding it inside one long integral.

Room 2: Why Straight Section 1 Gives Zero

  • For section 1, the current element $$d\vec s$$ is collinear with the line from the element to the center.
  • That makes the angle $$\theta=0^\circ,$$ so $$d\vec s \times \hat r = 0.$$
  • Therefore the entire first straight section contributes no magnetic field at $$C.$$

Room 3: Straight Section 2 Also Vanishes

  • The same geometry appears in straight section 2.
  • Again the current-length element points along the line to the center, so $$\sin\theta=0.$$
  • That means $$B_2=0$$ as well.

Room 4: Keep Only the Quarter-Circle

  • The circular part is a quarter-circle, so $$\phi=\pi/2.$$
  • Use $$B=\frac{\mu_0 i\phi}{4\pi R}$$ to get $$B_3=\frac{\mu_0 i}{8R}.$$
  • The right-hand rule gives the direction into the page.

Room 5: Write the Net Field Cleanly

  • Since the straight sections vanish, the total field is just the arc field.
  • The scalar answer is $$B=\frac{\mu_0 i}{8R}.$$
  • The vector answer is $$\vec B=\frac{\mu_0 i}{8R}(-\hat k),$$ meaning into the page.

Main Concept

Main concept: before calculating anything hard, remove every wire segment whose current element points directly toward or away from the observation point.

Why This Page Matters

This sample is the cleanest Chapter 29 example of geometry deciding the physics before algebra does.

Sample Route

Point 1

Break the wire into the two straight sections and the arc.

Point 2

Use the cross-product angle to kill both straight sections immediately.

Point 3

Evaluate only the quarter-circle and send the result into the page with the right-hand rule.
