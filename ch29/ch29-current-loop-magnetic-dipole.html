29-6 Main Concept

A Current-Carrying Coil as a Magnetic Dipole

Chapter 29 closes by connecting current loops to magnetic dipoles. A coil in an external magnetic field behaves like a dipole with its own magnetic moment.

$$\vec\tau=\vec\mu\times\vec B,\qquad \mu=NiA$$

Core Rooms

Room 1: Why the Coil Is a Dipole

  • A current loop produces a magnetic pattern with a preferred axis.
  • That makes it behave like a magnetic dipole in an external field.
  • This closes the loop between moving charge, current, and magnetic moment.

Room 2: Magnetic Dipole Moment

  • The coil's dipole moment magnitude is $$\mu=NiA.$$
  • Here $$N$$ is number of turns, $$i$$ is current, and $$A$$ is area of each turn.
  • More turns, more current, or larger area all strengthen the dipole.

Room 3: Direction of the Dipole Moment

  • Curl your right-hand fingers with the current around the loop.
  • Your thumb points in the direction of the dipole moment.
  • That is the standard direction rule for a current loop.

Room 4: Torque in an External Field

  • The torque law is $$\vec\tau=\vec\mu\times\vec B.$$
  • The torque tries to align the dipole moment with the external field.
  • So the coil tends to rotate into magnetic alignment.

Room 5: Link Back to Chapter 28

  • Chapter 28 defined magnetic force on moving charges and currents.
  • Chapter 29 shows how currents create fields and how loops behave as dipoles.
  • Together they complete the current-field-force cycle.

Main Concept

Main concept: a coil is not just a current path; it is a magnetic dipole with a moment vector.
