Two-Wire Fields and Parallel-Current Force

Chapter 29 uses straight-wire fields in two ways: first to add magnetic fields from multiple wires, and then to find the force between parallel currents.

$$B=\frac{\mu_0 i}{2\pi R},\qquad \frac{F}{L}=\frac{\mu_0 i_1 i_2}{2\pi d}$$

Core Rooms

Room 1: Find Each Wire's Field First

  • At the observation point, each wire gives $$B=\frac{\mu_0 i}{2\pi R}.$$
  • Treat each field separately before trying to add them.
  • Draw the direction of each field with the right-hand rule.

Room 2: Add the Fields as Vectors

  • Do not add magnitudes unless the fields are already parallel.
  • In the chapter sample, the two fields are at right angles, so the net field comes from the Pythagorean theorem.
  • Direction comes from the geometry after the vector sum is built.

Room 3: Force on One Wire Comes from the Other's Field

  • Wire a creates a magnetic field at wire b.
  • That field acts on the current in wire b and produces a magnetic force.
  • So the force between currents is really a field-plus-force story, not a separate new law.

Room 4: Parallel-Current Force Formula

  • For two long parallel wires separated by $$d,$$ $$\frac{F}{L}=\frac{\mu_0 i_1 i_2}{2\pi d}.$$
  • The dependence is $$1/d,$$ so the force weakens with separation.
  • This formula is one of the flagship Chapter 29 results.

Room 5: Attract or Repel

  • Currents in the same direction attract.
  • Currents in opposite directions repel.
  • A good memory cue: parallel motion likes to stay together; opposite motion pushes apart.

Main Concept

Main concept: every multi-wire problem still starts from the magnetic field of a straight wire.

Why This Page Matters

The parallel-current force result becomes much easier to remember once you see it as current in a magnetic field.

Sample-Problem Focus

Point 1

When two wires affect a point off to the side, the hard part is usually direction, not magnitude.

Point 2

When two wires act on each other, the hard part is remembering that one wire's field is what pushes on the other wire.

Point 3

That makes the chapter flow feel cleaner: field first, force second.
