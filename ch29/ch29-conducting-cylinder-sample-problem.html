Sample Problem: Field Inside a Long Cylinder of Current

This Ampere-law sample finds the magnetic field at a chosen radius inside a cylindrical conductor by building the enclosed current first and then using a circular Amperian loop.

$$i_{\text{enc}}=\int J\,dA,\qquad \oint \vec B\cdot d\vec s=\mu_0 i_{\text{enc}}$$

Quick Data
Geometry
hollow cylindrical conductor

$$J=cr^2$$

integrate $$J\,dA$$ first

Ampere's law on a concentric circle
Core Rooms

Room 1: Use Cylindrical Symmetry

  • Because the current distribution depends only on radius, use a concentric circular Amperian loop.
  • At radius $$r,$$ the magnetic field is tangent to the loop and has the same magnitude everywhere on it.
  • That turns the left side of Ampere's law into a simple product.

Room 2: Build a Thin Ring of Current

  • For a thin ring, $$dA=(2\pi r)\,dr.$$
  • Then $$di=J\,dA=cr^2(2\pi r\,dr).$$
  • This ring method is the clean way to handle nonuniform current density.

Room 3: Integrate to Get the Enclosed Current

  • Integrate from the inner radius $$a$$ to the loop radius $$r.$$
  • That gives $$i_{\text{enc}}=\int_a^r cr^2(2\pi r\,dr)=\frac{\pi c}{2}(r^4-a^4).$$
  • The chapter sample is valuable because it shows that enclosed current may itself require calculus.

Room 4: Apply Ampere's Law

  • For the circular loop, $$\oint \vec B\cdot d\vec s=B(2\pi r).$$
  • So $$B(2\pi r)=\mu_0 i_{\text{enc}}.$$
  • Substitute the integrated current and solve for $$B.$$

Room 5: Set the Direction

  • Use the right-hand rule with the loop orientation.
  • The sign of enclosed current tells whether the field follows or opposes your chosen circulation direction.
  • The sample ends with both a magnitude and a loop direction around the axis.

Main Concept

Main concept: Ampere's law can still work beautifully with nonuniform current, but i_enc must be built carefully.

Why This Page Matters

This sample is more about constructing enclosed current than about the final algebra.

Why This Sample Matters

Point 1

This page teaches the exact place many students get stuck: Ampere's law is easy only after you have the correct enclosed current.

Point 2

When current density is nonuniform, never jump to a proportionality shortcut unless the distribution is uniform.

Point 3

The safe route is always: thin element, integrate current, then apply the law.
