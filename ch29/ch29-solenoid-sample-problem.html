29-5 Sample Problem

Sample Problem: Field Inside a Solenoid

This sample finds the magnetic field near the center of a long solenoid by computing the turns per unit length and then using the ideal-solenoid formula.

$$B=\mu_0 n i,\qquad n=\frac{N}{L}$$

Core Rooms

Room 1: Recognize the Ideal-Solenoid Model

  • The sample treats the coil as a long, tightly packed solenoid.
  • That means the chapter uses the ideal approximation near the center.
  • So the working formula is immediately the solenoid formula, not Biot-Savart point by point.

Room 2: Compute Turns per Unit Length

  • Use $$n=N/L.$$
  • If there are several identical close-packed layers, multiply the turns-per-length for one layer by the number of layers.
  • This is the real calculation step in the sample.

Room 3: Use the Solenoid Formula

  • Insert the result into $$B=\mu_0 n i.$$
  • Notice that the inner diameter does not appear in the final ideal-solenoid expression.
  • That is one of the chapter's most useful simplifications.

Room 4: Interpret the Result

  • The answer gives the field magnitude throughout most of the solenoid interior.
  • The field is approximately uniform there.
  • So this sample is really about estimating the central field of a practical coil.

Room 5: Exam-Memory Shortcut

  • Count turns per length first.
  • Then multiply by current and ?0.
  • If the problem says long, tightly packed, and central field, think ideal solenoid immediately.

Main Concept

Main concept: solenoid samples are mostly bookkeeping for winding density.

Why This Page Matters

This is exactly the kind of chapter problem where a clean model choice saves pages of work.

Main Lesson from the Sample

Point 1

The field strength is controlled by current and winding density, not by the coil diameter in the ideal model.

Point 2

That is why the sample problem feels easier after you identify the real target variable: n.

Point 3

Once n is correct, the rest is just one substitution.
