29-2 Results

Long Straight Wires and Circular Arcs

These are the two most reusable Biot-Savart results in the chapter: the field of a long straight wire and the field at the center of a circular arc.

$$B_{\text{long wire}}=\frac{\mu_0 i}{2\pi R},\qquad B_{\text{arc center}}=\frac{\mu_0 i\,\phi}{4\pi R}$$

Core Rooms

Room 1: Long Straight Wire

  • At perpendicular distance $$R$$ from an infinite straight wire, $$B=\frac{\mu_0 i}{2\pi R}.$$
  • The field lines are concentric circles around the wire.
  • The magnitude falls as $$1/R,$$ not $$1/R^2.$$

Room 2: Direction Around the Wire

  • Grip the wire with your right thumb in the current direction.
  • Your fingers curl in the magnetic-field direction.
  • Near a straight wire, the field is always tangent to a circle centered on the wire.

Room 3: Circular Arc Result

  • At the center of a circular arc, $$B=\frac{\mu_0 i\phi}{4\pi R}$$ with $$\phi$$ in radians.
  • A quarter-circle has $$\phi=\pi/2,$$ so $$B=\frac{\mu_0 i}{8R}.$$
  • The direction is perpendicular to the plane of the arc, found by the curled-finger rule.

Room 4: Why Some Straight Sections Give Zero

  • If the current element points directly toward or away from the center point, then $$\theta=0^\circ$$ or $$180^\circ.$$
  • That makes $$\sin\theta=0,$$ so that whole section contributes no magnetic field there.
  • This is the key simplification in the circular-arc sample problem.

Room 5: Sample-Problem Strategy

  • Split the wire into sections.
  • Discard any section with zero contribution.
  • Use the straight-wire or arc formula only on the surviving sections and then combine directions carefully.

Main Concept

Main concept: memorize the straight-wire and arc-center formulas because they are reused constantly.

Why This Page Matters

Sample problems in this section reward geometric filtering more than long algebra.

Sample Problem: Arc with Two Straight Sections

Point 1

In the chapter sample, the two straight sections give zero because their current elements are collinear with the radius toward the center.

Point 2

Only the quarter-circle survives, so the net field is $$B=\frac{\mu_0 i}{8R}.$$

Point 3

That is a perfect reminder that the fastest solution is usually to remove dead sections before doing any real calculation.
