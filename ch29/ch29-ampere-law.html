29-4 Core Concept

Ampere's Law

Ampere's law replaces hard Biot-Savart integrals when the magnetic field has enough symmetry. The skill is not just using the law; it is choosing the right loop.

$$\oint \vec B\cdot d\vec s=\mu_0 i_{\text{enc}}$$

Core Rooms

Room 1: What Ampere's Law Says

  • The circulation of $$\vec B$$ around a closed loop equals $$\mu_0$$ times the enclosed current.
  • Only current passing through the loop matters for the right side.
  • So the loop is not decorative; it controls the whole calculation.

Room 2: Choose an Amperian Loop

  • Pick a loop that matches the symmetry of the current distribution.
  • The goal is to make B constant over part of the loop and zero or perpendicular over the rest.
  • If the loop does not simplify the integral, it is the wrong loop.

Room 3: Straight-Wire Application

  • For a long straight wire, use a concentric circle of radius $$r.$$
  • Then $$B$$ is constant and tangent everywhere, so $$B(2\pi r)=\mu_0 i.$$
  • That gives $$B=\frac{\mu_0 i}{2\pi r}.$$

Room 4: Inside vs Outside

  • Outside a current distribution, the full enclosed current often appears.
  • Inside a conductor, only the current inside the chosen loop appears.
  • So Ampere's law naturally splits many problems into inside and outside formulas.

Room 5: When Ampere Beats Biot-Savart

  • Use Ampere's law for straight wires, cylinders, solenoids, and toroids.
  • Use Biot-Savart when the geometry is finite or asymmetric.
  • The chapter's fastest solutions all begin with the question: what symmetry do I own here?

Main Concept

Main concept: Ampere's law is powerful because symmetry converts a loop integral into plain multiplication.
