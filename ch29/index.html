Chapter 29

Magnetic Fields Due to Currents

This chapter set mirrors the Chapter 28 memory system but shifts the emphasis toward the main derivations, the reusable field formulas, and the sample-problem routes that show up most often.

$$d\vec B=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{i\,d\vec s\times \hat r}{r^2},\qquad \oint \vec B\cdot d\vec s=\mu_0 i_{\text{enc}},\qquad B_{\text{solenoid}}=\mu_0ni$$

Biot-Savart Law

Start Chapter 29 with the source rule itself: a current element creates a small magnetic field, and the total field is found by vector integration over the whole current distribution.
Stop 2

Long Straight Wires and Circular Arcs

These are the two most reusable Biot-Savart results in the chapter: the field of a long straight wire and the field at the center of a circular arc.
Stop 3

Sample Problem: Circular Arc Center Field

This worked example shows how to split the wire into sections, discard the zero-contribution straight pieces, and keep only the arc term at the center.
Stop 4

Sample Problem: Field Off to the Side of Two Wires

This page isolates the two-wire sample where each straight wire creates its own field at point B and the result comes from clean vector addition.
Stop 5

Two-Wire Fields and Parallel-Current Force

Chapter 29 uses straight-wire fields in two ways: first to add magnetic fields from multiple wires, and then to find the force between parallel currents.
Stop 6

Ampere's Law

Ampere's law replaces hard Biot-Savart integrals when the magnetic field has enough symmetry. The skill is not just using the law; it is choosing the right loop.
Stop 7

Sample Problem: Field Inside a Long Cylinder of Current

This Ampere-law sample evaluates the magnetic field at a chosen radius inside a cylindrical conductor by integrating the enclosed current first and then applying the loop law.
Stop 8

Sample Problem: Field Inside a Solenoid

This sample uses $$B=\mu_0ni$$ and turns-per-length bookkeeping to find the magnetic field near the center of a long coil.
Stop 9

Solenoids and Toroids

Ampere's law becomes especially powerful for tightly packed current loops. In Chapter 29, the two big devices are the long ideal solenoid and the toroid.
Stop 10

A Current-Carrying Coil as a Magnetic Dipole

Chapter 29 closes by connecting current loops to magnetic dipoles. A coil in an external magnetic field behaves like a dipole with its own magnetic moment.
