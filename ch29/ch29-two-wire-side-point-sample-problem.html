29-2 Sample Problem

Sample Problem: Field Off to the Side of Two Wires

This worked example shows the most important two-wire habit in the chapter: find each straight-wire field separately, resolve the geometry carefully, and only then combine the vectors.

$$B_1=\frac{\mu_0 i_1}{2\pi R_1},\qquad B_2=\frac{\mu_0 i_2}{2\pi R_2},\qquad B=\sqrt{B_1^2+B_2^2}$$
Study RouteOpen in Memory Builder
Quick Data
Given distances
$$R_1=4.5\text{ cm},\quad R_2=3.0\text{ cm}$$
Currents
$$i_1=4.0\text{ A},\quad i_2=3.2\text{ A}$$
Resultant size
$$B\approx1.89\times10^{-5}\text{ T}$$
Resultant angle
$$\phi\approx45^\circ+25^\circ=70^\circ$$
Core Rooms

Room 1: Find Each Wire's Field Magnitude

  • Use the straight-wire formula separately for each current.
  • That gives $$B_1=\frac{\mu_0 i_1}{2\pi R_1}$$ and $$B_2=\frac{\mu_0 i_2}{2\pi R_2}.$$
  • In the sample, both magnitudes come out from the same reusable Chapter 29 result.

Room 2: Lock Each Direction with the Right-Hand Rule

  • Point your right thumb along the current in each wire.
  • The magnetic field at point $$B$$ is tangent to a circle centered on that wire.
  • In this geometry the two field vectors turn out to be perpendicular.

Room 3: Resolve the 45 Degree Geometry

  • The sketch is an isosceles right triangle, so the angle used in the components is $$45^\circ.$$
  • That gives $$B_1=\frac{\mu_0 i_1}{2\pi d\cos45^\circ}$$ and $$B_2=\frac{\mu_0 i_2}{2\pi d\cos45^\circ}.$$
  • The geometry matters just as much as the wire formula itself.

Room 4: Add the Two Fields as Vectors

  • Because the two fields are perpendicular, use the Pythagorean theorem.
  • The net magnitude is $$B=\sqrt{B_1^2+B_2^2}\approx1.89\times10^{-5}\text{ T}.$$
  • This sample is a nice reminder not to add field magnitudes blindly.

Room 5: Finish with the Direction Angle

  • The offset angle from one component is $$\tan^{-1}(B_2/B_1)=\tan^{-1}(i_2/i_1)=25^\circ.$$
  • Add that to the built-in $$45^\circ$$ from the geometry.
  • The net direction is therefore about $$70^\circ$$ in the direction shown by the chapter figure.

Main Concept

Main concept: in multi-wire problems, treat each wire as its own source first and postpone vector addition until the individual fields are fully known.

Why This Page Matters

This is the chapter's cleanest example of mixing a standard field formula with careful geometry and vector addition.

Sample Route

Point 1

Compute $$B_1$$ and $$B_2$$ from the long straight wire formula.

Point 2

Use the right-hand rule to see that the two fields are perpendicular.

Point 3

Build the net magnitude with the Pythagorean theorem and then finish with the angle.
