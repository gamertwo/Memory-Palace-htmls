29-2 Core Concept

Biot-Savart Law

Start Chapter 29 with the source rule itself: a current element creates a small magnetic field, and the total field is found by vector integration over the whole current distribution.

$$dB=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{i\,ds\sin\theta}{r^2},\qquad d\vec B=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{i\,d\vec s\times \hat r}{r^2}$$

Core Rooms

Room 1: Start from a Current Element

  • Break the wire into tiny current-length elements $$i\,d\vec s.$$
  • Each element produces only a differential field $$d\vec B,$$ not the full field.
  • The net field is the vector sum of all those tiny contributions.

Room 2: Magnitude Form

  • The size of one contribution is $$dB=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{i\,ds\sin\theta}{r^2}.$$
  • The factor $$\sin\theta$$ means the field is largest when the current element is perpendicular to the line toward the point.
  • If $$\theta=0^\circ$$ or $$180^\circ,$$ then that element contributes zero field there.

Room 3: Vector Form

  • Direction comes from the cross product $$d\vec s\times \hat r.$$
  • That is why the vector form $$d\vec B=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{i\,d\vec s\times \hat r}{r^2}$$ is the real working form.
  • Whenever several wire pieces contribute, add them as vectors.

Room 4: Right-Hand Rule

  • Point your right thumb with the current direction.
  • Your curled fingers show the local circulation direction of the magnetic field lines.
  • Use that rule before integrating so the sign and direction stay correct.

Room 5: When to Use It

  • Use Biot-Savart when the geometry is specific enough that you can sum contributions directly.
  • For highly symmetric cases, Ampere's law is often faster later in the chapter.
  • The chapter keeps returning to one question: can symmetry replace integration, or do we need the full Biot-Savart route?
