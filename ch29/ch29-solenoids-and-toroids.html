29-5 Core Concept

Solenoids and Toroids

Ampere's law becomes especially powerful for tightly packed current loops. In Chapter 29, the two big devices are the long ideal solenoid and the toroid.

$$B_{\text{solenoid}}=\mu_0 n i,\qquad B_{\text{toroid}}=\frac{\mu_0 N i}{2\pi r}$$

Core Rooms

Room 1: Solenoid Field Pattern

  • Inside a long tightly packed solenoid, many turns reinforce one another.
  • Outside, the fields mostly cancel, so the external field is very weak.
  • That is why the ideal-solenoid model says strong inside, nearly zero outside.

Room 2: Solenoid Formula

  • For an ideal solenoid, $$B=\mu_0 n i,$$ where $$n$$ is turns per unit length.
  • Here $$n=N/L$$ if the solenoid has $$N$$ turns over length $$L.$$
  • The field does not depend on the solenoid diameter in the ideal model.

Room 3: Why Ampere's Law Works Here

  • Use a rectangular Amperian loop with one side inside the solenoid and one side outside.
  • The inside segment contributes Bh, the outside segment contributes about zero, and the perpendicular segments contribute zero.
  • That reduction leads directly to the ideal-solenoid formula.

Room 4: Toroid Formula

  • For a toroid, $$B=\frac{\mu_0Ni}{2\pi r}.$$
  • Unlike the ideal solenoid, the toroid field depends on radius $$r,$$ so it is not uniform across the cross section.
  • The field lines wrap in circles inside the toroid body.

Room 5: Compare the Two

  • Solenoid: nearly uniform field inside, nearly zero outside.
  • Toroid: field confined to the ring, changing with radius.
  • Both are Ampere-law winners because their symmetry makes the loop integral simple.

Main Concept

Main concept: long coils create controlled magnetic fields that symmetry makes easy to calculate.

Why This Page Matters

This is one of the most practical parts of the chapter because the formulas are both clean and reusable.

Sample-Problem Focus

Point 1

For solenoids, sample problems usually reduce to computing $$n=N/L$$ and then plugging into $$B=\mu_0 n i.$$

Point 2

For toroids, the central memory line is $$B=\mu_0Ni/(2\pi r).$$

Point 3

That makes this section formula-heavy in a good way: once the geometry is identified, the route is quick.
