Electrostatic Equilibrium

Charged Conductor Rules

This page locks the conductor results that are used again and again in Chapter 23. In electrostatic equilibrium, the field inside a conductor is zero, excess charge lives on the outer surface, and the external field just outside the surface is perpendicular to the surface.

$$E_{\text{inside conductor}}=0,\qquad E_{\text{just outside}}=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}$$

Rooms: Core Rules

Room 1: Internal Electric Field Must Vanish

  • If there were a nonzero electric field inside a conductor, free charges would move.
  • But electrostatic equilibrium means there is no continuing charge motion.
  • Therefore the net electric field inside the conductor is zero.

Why This Step

This is the foundational conductor fact. Every later result about surface charge and surface potential grows from the equilibrium condition of zero internal field.

Room 2: Excess Charge Moves to the Surface

  • Choose a Gaussian surface just inside the conductor.
  • Because $$\vec E=0$$ there, the net flux is zero and so the net enclosed excess charge is zero.
  • That forces any excess charge to reside on the actual surface, not in the bulk interior.

Why This Step

Gauss's law converts the zero-field statement into a charge-distribution statement. This is the clean proof that surface charge is not merely intuitive but required by the law.

Room 3: The External Field Is Perpendicular to the Surface

  • If the field had a tangential component at the surface, surface charges would slide along the conductor.
  • That would violate electrostatic equilibrium.
  • So the field just outside must be normal to the conductor surface.

Why This Step

This explains the direction of the field near conductors. It is not a memorized arrow rule; it follows from the fact that charges would otherwise keep moving.

Room 4: Use a Tiny Gaussian Pillbox at the Surface

  • One end cap lies inside the conductor, where the field is zero.
  • The curved surface contributes no flux because the field is parallel to it.
  • The only flux is through the outside end cap, giving $$EA=q_{\text{enc}}/\epsilon_0=\sigma A/\epsilon_0.$$

Why This Step

The pillbox isolates the tiny patch of surface charge and extracts the field right outside the conductor without needing the global shape of the object.

Room 5: Write the Surface-Field Formula

  • From the pillbox result, $$E=\sigma/\epsilon_0$$ just outside the surface.
  • Inside the conductor, the field remains zero.
  • This jump in field is one of the signature features of a charged conductor.

Why This Step

This is the reusable result for surface field magnitude. It is especially useful whenever a conductor boundary appears in a Chapter 23 problem.
