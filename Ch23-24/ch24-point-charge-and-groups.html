Core Potential Formula

Potential Due to a Point Charge and a Group of Charges

A point charge gives the cleanest potential formula in Chapter 24, and then superposition extends it to any number of point charges. The main conceptual payoff is huge: unlike electric fields, potentials add as scalars.

$$V=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{r},\qquad V_{\text{net}}=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_i \frac{q_i}{r_i}$$

Rooms: Core Formulas

Room 1: Derive the Point-Charge Potential from Infinity

  • Take the reference potential at infinity to be zero.
  • Integrate the radial point-charge field using $$V(r)-V(\infty)=-\int_\infty^r \vec E\cdot d\vec s.$$
  • This gives $$V=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{r}.$$

Why This Step

This formula is the scalar equivalent of the point-charge field law. It becomes the starting block for nearly every multi-charge potential problem.

Room 2: Keep the Sign of the Charge

  • A positive charge produces positive potential.
  • A negative charge produces negative potential.
  • The sign travels directly with $$q$$ in the formula.

Why This Step

Potential is scalar, so sign enters more directly than it often does in field problems. This makes algebraic addition both easier and more sensitive to sign mistakes.

Room 3: Superpose Many Point Charges Algebraically

  • For many point charges, add the separate potentials:
  • $$V_{\text{net}}=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_i \dfrac{q_i}{r_i}.$$
  • No vector decomposition is needed.

Why This Step

This is the big computational advantage of potential over field. A messy multi-direction vector sum becomes one ordinary algebraic sum.

Room 4: Use Orientation Awareness Correctly

  • For electric field, orientation changes the vector answer.
  • For potential, only the distances and charge signs matter.
  • That is why two geometrically different arrangements can share the same potential at a point while having different fields there.

Why This Step

This prevents a common confusion. Potential remembers distance and sign, but it forgets directional geometry in a way the electric field does not.
