Derivation

Field of a Sheet of Charge and Two Parallel Plates

Planar symmetry is the second major Gauss's-law pattern. A single infinite sheet needs a pillbox Gaussian surface, and two large oppositely charged plates are then handled by superposition of the individual sheet fields.

$$E_{\text{sheet}}=\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0},\qquad E_{\text{between plates}}=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}$$

Rooms: Full Derivation

Room 1: Use a Pillbox Gaussian Surface for One Sheet

  • Choose a short cylinder whose flat end caps lie on opposite sides of the sheet.
  • The sheet cuts through the middle of the pillbox.
  • This matches planar symmetry and isolates the charge enclosed.

Why This Step

The pillbox is the planar version of the cylinder for line charge. It places the relevant flux on the end caps while making the curved wall irrelevant.

Room 2: Identify Where the Flux Lives

  • The field is perpendicular to the sheet, so it pierces the two end caps.
  • The field is parallel to the curved side of the pillbox, so there is no flux there.
  • The total flux is therefore $$EA+EA=2EA.$$

Why This Step

This is the key simplification. The curved wall exists only to close the surface, not to contribute to the flux.

Room 3: Solve for the Single-Sheet Field

  • The enclosed charge is $$q_{\text{enc}}=\sigma A.$$
  • Gauss's law gives $$2EA=\sigma A/\epsilon_0.$$
  • So $$E=\sigma/(2\epsilon_0).$$

Why This Step

The factor of 2 comes from the sheet sending field equally out on both sides. That is why the field of one sheet is half the plate-field result.

Room 4: Superpose Two Oppositely Charged Plates

  • Each plate produces a field of magnitude $$\sigma/(2\epsilon_0).$$
  • Between the plates, the two fields point in the same direction and add.
  • Outside the plates, they point oppositely and cancel.

Why This Step

The two-plate result is not a new Gauss's-law derivation from scratch. It is the physical superposition of two already-known sheet fields.

Room 5: Write the Final Plate Result

  • Inside the plates, $$E=\sigma/\epsilon_0.$$
  • Outside the plates, $$E=0.$$
  • This is the ideal parallel-plate capacitor field pattern.

Why This Step

This page matters because it gives a nearly uniform field model used throughout later electrostatics and circuit analogies.
