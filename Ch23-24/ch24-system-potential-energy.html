Electric Potential Energy of a System of Point Charges

This page closes the chapter with the assembly view of electric potential energy. Imagine bringing charges in from infinity one by one and storing the work required to build the final configuration. That accumulated assembly work is the system's electric potential energy.

Rooms: Core Idea

Room 1: Assemble Two Charges from Infinity

  • Bring the first charge in from infinity: no work is needed because no other charge is present yet.
  • Bring the second charge into the potential created by the first.
  • The work required becomes the two-charge system energy.

Why This Step

The assembly picture gives a physical meaning to potential energy. It tells us where the stored energy comes from: the work needed to build the configuration.

Room 2: Write the Two-Charge Formula

  • The potential due to the first charge at the second charge is $$V=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q_1}{r}.$$
  • Multiplying by $$q_2$$ gives $$U=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q_1q_2}{r}.$$
  • The sign depends on the signs of the two charges.

Why This Step

This shows how system energy is built from potential. It also explains why like charges give positive energy and opposite charges give negative energy.

Why This Step

The pairwise sum is the clean way to avoid double counting. Each interacting pair should contribute exactly one time to the system energy.

Room 4: Interpret the Sign Physically

  • Positive system energy means work had to be done against repulsion to assemble the charges.
  • Negative system energy means the assembly is energetically favorable because attraction helps pull charges together.
  • The sign therefore tells a story about the configuration's stability and assembly cost.

Why This Step

This stops the formula from becoming pure algebra. The sign of the energy has direct physical meaning about whether the arrangement had to be forced together or naturally wanted to form.
