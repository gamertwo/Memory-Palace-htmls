Derivation

Electric Field of an Infinite Line of Charge

This is the first big Gauss's-law win. A very long line of uniform charge has cylindrical symmetry, so the natural Gaussian surface is a coaxial cylinder. Once that surface is chosen, the flux integral collapses almost instantly.

$$E=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0 r}$$
Major GuideOpen in Memory Builder
Rooms: Full Derivation

Room 1: Match the Symmetry with a Cylinder

  • An infinite line looks the same after rotation around its axis and after sliding along its length.
  • So the natural Gaussian surface is a coaxial cylinder of radius $$r$$ and length $$h.$$
  • This surface mimics the problem's symmetry exactly.

Why This Step

Gauss's law becomes powerful only when the Gaussian surface copies the symmetry of the source. A sphere or cube would enclose the charge but would not simplify the field.

Room 2: Read the Direction and Constancy of the Field

  • The field must point radially outward from the line for positive charge.
  • At every point on the curved side of the Gaussian cylinder, the field has the same magnitude $$E.$$
  • The field is perpendicular to that curved surface.

Why This Step

This is the payoff of symmetry. It lets us treat the field as constant over the curved side and normal to it, which is what makes the flux integral easy.

Room 3: Know Which Surfaces Matter for Flux

  • The curved side contributes flux because the field pierces it normally.
  • The two end caps contribute zero because the field is parallel to them.
  • So the entire net flux is through the curved surface alone.

Why This Step

The end caps are present only to close the surface. They do not help the flux because the field skims them instead of piercing them.

Room 4: Write Gauss's Law and Solve

  • The curved area is $$A=2\pi rh,$$ so the flux is $$\Phi=E(2\pi rh).$$
  • The enclosed charge is $$q_{\text{enc}}=\lambda h.$$
  • Gauss's law gives $$E(2\pi rh)=\lambda h/\epsilon_0,$$ so $$E=\lambda/(2\pi\epsilon_0 r).$$

Why This Step

Once the right surface and symmetry statements are in place, the derivation is almost automatic. That is exactly why Gauss's law is so valuable here.
