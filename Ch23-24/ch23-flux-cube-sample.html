Worked Example

Flux Through a Closed Cube in a Nonuniform Field

This is the best first flux workout because it forces you to think face by face. The field is $$\vec E=(3x)\hat i+4\hat j$$ in SI units, and the cube extends from $$x=1$$ m to $$x=3$$ m with face area $$A=4.0\text{ m}^2.$$ The trick is to stop treating the whole cube at once and instead ask which field component actually pierces each face.

$$\Phi_{\text{net}}=24\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C},\qquad q_{\text{enc}}=\epsilon_0\Phi_{\text{net}}=2.1\times10^{-10}\text{ C}$$

Given Data
Field
$$\vec E=(3x)\hat i+4\hat j$$
Cube Position
Left face at $$x=1$$ m, right face at $$x=3$$ m
Face Area
$$A=4.0\text{ m}^2$$
Goal
Find the net flux through the closed cube and then the enclosed charge.
Rooms: Full Solution

Room 1: Split the Field Into x and y Components

  • The field has an x-part $$3x\hat i$$ and a y-part $$4\hat j.$$
  • Different faces care about different parts of the field.
  • A component contributes only if it pierces the face normally.

Why This Step

The dot product works face by face. Splitting the field into components tells us immediately which parts are relevant for each face and which ones only skim along it.

Room 2: Do the Right and Left Faces Using the x-Component

  • On the right face, $$x=3$$ m and the outward normal is $$+\hat i,$$ so $$\Phi_R=(9)(4.0)=36.$$
  • On the left face, $$x=1$$ m and the outward normal is $$-\hat i,$$ so $$\Phi_L=-(3)(4.0)=-12.$$
  • The y-component gives zero flux on these faces because it skims along them.

Why This Step

The x-component is the only part aligned with the normals of the left and right faces. The sign flips because the outward normal flips.

Room 3: Do the Top and Bottom Faces Using the y-Component

  • The top face has outward normal $$+\hat j,$$ so $$\Phi_T=(4)(4.0)=16.$$
  • The bottom face has outward normal $$-\hat j,$$ so $$\Phi_B=-(4)(4.0)=-16.$$
  • These two contributions cancel exactly.

Why This Step

The y-component is constant, so the top and bottom faces are a perfect sign-canceling pair. This is a quick symmetry checkpoint inside the calculation.

Room 4: Recognize the Front and Back Faces Give Zero

  • The outward normals of the front and back faces point along $$\pm \hat k.$$
  • The field has no $$\hat k$$ component.
  • So $$\Phi_{\text{front}}=\Phi_{\text{back}}=0.$$

Why This Step

No normal component means no flux. This is the cleanest application of the “piercing versus skimming” idea from the basics page.

Room 5: Add All Six Faces and Use Gauss's Law

  • $$\Phi_{\text{net}}=36-12+16-16+0+0=24\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}.$$
  • Then $$q_{\text{enc}}=\epsilon_0\Phi_{\text{net}}=(8.85\times10^{-12})(24)=2.1\times10^{-10}\text{ C}.$$
  • The positive net flux means the net enclosed charge is positive.

Why This Step

Flux is additive over the faces of a closed surface. Once the total is known, Gauss's law turns that total into the enclosed charge in one direct step.
