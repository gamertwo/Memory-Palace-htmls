Example 7-15

This example finds the response of an RC circuit to an exponential forcing function. The total response is the sum of a natural exponential and a forced exponential, and the initial capacitor voltage is given as $v(0)=V_0$.

$$v(t)=\left[V_0-\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}\right]e^{-t/RC}+\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}e^{-\alpha t},\qquad t\ge 0$$
Rooms

Room 1: Start with the Natural Response

  • As before, the natural response of an RC circuit is
  • $$v_N(t)=Ke^{-t/RC},\qquad t\ge 0.$$

Room 2: Write the Forced-Response Differential Equation

  • For the exponential input, the forced part satisfies
  • $$RC\frac{dv_F(t)}{dt}+v_F(t)=V_Ae^{-\alpha t},\qquad t\ge 0.$$

Room 3: Guess the Forced Response Shape

  • Because the input is exponential, choose a forced response of the same form:
  • $$v_F(t)=K_F e^{-\alpha t}.$$

Room 4: Substitute into the Equation

  • Differentiate:
  • $$\frac{d}{dt}\left(K_F e^{-\alpha t}\right)=-\alpha K_F e^{-\alpha t}.$$
  • Substitute:
  • $$RC\left(-\alpha K_F e^{-\alpha t}\right)+K_F e^{-\alpha t}=V_A e^{-\alpha t}.$$

Room 5: Solve for $K_F$

  • Cancel the common exponential:
  • $$-\alpha RC K_F+K_F=V_A.$$
  • So
  • $$K_F(1-\alpha RC)=V_A$$
  • and therefore
  • $$K_F=\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}.$$
  • This step assumes $$1-\alpha RC\neq 0.$$

Room 6: Build the Total Response

  • The total response is natural plus forced:
  • $$v(t)=v_N(t)+v_F(t)=Ke^{-t/RC}+\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}e^{-\alpha t},\qquad t\ge 0.$$

Room 7: Apply the Initial Condition

  • Given $$v(0)=V_0,$$ substitute $$t=0:$$
  • $$V_0=K+\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}.$$
  • So
  • $$K=V_0-\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}.$$

Room 8: Final Answer

  • Substitute $K$ back into the total response:
  • $$\boxed{v(t)=\left[V_0-\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}\right]e^{-t/RC}+\frac{V_A}{1-\alpha RC}e^{-\alpha t}},\qquad t\ge 0.$$
  • The waveform is the sum of two decaying exponentials.
  • The one with the larger time constant decays more slowly and becomes the dominant exponential.
Key Reading Rule

Natural + Forced

The fastest way to remember this page is: keep the natural RC exponential, then guess a forced term with the same shape as the input. Solve its coefficient, then use the initial condition only at the very end to fix the natural constant.
