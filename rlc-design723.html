Design Exercise 7-23

For a series RLC circuit, work in both directions: in part (a) compute the roots from given $$R_T,$$ $$L,$$ and $$C;$$ in part (b) choose $$R_T$$ and $$C$$ from a target complex root pair; in part (c) design for a repeated root at $$-10^4$$ and explain why that condition does not produce a unique circuit.

$$LCs^2+R_TC s+1=0,\qquad s^2+\frac{R_T}{L}s+\frac{1}{LC}=0$$ $$\text{Target roots control the coefficients, and the coefficients control the design.}$$
Quick Data
Part (a) Data
$$R_T=2\ \text{k}\Omega,\quad L=100\ \text{mH},\quad C=0.4\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}$$
Part (b) Target Roots
$$s_{1,2}=-1000\pm j2000$$ with $$L=100\ \text{mH}$$
Part (c) Target Root
$$s_1=s_2=-10^4$$
Series Match
$$\dfrac{R_T}{L}$$ sets the damping term and $$\dfrac{1}{LC}$$ sets the constant term.
Known Answers
Part (a): roots near $$-1340$$ and $$-18{,}660.$$ Part (b): $$R_T=200\ \Omega,\ C=2\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}.$$
Part (c) Idea
Two equations in three unknowns means there is no unique design.
Answer Board
Part (a)
$$s_1\approx -1340,\qquad s_2\approx -18{,}660$$
Part (b)
$$R_T=200\ \Omega,\qquad C=2\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}$$
Part (c)
No unique answer. One valid choice is $$C=1\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F},\ L=10\ \text{mH},\ R_T=200\ \Omega.$$

Rooms

Room 1: Keep the Characteristic Skeleton Ready

  • A series RLC circuit always uses $$LCs^2+R_TC s+1=0$$ or, after normalization, $$s^2+\dfrac{R_T}{L}s+\dfrac{1}{LC}=0.$$
  • Part (a) goes from components to roots.
  • Parts (b) and (c) go from roots back to components.

Room 2: Build the Part (a) Equation

  • Use $$R_T=2000\ \Omega,$$ $$L=0.1\ \text{H},$$ and $$C=0.4\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}. $$
  • Then $$LC=4\times 10^{-8}$$ and $$R_TC=8\times 10^{-4}. $$
  • So the characteristic equation is $$4\times 10^{-8}s^2+8\times 10^{-4}s+1=0,$$ or normalized as $$s^2+20{,}000s+25\times 10^6=0.$$

Room 3: Solve Part (a)

  • Apply the quadratic formula to $$s^2+20{,}000s+25\times 10^6=0.$$
  • The roots are $$s=\dfrac{-20{,}000\pm\sqrt{20{,}000^2-4(25\times 10^6)}}{2}. $$
  • That gives $$s_1\approx -1340$$ and $$s_2\approx -18{,}660.$$

Room 4: Turn the Part (b) Target Roots into a Polynomial

  • The target roots are $$-1000\pm j2000.$$
  • Their characteristic equation is $$\left(s+1000-j2000\right)\left(s+1000+j2000\right)=0.$$
  • Expanding gives $$s^2+2000s+5\times 10^6=0.$$

Room 5: Solve Part (b) by Coefficient Matching

  • Match $$s^2+2000s+5\times 10^6$$ with $$s^2+\dfrac{R_T}{L}s+\dfrac{1}{LC}. $$
  • From the linear term, $$\dfrac{R_T}{L}=2000,$$ so $$R_T=2000(0.1)=200\ \Omega.$$
  • From the constant term, $$\dfrac{1}{LC}=5\times 10^6,$$ so $$C=\dfrac{1}{5\times 10^6(0.1)}=2\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F}. $$

Room 6: Translate the Part (c) Double Root

  • The requirement $$s_1=s_2=-10^4$$ means the normalized quadratic is $$\left(s+10^4\right)^2=s^2+2\times 10^4 s+10^8. $$
  • Matching with $$s^2+\dfrac{R_T}{L}s+\dfrac{1}{LC}$$ gives $$\dfrac{R_T}{L}=2\times 10^4$$ and $$LC=10^{-8}. $$
  • In unnormalized form the same conditions are $$R_TC=2\times 10^{-4}$$ and $$LC=10^{-8}. $$

Room 7: Explain Why Part (c) Has No Unique Answer

  • Part (c) asks for $$R_T,$$ $$L,$$ and $$C,$$ but the repeated-root condition supplies only two independent equations.
  • That means infinitely many series RLC circuits satisfy the same root requirement.
  • One valid choice is $$C=1\times 10^{-6}\ \text{F},$$ which forces $$L=10\ \text{mH}$$ from $$LC=10^{-8},$$ and then $$R_T=200\ \Omega$$ from $$R_TC=2\times 10^{-4}. $$

Room 8: Design Takeaway

  • When roots are given, they determine the characteristic coefficients.
  • Those coefficients determine algebraic relationships among $$R_T,$$ $$L,$$ and $$C.$$
  • If the number of independent relationships is smaller than the number of unknowns, the design is not unique.

Design Direction Check

Part (a) moves from elements to roots. Parts (b) and (c) move from roots to element constraints. The algebra is the same in both directions because the characteristic equation is the shared bridge.

Key Shortcut

Coefficient Matching First

In design problems, do not guess element values. First convert the target roots into a characteristic polynomial, then compare that polynomial with the standard series RLC form. The comparison tells you exactly which combinations are fixed and whether the answer is unique.
