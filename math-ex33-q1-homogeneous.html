Exercise 3.3 Question 1

This equation is solved by the homogeneous method. First convert it into $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ form, then use the standard substitution $y=vx$.

$$(y^2-xy)\,dx+x^2\,dy=0$$
Rooms

Room 1: Convert to Differential Form

  • Move to $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ form:
  • $$x^2\frac{dy}{dx}=-(y^2-xy)=xy-y^2.$$
  • So
  • $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{xy-y^2}{x^2}=\frac{y}{x}-\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^2.$$

Room 2: Identify the Homogeneous Pattern

  • The right side depends only on the ratio $\dfrac{y}{x}$.
  • So use the substitution
  • $$y=vx \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \frac{dy}{dx}=v+x\frac{dv}{dx}.$$

Room 3: Substitute and Simplify

  • Replace $\dfrac{y}{x}$ by $v$:
  • $$v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=v-v^2.$$
  • Subtract $v$ from both sides:
  • $$x\frac{dv}{dx}=-v^2.$$

Room 4: Separate Variables

  • Bring $v$ terms to the left and $x$ terms to the right:
  • $$\frac{dv}{v^2}=-\frac{dx}{x}.$$

Room 5: Integrate

  • Integrate both sides:
  • $$\int v^{-2}\,dv=-\int \frac{dx}{x}.$$
  • $$-\frac{1}{v}=-\ln|x|+C.$$
  • Absorb the sign into the constant:
  • $$\frac{1}{v}=\ln|x|+C.$$

Room 6: Back-Substitute

  • Since $$v=\frac{y}{x}, \qquad \frac{1}{v}=\frac{x}{y}.$$
  • Therefore
  • $$\boxed{\frac{x}{y}=\ln|x|+C}.$$
  • If the textbook assumes $x>0$, this is written as $$\dfrac{x}{y}=\log x+C.$$
Final Answer

Using the homogeneous substitution $y=vx$, the solution is $$\boxed{\frac{x}{y}=\ln|x|+C}.$$ In the same textbook style, this matches $$\boxed{\frac{x}{y}=\log x+C}$$ when $x>0$.
