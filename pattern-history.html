🔬 Exercise 7-9 — Full Walkthrough Using the 5 Palace Moves
Problem (a) — Voltage / RC
Problem (b) — Current / RL
Given:   $10^{-4}\dfrac{dv_C}{dt} + v_C = -5u(t)$   with   $v_C(0) = 5\text{ V}$
👁
I
Move I · Recognize
Is this a first-order circuit?
The ODE has the form $\tau\dot{x} + x = f(t)$ — linear, first-order, constant coefficients. One energy-storage element. ✓ First-order RC confirmed. The solution will be $v_C(t) = A + Be^{-t/\tau}$.
⏱
II
Move II · Flip → Extract τ
The coefficient in front of $\dot{v}_C$ IS the time constant
ODE coefficient directly gives τ
$$10^{-4}\frac{dv_C}{dt} + v_C = -5u(t) \;\Rightarrow\; \tau = 10^{-4}\text{ s}$$
When ODE is in standard form $\tau\dot{x}+x=f$, the leading coefficient IS τ. No flipping needed here — it's already readable. $k = 1/\tau = 10{,}000$
Result: $\tau = 10^{-4}$ s $= 0.1$ ms  →  exponential decay rate $k = \mathbf{10{,}000}$ s⁻¹
🔥
III-a
Move III · Melt → Forced Response x(∞)
What does the circuit settle at as $t \to \infty$?
At steady state: $\dot{v}_C = 0$, so the ODE collapses to:
$$\tau \cdot 0 + v_C(\infty) = -5 \;\Rightarrow\; v_C(\infty) = -5\text{ V}$$
The derivative term vanishes. DC steady state = whatever is on the right-hand side (if τẋ+x=A, then x(∞)=A)
Forced response = −5 V. This is where the step input drives the circuit. Negative because the source is $-5u(t)$.
❄️
III-b
Move III · Freeze → Initial Condition x(0)
What is the starting value?
Given directly in the problem statement
$$v_C(0) = +5\text{ V}$$
Capacitor had +5 V stored before $t = 0$. The step switches on at $t = 0$ but the capacitor voltage cannot jump — it starts exactly where it was.
✂️
IV
Move IV · Split → Find Amplitude B
The natural response amplitude = difference between start and end
From the universal template: $B = x(0) - x(\infty)$
$$B = v_C(0) - v_C(\infty) = 5 - (-5) = +10$$
Why: At $t=0$, the solution must give $x(0)$:   $A + B\cdot e^0 = A + B = x(0)$  →  $B = x(0)-A = x(0)-x(\infty)$.
  • →$x(\infty) = -5$, $x(0)=+5$. The gap is $+10$.
  • →The natural term must cover this gap and decay it away.
  • →$B = +10$ means the exponential starts positive and decays to zero.
📐
V
Move V · Blueprint → Assemble the Answer
Fill the skeleton with your extracted values
Template → Substitute
$$v_C(t) = \underbrace{x(\infty)}_{-5} + \underbrace{[x(0)-x(\infty)]}_{+10}\,e^{-t/\tau}$$
✅ Final Answer — Part (a)
$$v_C(t) = -5 + 10\,e^{-10{,}000\,t}\;\text{V} \qquad t \geq 0$$
Verify: At $t=0$: $-5+10(1) = +5$ V ✓  ·  As $t\to\infty$: $-5+10(0) = -5$ V ✓
The voltage starts at +5 V, passes through 0 V at $t = \ln2/10{,}000 \approx 69.3\;\mu$s, then settles at −5 V.
Waveform: $v_C(t) = -5+10e^{-10{,}000t}$ V
Given:   $5{\times}10^{-2}\dfrac{di_L}{dt} + 2000\,i_L = 10u(t)$   with   $i_L(0)=-5$ mA
👁
I
Move I · Recognize
First-order — but now it's an RL circuit!
Same ODE shape, different physical element. The inductor's $L\frac{di}{dt}$ gives the same first-order structure. The skeleton is universal — it doesn't care about RC vs RL. $x(t) = A + Be^{-t/\tau}$ still applies.
⏱
II
Move II · Extract τ — ODE not in standard form!
Must divide through to isolate $\dot{i}_L$ coefficient
Standard form requires coefficient of x to be 1
$$5{\times}10^{-2}\dot{i}_L + 2000\,i_L = 10u(t)$$ $$\div 2000: \;\;\frac{5{\times}10^{-2}}{2000}\dot{i}_L + i_L = \frac{10}{2000}u(t)$$ $$\tau\dot{i}_L + i_L = 0.005\,u(t) \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \tau = \frac{5{\times}10^{-2}}{2000} = 25\;\mu\text{s}$$
Key move: When the coefficient on $x$ isn't 1, divide the whole equation by it first. Then the leading coefficient IS τ.   $k = 1/\tau = 40{,}000$ s⁻¹
🔥
III
Move III · Freeze & Melt — Boundary Conditions
Initial: given. Final: from RHS of standard form ODE.
Melt: steady state from standard form
$$i_L(\infty) = \frac{10}{2000} = 0.005\text{ A} = 5\text{ mA}$$
Freeze: given initial condition
$$i_L(0) = -5\text{ mA} \quad\text{(given)}$$
✂️
IV
Move IV · Split → Amplitude B
Natural response amplitude = gap between start and end
Same formula as always
$$B = i_L(0) - i_L(\infty) = -5 - 5 = -10\text{ mA}$$
B is negative here — the circuit starts below the forced value and climbs up. The exponential term contributes $-10e^{-40000t}$, which starts at $-10$ and grows toward zero.
📐
V
Move V · Blueprint → Final Answer
Assemble the skeleton
$$i_L(t) = \underbrace{5}_{x(\infty)} + \underbrace{(-10)}_{B}\,e^{-40{,}000\,t}\;\text{mA}$$
✅ Final Answer — Part (b)
$$i_L(t) = 5 - 10\,e^{-40{,}000\,t}\;\text{mA} \qquad t \geq 0$$
Verify: $t=0$: $5-10(1)=-5$ mA ✓  ·  $t\to\infty$: $5-10(0)=+5$ mA ✓
Starts at −5 mA, charges toward +5 mA with τ = 25 μs.
Waveform: $i_L(t) = 5-10e^{-40{,}000t}$ mA
🔑 Pattern Summary — What Your Brain Should Auto-Fire
👁 See the Trigger
First-order ODE or $e^{-kt}$ in equation → I know this shape
Works for RC and RL circuits identically
Works whether response or ODE is given — same moves
⚙️ Do the Moves
τ: Flip exponent coefficient, OR read ODE leading coeff (after normalizing)
x(∞): $t\to\infty$ kills exponential. Only DC survives. (= RHS of normalized ODE)
x(0): Plug $t=0$ (e⁰=1) or read from given initial condition
B: Always = $x(0) - x(\infty)$
✅ The Skeleton (Never Changes)
$x(t) = x(\infty) + [x(0)-x(\infty)]\,e^{-t/\tau}$
ODE: $\tau\dot{x} + x = x(\infty)\,u(t)$
Zero crossing: set $=0$, isolate $e^{-kt}$, take $\ln$
Verify: check $t=0$ and $t\to\infty$ always ✓
