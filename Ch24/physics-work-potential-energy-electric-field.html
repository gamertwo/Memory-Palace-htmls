Work and Potential Energy in an Electric Field

An electron near Earth moves upward through a downward electric field. The page is really a sign-check exercise: compute the work done by the electric force, then flip it to electric potential-energy change with $$\Delta U=-W.$$

$$W=\vec F\cdot\vec d=q\vec E\cdot\vec d=qEd\cos180^\circ=1.2\times10^{-14}\,\text{J},\qquad \Delta U=-W=-1.2\times10^{-14}\,\text{J}$$

What To Notice

  • The field points downward, but the electron moves upward.
  • Because the charge is negative, $$\vec F=q\vec E$$ points opposite the field.
  • The electric force does positive work, so the electric potential energy decreases.
Quick Anchors
Given
Uniform atmospheric field $$E=150\,\text{N/C}$$ downward and displacement $$d=520\,\text{m}$$ upward.
Charge
For an electron, $$q=-1.60\times10^{-19}\,\text{C}.$$
Work Link
Use $$W=qEd\cos\theta$$ with $$\theta=180^\circ$$ between $$\vec E$$ and $$\vec d.$$
Energy Link
Once the work is known, convert with $$\Delta U=-W.$$
Rooms

Room 1: Separate Field Direction from Force Direction

  • The electric field near Earth points downward.
  • The electron has negative charge, so the electric force on it points upward.
  • That already hints that the force helps the upward motion.

Room 2: Write the Constant-Force Work Formula

  • For a constant force, $$W=\vec F\cdot\vec d.$$
  • In electrostatics, replace the force by $$\vec F=q\vec E.$$
  • That turns the work into $$W=q\vec E\cdot\vec d=qEd\cos\theta.$$

Room 3: Choose the Correct Angle

  • The field is downward and the displacement is upward.
  • Those directions are opposite, so $$\theta=180^\circ.$$
  • Therefore $$\cos\theta=-1.$$

Room 4: Substitute the Charge Sign Carefully

  • The electron charge is negative: $$q=-1.60\times10^{-19}\,\text{C}.$$
  • Multiplying a negative charge by $$\cos180^\circ=-1$$ produces a positive work result.
  • That is the sign checkpoint most people need here.

Room 5: Compute the Work

  • Insert the values into $$W=qEd\cos180^\circ.$$
  • The product gives $$W=1.2\times10^{-14}\,\text{J}.$$
  • Positive work means the field transfers energy into kinetic form during the upward motion.

Room 6: Convert Work into Potential-Energy Change

  • Use the chapter relation $$\Delta U=-W.$$
  • So the potential-energy change is $$-1.2\times10^{-14}\,\text{J}.$$
  • The minus sign means the electric potential energy becomes smaller.

Room 7: Interpret the Physics

  • The electron moves in the same direction as the electric force acting on it.
  • When a conservative force does positive work, the corresponding potential energy drops.
  • So the answer is not just a number; it matches the motion logic.

Room 8: Keep the One-Line Memory

  • Negative charge plus opposite field/displacement directions can still yield positive work.
  • The chapter shortcut is: find work first, then flip the sign for $$\Delta U.$$
  • That one habit avoids most sign mistakes in early potential problems.

Geometry of the Sign

The field points downward, but the electron is pushed upward because its charge is negative. That is why the work comes out positive.

$$\vec F=q\vec E$$
$$W=qEd\cos180^\circ$$
$$W=+1.2\times10^{-14}\,\text{J}$$
$$\Delta U=-1.2\times10^{-14}\,\text{J}$$

Calculation Ladder

The whole sample compresses into a four-step ladder: force, work, numerical sign, then potential-energy change.

Fast Check

If the electric force helps the motion, its work should be positive. Once you see that, you already know $$\Delta U$$ must be negative before touching the calculator.
