24-4 | Concept + Problem: Scalar vs Vector

Potential Is Not a Vector; Orientation Is Irrelevant

Twelve electrons equally spaced on a circle make a clean contrast. The potential at the center depends only on the scalar sum of identical contributions, while the electric field at the same center can cancel completely by symmetry and then reappear when the symmetry is broken.

$$V=-12\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{e}{R}=-\frac{3e}{\pi\varepsilon_0 R},\qquad \vec E=0\text{ for the full circle, but }\vec E\neq0\text{ for the 120^\circ arc}$$

What To Notice

  • Potential is scalar, so rotating charges around the center does not change their individual distances to the center.
  • Field is vector, so directional cancellation matters.
  • Breaking symmetry leaves $$V$$ unchanged but changes $$\vec E.$$
Quick Anchors
Equal Circle
All 12 electrons remain a distance $$R$$ from the center.
Potential
Each electron contributes the same scalar amount $$-\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{e}{R}.$$
Field
The full circle is symmetric enough to make the center field cancel to zero.
Arc Case
Moving the electrons into a 120-degree arc leaves the center potential the same but produces a net field toward the arc.
Rooms

Room 1: Potential Only Cares About Distance and Sign

  • At the center $$C,$$ every electron is the same distance $$R$$ away.
  • Each one contributes the same negative scalar potential.
  • So the total potential is just 12 identical terms added together.

Room 2: Write the Center Potential

  • The contribution of one electron is $$-\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{e}{R}.$$
  • Multiplying by 12 gives $$V=-12\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{e}{R}.$$
  • The compact form is $$V=-\dfrac{3e}{\pi\varepsilon_0 R}.$$

Room 3: Why the Field Is Zero for the Full Circle

  • The electric field is a vector, so equal and opposite contributions can cancel.
  • For every electron on the circle, another sits opposite it.
  • At the center, those paired field contributions cancel exactly.

Room 4: Change the Arrangement Without Changing the Radius

  • Now slide the same 12 electrons along the circle into a 120-degree arc.
  • No electron changes its distance from the center.
  • So the potential contribution of each electron remains the same.

Room 5: Potential Stays the Same

  • Because each electron is still distance $$R$$ away, the scalar sum is unchanged.
  • The center potential is still the same value from the full-circle case.
  • This is the page’s headline claim: orientation does not matter for scalar potential.

Room 6: Field No Longer Cancels

  • The arc arrangement is not symmetric around the center.
  • So the field contributions no longer cancel to zero.
  • The net field points toward the charge distribution because the electrons are negative.

Room 7: Keep the Contrast Straight

  • Potential: scalar, algebraic, orientation-irrelevant here.
  • Field: vector, directional, symmetry-sensitive.
  • This contrast is one of the most useful intuition checks in Chapter 24.
