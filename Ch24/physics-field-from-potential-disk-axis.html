Finding the Field from the Potential

This is the derivative page of the chapter. Start from the known axis potential of a uniformly charged disk, then use $$E_z=-dV/dz$$ to recover the electric field along the symmetry axis.

$$V(z)=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\left(\sqrt{z^2+R^2}-z\right),\qquad E_z=-\frac{dV}{dz}=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\left(1-\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2+R^2}}\right)$$

What To Notice

  • The disk’s symmetry means the axis field has only a $$z$$ component.
  • The minus sign in $$E_z=-dV/dz$$ is the whole move.
  • The result matches the axial field obtained earlier by direct integration.

Rooms

Room 1: Pick the Correct Direction

  • By symmetry, the disk’s field on its centerline points only along the axis.
  • So we only need the $$z$$ component.
  • That reduces the vector problem to a one-variable derivative.

Room 2: Start from the Potential Expression

  • The chapter gives $$V(z)=\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\left(\sqrt{z^2+R^2}-z\right).$$
  • This already contains all the field information.
  • We just need to differentiate it.

Room 3: Apply $$E_z=-dV/dz$$

  • The negative derivative converts potential slope into field.
  • Carry the constant factor $$\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0)$$ outside the derivative.
  • Then differentiate only the bracket.

Room 4: Differentiate the Square Root Carefully

  • Use the chain rule on $$\sqrt{z^2+R^2}.$$
  • Its derivative is $$z/\sqrt{z^2+R^2}.$$
  • The derivative of $$-z$$ is just $$-1.$$

Room 5: Clean the Signs

  • Inside the brackets, differentiation gives $$z/\sqrt{z^2+R^2}-1.$$
  • The overall minus sign flips that to $$1-z/\sqrt{z^2+R^2}.$$
  • That sign cleanup is the only delicate step.

Room 6: Keep the Interpretation

  • The field points away from the positively charged disk along the axis.
  • The factor in parentheses shrinks with increasing $$z,$$ so the field weakens farther away.
  • The answer agrees with the direct-integration result from the earlier disk-field chapter.

Axis Geometry

The disk symmetry removes every sideways component, leaving only the field along the centerline.

$$V(z)=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\left(\sqrt{z^2+R^2}-z\right)$$
$$E_z=-\frac{dV}{dz}$$
$$\frac{d}{dz}\sqrt{z^2+R^2}=\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2+R^2}}$$
$$E_z=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\left(1-\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2+R^2}}\right)$$

Derivative Ladder

The derivation is short once you keep the constant outside, differentiate the bracket, and let the outer minus sign clean up the final form.

Fast Check

If the potential decreases as you move in the positive $$z$$ direction, the field must point in the positive $$z$$ direction because $$E_z=-dV/dz.$$
