Potential Energy of a System of Three Charged Particles

Three charges sit at the corners of an equilateral triangle. The clean solution is to forget any imagined order of assembly and just add the three pairwise interaction energies: $$U_{12}+U_{13}+U_{23}.$$

$$U=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 d}(q_1q_2+q_1q_3+q_2q_3)=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 d}(-4q^2+2q^2-8q^2)=-\frac{10q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 d}=-1.7\times10^{-2}\,\text{J}$$

What To Notice

  • The triangle is equilateral, so each pair is separated by the same distance $$d=0.12\,\text{m}.$$
  • The charges are $$q_1=+q,$$ $$q_2=-4q,$$ and $$q_3=+2q$$ with $$q=150\,\text{nC}.$$
  • The system energy is negative because the attractive pairs dominate.

Rooms

Room 1: Think Pairwise, Not Particlewise

  • The energy of a system of point charges is the sum of the interaction energies of every pair.
  • For three charges, there are only three pairs.
  • That gives the roadmap immediately: $$U=U_{12}+U_{13}+U_{23}.$$

Room 2: Write the Pair Formula Once

  • Each pair contributes $$U_{ij}=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q_iq_j}{r_{ij}}.$$
  • Here every separation is the same: $$r_{ij}=d.$$
  • So the common denominator can be factored.

Room 3: Evaluate the Three Products

  • $$q_1q_2=(+q)(-4q)=-4q^2.$$
  • $$q_1q_3=(+q)(+2q)=+2q^2.$$
  • $$q_2q_3=(-4q)(+2q)=-8q^2.$$

Room 4: Add the Coefficients Before Substituting

  • The coefficient sum is $$-4+2-8=-10.$$
  • So the whole energy becomes $$U=-10q^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 d).$$
  • This is the fastest simplification step on the page.

Room 5: Insert the Data

  • Use $$q=150\times10^{-9}\,\text{C}$$ and $$d=0.12\,\text{m}.$$
  • The calculation gives $$U=-1.7\times10^{-2}\,\text{J}.$$
  • The magnitude is set by the square of the charge scale.

Room 6: Interpret the Negative Result

  • A negative system energy means the assembled arrangement is bound relative to infinite separation.
  • You would need to do positive work to pull all three charges infinitely far apart.
  • The attractive pair terms dominate the single repulsive term.

Room 7: Keep the Core Habit

  • For system energy problems, list the pairs first.
  • If distances are equal, factor that denominator immediately.
  • Then let the charge products decide the sign of the whole structure.

Three Charges, Three Pairs

The equilateral geometry makes all three separations identical, which is why the denominator is the same in every pair-energy term.

$$U_{12}=-4q^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 d)$$
$$U_{13}=+2q^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 d)$$
$$U_{23}=-8q^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 d)$$
sum coefficients: $$-10$$

Pairwise Accounting

Once the three pair products are written, the rest is just coefficient bookkeeping followed by one numerical substitution.

Fast Check

Count the attractive and repulsive pairs. If the stronger attractive products dominate, the total system energy should come out negative.
