Work and Potential Energy in an Electric Field

Starts with the work-energy link in a uniform electric field and tracks the sign carefully when an electron moves upward while the field points downward.

Uses W = F·d and then flips to ΔU = -W.
Best opener for locking in the sign logic behind electric potential energy.

Finding the Potential Change from the Electric Field

Shows that the potential difference between two points in a uniform field is path independent even when the path bends.

Compares a straight path with a two-segment path containing a perpendicular leg.
Good page when line integrals feel formal and you want the geometric meaning.

Sample Problem

Net Potential of Several Charged Particles

Adds the scalar potentials from four charges at the center of a square and shows how signs matter while directions do not.

Every charge sits at the same distance from the center, so the arithmetic compresses nicely.
Useful bridge from single-charge potential to multi-charge superposition.

Concept + Problem

Potential Is Not a Vector

Contrasts potential and field by arranging electrons on a circle, then redistributing them along an arc to show what changes and what does not.

Potential depends only on scalar contributions at the center.
Field still cares about direction, so symmetry breaking changes E but not V.

Finding the Field from the Potential

Differentiates the disk potential along its symmetry axis to recover the electric field component on that axis.

Pure derivative page: start from V(z) and use E_z = -dV/dz.
Helpful if you want to connect Chapter 22 field results to Chapter 24 potential results.

Sample Problem

Potential Energy of a Three-Charge System

Builds a system energy from the sum of pair energies for three charges fixed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle.

The clean move is to add U12 + U13 + U23.
Best page for remembering that system energy is pairwise, not particle-by-particle.

Sample Problem

Conservation of Mechanical Energy

Uses the turning point of an alpha particle near a gold nucleus to convert initial kinetic energy into final electric potential energy.

At the closest approach, the alpha particle stops for an instant, so K_f = 0.
Great closing page because it combines Coulomb interaction with energy conservation in one move.

Chapter Shift

Instead of asking what force a charge feels, these pages keep asking what energy or potential a configuration implies. That is the main mental pivot from Chapters 22 and 23.

Suggested Order

  • Move from work and potential energy to potential difference in a field, then to scalar potential superposition.
  • After that, lock in the scalar-versus-vector idea, differentiate a potential into a field, then finish with system energy and conservation of energy.
