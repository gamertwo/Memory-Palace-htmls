24-3 | Sample Problem: Scalar Superposition

Net Potential of Several Charged Particles

At the center of a square, the potential from four corner charges is a pure scalar sum. Because each corner is the same distance from the center, the geometry collapses and only the algebra of the charge values remains.

$$V=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_i}{r_i}=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1+q_2+q_3+q_4}{0.919\,\text{m}}\approx350\,\text{V}$$

What To Notice

  • The four corner charges are $$+12\,\text{nC}, +31\,\text{nC}, -24\,\text{nC}, +17\,\text{nC}.$$
  • The center is the same distance from all four charges: $$r=d/\sqrt2=0.919\,\text{m}.$$
  • Potential adds as a scalar, so direction never enters this calculation.
Quick Anchors
Geometry
Square side length $$d=1.3\,\text{m}$$ gives center-to-corner distance $$r=d/\sqrt2.$$
Charge Sum
$$q_1+q_2+q_3+q_4=(12+31-24+17)\,\text{nC}=36\,\text{nC}.$$
Superposition
Potential is the algebraic sum of each charge contribution.
Answer
The center potential is positive because the positive charges win the sum.
Rooms

Room 1: Start with the Scalar Formula

  • The potential at a point is the sum of the potentials from each charge.
  • For four charges, $$V=\sum \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q_i}{r_i}.$$
  • No vector components are needed.

Room 2: Notice the Symmetry of Distance

  • Point $$P$$ is at the center of the square.
  • So each charge sits the same distance from $$P.$$
  • That lets us factor the common distance out of the sum.

Room 3: Compute the Center-to-Corner Distance

  • For a square of side $$d,$$ the diagonal is $$d\sqrt2.$$
  • The center is halfway along that diagonal, so $$r=d/\sqrt2.$$
  • With $$d=1.3\,\text{m},$$ the common distance is $$0.919\,\text{m}.$$

Room 4: Add the Charges Algebraically

  • Now combine the charge values: $$12+31-24+17=36.$$
  • So the net numerator is $$36\times10^{-9}\,\text{C}.$$
  • The negative charge matters, but only by subtraction.

Room 5: Substitute Once

  • Insert the charge sum and common distance into the potential formula.
  • That yields $$V=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{36\times10^{-9}\,\text{C}}{0.919\,\text{m}}.$$
  • The computation is straightforward because the geometry was simplified first.

Room 6: Read the Sign of the Result

  • The answer comes out positive: about $$350\,\text{V}.$$
  • That means the positive charges outweigh the lone negative contribution.
  • If the charge sum had been negative, the potential would have been negative too.

Room 7: Keep the Big Lesson

  • Potential cares about scalar addition, not geometric cancellation of directions.
  • Equal distances can turn a multi-charge problem into one clean numerator sum.
  • This is the chapter’s easiest example of potential superposition.

Potential Landscape

Potential can be visualized as a scalar landscape: it rises near positive charge and falls near negative charge, but the value at a point is still just one number.

Fast Check

If every charge is the same distance from the point, add the charges first. That usually reveals the sign of the answer before you finish the arithmetic.
