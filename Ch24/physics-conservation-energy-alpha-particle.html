24-7 | Sample Problem: Turning-Point Energy

Conservation of Mechanical Energy with Electric Potential Energy

An alpha particle heads toward a gold nucleus, slows under electric repulsion, and stops momentarily at the closest approach. That turning point is the gift: when $$K_f=0,$$ the initial kinetic energy must equal the final electric potential energy.

$$K_i+U_i=K_f+U_f,\qquad U_i=0,\qquad K_f=0,\qquad K_i=U_f=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{(2e)(79e)}{r}=4.6\,\text{MeV}$$

What To Notice

  • The alpha particle and gold nucleus both carry positive charge, so the interaction is repulsive.
  • At the turning point, the alpha particle is instantaneously at rest.
  • The closest-approach distance is $$r=9.23\,\text{fm}.$$
Quick Anchors
Charges
Alpha particle charge $$=+2e,$$ gold nucleus charge $$=+79e.$$
Turning Point
At closest approach, $$K_f=0.$$
Reference
Initially far away, take $$U_i=0.$$
Answer
The initial kinetic energy is $$4.6\,\text{MeV}.$$
Rooms

Room 1: Recognize a Repulsive Coulomb Approach

  • The alpha particle and gold nucleus are both positively charged.
  • So the electric force is repulsive.
  • As the alpha particle approaches, it slows down and stores energy as electric potential energy.

Room 2: Use Mechanical Energy Conservation

  • No nonconservative work is included, so mechanical energy is conserved.
  • Write $$K_i+U_i=K_f+U_f.$$
  • This is the one equation the entire sample sits on.

Room 3: Simplify with the Boundary Conditions

  • Initially far away, take the interaction energy to be zero: $$U_i=0.$$
  • At the turning point, the alpha particle is instantaneously stopped: $$K_f=0.$$
  • So the equation reduces to $$K_i=U_f.$$

Room 4: Write the Final Potential Energy

  • The final separation is $$r=9.23\,\text{fm}=9.23\times10^{-15}\,\text{m}.$$
  • Use $$U_f=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q_\alpha q_{\text{Au}}}{r}.$$
  • Here that means $$q_\alpha q_{\text{Au}}=(2e)(79e).$$

Room 5: Compute the Energy

  • Substituting the charges and distance gives $$K_i=7.4\times10^{-13}\,\text{J}.$$
  • Converting to electron-volts gives $$4.6\,\text{MeV}.$$
  • The answer is large because the charges are large and the final distance is nuclear-scale small.

Room 6: Interpret the Turning Point

  • The alpha particle cannot get closer unless it starts with even more kinetic energy.
  • The turning point is the place where all the initial kinetic energy has been traded into potential energy.
  • Then the particle reverses and moves back outward.

Room 7: Keep the Template

  • Closest-approach problems are usually turning-point energy problems in disguise.
  • If an object stops momentarily, set its kinetic energy there to zero.
  • Then use conservation of energy to read the initial speed or initial kinetic energy.
