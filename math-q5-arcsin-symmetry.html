Q5 · Arcsin Symmetry

Given: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{\sqrt{1-y^2}}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{\sqrt{1-y^2}}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$$
Step 1: Separate variables: $\dfrac{dy}{\sqrt{1-y^2}}=\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$.
Step 2: Integrate both sides.
$$\sin^{-1}y=\sin^{-1}x+C$$
$$\boxed{\sin^{-1}y=\sin^{-1}x+C}$$
Memory cues:
Room 1
Square-root denominator $\sqrt{1-u^2}$ instantly triggers arcsin integral.
Room 2
Both sides mirror the same pattern, so both integrate to inverse-sine.
Room 3
Final answer kept in inverse-trig implicit form for clean scoring.
